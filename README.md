|**2026-07-30**|**A Cartesian Grid Method for Advection-Diffusion Equations with Robin Boundary Conditions on Moving Domains**|Han Zhou, Yoichiro Mori, Lingxing Yao|[[arxiv:2607.26341](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26341)]|This paper introduces a Cartesian grid method for solving advection-diffusion equations with Robin boundary conditions on moving domains, reformulating the problem as an interface problem on a fixed box. The method uses a density function on the moving interface to enforce the Robin condition, discretizes the bulk equation with cell-centered finite differences, and solves local problems near the interface. Key results show that the GMRES iteration count is essentially mesh-independent, computational cost scales linearly with the number of unknowns, and first-order temporal with second-order spatial convergence is proven. This approach significantly simplifies computational geometry by avoiding remeshing and cut cells, making it both efficient and accurate for such problems.|
|**2026-07-30**|**The boundary-driven multispecies harmonic process**|Francesco Casini, Rouven Frassek, Cristian Giardin\`a|[[arxiv:2607.26262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26262)]|This paper introduces a multispecies version of the harmonic process on a one-dimensional chain with boundary reservoirs, modeling out-of-equilibrium systems where colored particles are exchanged. The method involves connecting the Markov generator of this process to an integrable Hamiltonian of an open rational Heisenberg spin chain, using R- and K-matrices to construct the double-row transfer matrix. Key results include the factorization of the R-matrix into two operators for left and right moving particles, as well as the definition of three dual models: absorbing particle, hidden parameter, and heat conduction models. The significance lies in extending integrable boundary-driven dynamics to multispecies systems, offering a framework for analyzing non-equilibrium steady states.|
|**2026-07-30**|**Symplectic Isomorphism Between Strong-Coupling Criticality and Finite-Strain Bifurcation**|Yu-Xin Xie|[[arxiv:2606.18635](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.18635)]|The paper addresses the challenge of analytically determining critical scaling in strongly coupled field theories, which is limited by the non-summability of perturbative expansions. The authors establish a symplectic isomorphism between the infrared dynamics of scalar quantum field theories and finite-strain bifurcation in hyperelastic continua, reformulating renormalization group flow as Hamiltonian evolution. Key results include the identification of critical fixed points with spectral degeneration of a Riccati-Lyapunov system and the derivation of a self-consistent algebraic Ansatz for the anomalous dimension in the 3D Ising model, which recovers exact limits like Gaussian scaling. This geometric framework eliminates arbitrary perturbative truncations, providing a first-principles macroscopic analogue for non-perturbative screening and a transparent mathematical method for strong-coupling analysis.|

|**2026-07-30**|**Direct minimization versus iterative embedding in the ghost-Gutzwiller method: a comparative study of magnetism in Mott insulators**|Antonio Maria Tagliente, Ivan Pasqua, Michele Fabrizio|[[arxiv:2607.27156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27156)]|The paper addresses the challenge of accurately describing symmetry-invariant Mott insulators within the ghost-Gutzwiller method. It compares two solution approaches—iterative embedding, similar to dynamical mean-field theory, and direct minimization of the variational energy functional—across the Mott transition in the single-band Hubbard model. Key results show that the iterative scheme is efficient but fragile, failing in a Zeeman field with spurious artifacts, while direct minimization reliably produces a paramagnetic solution; however, iterative embedding works correctly for antiferromagnetic phases. The significance lies in delineating when iterative embedding can be trusted versus when direct minimization is necessary for accurate magnetic descriptions in Mott insulators.|
|**2026-07-30**|**Engineering SU( $N$)-Symmetric Hubbard Models with Microwave-Shielded Dipolar Molecules**|Jing-Lun Li, Ragheed Alhyder, Andreas Schindewolf, Kaden R. A. Hazzard, Mikhail Lemeshko, Georgios M. Koutentakis|[[arxiv:2607.27107](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.27107)]|This paper addresses the problem of lacking controllable on-site interactions in extended Hubbard models with polar molecules. The authors propose a method using microwave-shielded dipolar molecules, where tuning the Rabi frequency brings monomers on neighboring sites into resonance with a field-linked dimer (doublon) on a single site, enabling coherent conversion. Key results show that on-site and off-site interactions can be tuned nearly independently via microwave amplitude and orientation, and the authors characterize Hubbard parameters and dimer lifetimes. This work is significant because it provides a roadmap for implementing SU($N$ )-symmetric extended Hubbard models with controllable doublon fluctuations, enabling quantum simulation in the strongly dipolar regime.|
|**2026-07-30**|**Algebraic Diagrammatic Construction of the Multichannel Dyson Equation**|Thibault Demartini, J. Arjan Berger, Guillaume Blanchon, Thomas Duguet, Denis Lacroix, Pina Romaniello, Vittorio Som\`a|[[arxiv:2607.26609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26609)]|This paper clarifies the physical content of the multichannel Dyson equation (MCDE) approximation by deriving it from an extended algebraic diagrammatic construction (ADC) scheme. The method shows that the MCDE approximation sits between the ADC(2) and ADC(3) levels for the dynamical self-energy truncation. Tested on the periodic one-dimensional Hubbard model, MCDE delivers improved spectral features—such as sharper quasiparticle peaks and better satellite structures—compared to ADC(2). This work enhances theoretical understanding of MCDE and demonstrates its potential for more accurate many-body Green’s function calculations.|
|**2026-07-30**|**The boundary-driven multispecies harmonic process**|Francesco Casini, Rouven Frassek, Cristian Giardin\`a|[[arxiv:2607.26262](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26262)]|This paper introduces a multispecies version of the harmonic process on a one-dimensional chain connected to particle reservoirs at its boundaries. The authors map the Markov generator of this continuous-time, out-of-equilibrium system onto an integrable Hamiltonian of an open rational Heisenberg spin chain. They derive the necessary R- and K-matrices and construct the double-row transfer matrix, revealing a factorisation into left and right moving particle operators. The work further defines three dual models and extends the integrable framework to higher-rank particle systems.|
|**2026-07-30**|**Dimensional crossover of bound complexes in a two-species Bose-Hubbard lattice: Correlations and dynamics**|Deepak Gaur, Koushik Mukherjee, Stephanie M. Reimann|[[arxiv:2602.04741](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04741)]|This paper investigates the formation of four-particle bound states (tetramers) in a two-species Bose-Hubbard model on coupled chains. Using exact diagonalization, the authors characterize how the system transitions from dimers to tetramers as interchain hopping increases, finding that transverse connectivity expands the tetramer phase region. They identify a mechanism where lifting configurational degeneracies drives tetramer stabilization, and show this binding extends to larger complexes. The work provides a framework for engineering and probing few-body bound states in tunable lattice geometries.|
|**2026-07-30**|**Dipolar optimal control of entangled current states**|H\'ector Briongos-Merino, Felipe Isaule, Bruno Juli\'a-D\'iaz, Montserrat Guilleumas|[[arxiv:2507.22822](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.22822)]|This paper addresses the challenge of generating specific quantum states with entangled circulation using ultracold atoms. The proposed method employs quantum optimal control to manipulate the dipolar interaction on a lattice ring, relying only on time-dependent magnetic field orientation as two independent control functions. Numerical simulations using the extended Bose-Hubbard model show that this approach can engineer entangled current states with perfect fidelity for many systems, and achieves theoretical upper bounds for fidelity in the remaining cases. The significance lies in offering a feasible experimental technique for precise quantum state control, which is crucial for advancing quantum technologies.|
