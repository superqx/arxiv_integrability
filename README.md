|**2026-08-28**|**Fluctuation--response relations from an emergent $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry in the rotating stochastic Landau model**|Dhruv Kush, Nicki Mullins, Mauricio Hippert, Jorge Noronha|[[arxiv:2608.26468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26468)]|This paper investigates whether fluctuation-response relations can emerge from coarse-grained stochastic dynamics alone, using the rotating stochastic Landau model—an exactly solvable system of an overdamped charged Brownian particle in a magnetic field coupled to a rotating environment. The authors employ the Martin–Siggia–Rose path integral to reveal an emergent \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) symmetry that implements time-reversed dynamics, changing the action by a boundary term linked to entropy production via the Crooks fluctuation theorem. From this symmetry, they derive Ward identities that relate fluctuations and responses without fixing the noise strength, showing these identities are purely a consequence of coarse-grained dynamics. When the Einstein relation is imposed, these identities reduce to the standard high-temperature fluctuation-dissipation relations, demonstrating that macroscopic response laws can arise from stochastic structure alone, independent of microscopic details.|

|**2026-08-28**|**Exact Stiffness and Dynamical Responses from Fock-Space Fragmentation**|Jonah Herzog-Arbeitman, Eslam Khalaf, Zhaoyu Han|[[arxiv:2608.27446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27446)]|This paper introduces a new method for calculating dynamic responses in quantum many-body systems using Fock-space fragmentation. The authors analyze quantum geometric nesting (QGN) models, where excitations above the ground state decouple into invariant Krylov subspaces with fixed particle-hole counts. They prove that the stiffness of spontaneously broken symmetries matches the variational Gaussian value exactly, since infinitesimal twists only couple to a one-particle, one-hole fragment. The significance lies in enabling exact computation of observable quantities like optical conductivity and Green's functions, which are typically difficult to obtain from algebraic spectra alone.|
|**2026-08-28**|**Fluctuation--response relations from an emergent $\mathbb{Z}_2$ symmetry in the rotating stochastic Landau model**|Dhruv Kush, Nicki Mullins, Mauricio Hippert, Jorge Noronha|[[arxiv:2608.26468](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26468)]|This paper explores whether fluctuation-response relations can emerge purely from coarse-grained stochastic dynamics, without relying on microscopic details. Using the exactly solvable rotating stochastic Landau model, the authors employ a Martin–Siggia–Rose path integral to uncover an emergent \(\mathbb{Z}_2\) symmetry that implements time reversal and alters the action by a boundary term. This symmetry yields Ward identities that connect fluctuations and responses, with the boundary term identified as transition entropy via the Crooks fluctuation theorem. The results show that these relations arise from the stochastic theory alone and match high-temperature fluctuation-dissipation relations when the Einstein relation is imposed, highlighting the minimal ingredients needed for such universal behavior.|
|**2026-08-28**|**Dynamical signatures of deconfined spinons in dimerized sawtooth chains**|Nishan Ranabhat, Brandon B. Le, Seung-Hun Lee, Gia-Wei Chern|[[arxiv:2608.26347](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26347)]|This paper investigates the Heisenberg model on a sawtooth chain, focusing on its dynamical properties across a valence-bond-ordered (dimerized) phase. Using DMRG and TDVP methods, the authors compute the dynamical structure factor and find that the spectrum is a broad continuum, which they identify as deconfined two-spinon excitations (kink-antikink pairs). They validate this by deriving closed-form spinon dispersions and reproducing intensity distributions with a finite-separation calculation. The work provides a microscopic understanding of fractionalized excitations in this system, with potential relevance to Ti³⁺ kagome fluoride materials.|
|**2026-08-28**|**Critical Properties and Glass Transitions in Randomly Coupled Fields**|Amer Al-Hiyasat|[[arxiv:2608.26279](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26279)]|This paper introduces a model of N scalar fields coupled by quenched random interactions, effectively creating a spin glass where spins are extended into d-dimensional fields. The authors determine the critical properties of this system for large N, showing they are governed by the eigenvalue distribution of the random coupling matrix and identifying distinct Gaussian universality classes. Key findings include a divergent correlation length with a finite susceptibility for Wigner matrices, an upper critical dimension of one, and, in a solvable spherical version, a heat capacity jump with correlations maintaining their critical form throughout the glass phase. This work is significant for establishing a new type of glass transition exhibiting generic scale invariance without a Goldstone mode.|
|**2026-08-28**|**Spin-charge separation in the triangular-lattice Hofstadter-Hubbard model**|Yuntian Gu, Hui Yang, Zhehao Dai, Yantao Wu|[[arxiv:2608.02727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02727)]|This paper investigates the possibility of a chiral spin liquid phase in the triangular-lattice Hofstadter-Hubbard model, a system relevant to recent moiré material experiments. Using variational Monte Carlo with neural quantum states and projected entangled pair states, the authors directly observe spin-charge separation in real-space numerical simulations. While they find a positive two-electron binding energy on small systems, this effect diminishes with increasing system size, challenging the conjectured anyonic superconductivity. The work highlights the power of combining these two advanced computational methods for diagnosing topological order and fractionalized excitations in strongly correlated systems.|
|**2026-08-28**|**Excitation spectra and rank tomography of linear matrix product tangent spaces**|Otto T. P. The authors also develop a "rank tomography" technique that characterizes the expressivity of the MPS tangent space via particle-sector rank profiles. Benchmarking against the Bose-Hubbard model, the method successfully reproduces low-lying excitations and detects finite-size precursors of the Mott-insulator to superfluid transition. This work extends MPS-based excitation analysis to inhomogeneous systems, offering a new tool for probing quantum phase transitions.|
|**2026-08-28**|**Mpemba effect in a two-dimensional bistable potential**|Hisao Hayakawa, Satoshi Takada|[[arxiv:2603.24148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.24148)]|This paper introduces an exactly solvable two-dimensional model of the Mpemba effect, where a system cools faster from a hotter initial state than from a cooler one. The authors construct a radially symmetric, bistable potential using a piecewise quadratic-logarithmic function, allowing them to analytically map the Fokker-Planck operator to a Schrödinger-type eigenvalue problem. By deriving the relaxation spectrum and mode amplitudes, they show that the slowest mode's coefficient depends non-monotonically on temperature and establish a sufficient condition for the effect based on the Kullback-Leibler divergence. This work provides a rare analytically tractable example of anomalous relaxation in two dimensions without confining walls, extending prior one-dimensional findings and clarifying the role of radial geometry.|
|**2026-08-28**|**Exponential $U(1)$ Symmetry-Breaking Phase as a Disorder-Free Quantum Glass**|Yu-Min Hu, Zhaoyu Han, Biao Lian|[[arxiv:2512.21847](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.21847)]|This paper investigates a one-dimensional spin model with an unusual exponential U(1) symmetry, where a charge unit decreases with lattice position. Using numerical methods, the authors discover a spontaneous symmetry-breaking phase, termed a "quantum breakdown condensate," which possesses a massive number of ground states and a finite entropy density. This phase features a bulk gap that defies the Goldstone theorem, exhibits only a left-edge mode, and shows non-decaying local correlations without long-range order. The findings reveal a novel, disorder-free quantum glass phase that challenges current classifications of quantum matter.|
|**2026-08-28**|**Magnetic instabilities in three- and higher-dimensional Hubbard models with different lattice geometries: Impact of nonlocal spatial correlations**|Marvin Leusch, Alessandro Toschi, Andreas Hausoel, Giorgio Sangiovanni, Georg Rohringer|[[arxiv:2512.14396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.14396)]|This paper investigates how lattice geometry affects magnetic phase transitions in the Hubbard model, comparing dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT) results with the dynamical vertex approximation (DΓA), which includes nonlocal correlations. The authors calculate magnetic susceptibility and transition temperatures for simple cubic/hypercubic, body-centered cubic (bcc), and face-centered cubic (fcc) lattices in three to five dimensions. They find that nonlocal fluctuations significantly reduce DMFT transition temperatures for bipartite lattices, with the reduction shrinking as coordination number increases, while for the frustrated fcc lattice, the DMFT-predicted magnetic order disappears entirely within DΓA. These results offer quantitative guidance for estimating correlation-driven corrections to DMFT predictions in real materials and highlight the critical role of nonlocal effects in geometrically frustrated systems.|
|**2026-08-28**|**Spin triplet pairing by suppressing altermagnetism**|Xin Ma, Siqi Wu, Zilong Li, Lunhui Hu, Jianhui Dai, Chao Cao|[[arxiv:2509.09959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09959)]|This paper investigates whether spin-triplet superconductivity can emerge from suppressing altermagnetic order, a question studied using a minimal Hubbard model on a square lattice with a vacancy superstructure. The method involves modeling a metallic normal phase where altermagnetism exists at half-filling due to geometric frustration and Coulomb interactions, then introducing charge doping to suppress the magnetic order. Key results show that while conventional antiferromagnetic fluctuations favor spin-singlet pairing, dominant altermagnetic fluctuations uniquely induce spin-triplet pairing. The findings are significant as they establish a concrete theoretical pathway for achieving spin-triplet superconductivity by tuning altermagnetism, offering insights for identifying material candidates.|

Pedraza|[[arxiv:2608.26825](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26825)]|This paper is a topical review of Krylov state complexity, also known as spread complexity, as a diagnostic for quantum chaos and information spreading. The method involves using the Lanczos algorithm to construct an optimal basis and track the evolution of a state's complexity along the resulting Krylov chain. A key result is the characteristic overshoot in chaotic systems, where complexity peaks higher than in integrable ones before relaxing, a behavior linked to random-matrix spectral universality. The review's significance lies in unifying this phenomenon across diverse models—from billiards to SYK—and discussing its holographic connection to gravity, while outlining open questions for future research.|
