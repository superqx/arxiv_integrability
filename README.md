|**2026-11-04**|**Drinfeld J Presentation of Twisted Yangians**|Samuel Belliard, Vidas Regelskis|[[arxiv:1401.2143](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.2143)]|The paper addresses the problem of formulating twisted Yangians—quantized enveloping algebras associated with symmetric pairs—in the Drinfeld J presentation, which had been established for ordinary Yangians but not fully for twisted cases. The authors quantize a Lie coideal structure for twisted half-loop algebras of finite-dimensional simple complex Lie algebras, thereby deriving closure relations for the Drinfeld J presentation. They obtain these closure relations for all symmetric pairs of simple Lie algebras and for simple twisted even half-loop Lie algebras, and they give the explicit form of the relations for the case of sl₃. This extends the Drinfeld J presentation to twisted Yangians, providing a unified algebraic framework and concrete formulas useful for further study of their representation theory.|

|**2026-11-04**|**Fixed-grid schemes for fractional moving-boundary problems**|Conrard Giresse Tetsassi Feugmo|[[arxiv:2611.04026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04026)]|The paper addresses the failure of the standard Landau transformation for moving-boundary problems with fractional time derivatives, since no chain rule exists and the transformed operator becomes nonlocal. It develops a sharp-interface finite-difference scheme on a fixed grid for the memory-flux fractional Stefan problem, storing each node's melt time and advancing the front via an integrated energy balance with the singular source timed to the sub-step crossing. Against a known self-similar solution, the front constant is accurate to 0.02–0.11%, while competing formulations err by 17–27% at α = 0.6; the scheme is also verified against two new exact solutions with non-power-law fronts. The work shows that normalized-coordinate neural-network solvers silently alter the problem for α < 1 (a change vanishing at α = 1, where checks are usually done), and in the classical limit the scheme is more accurate in 12 ms than a reimplemented moving-boundary network in 177 s.|
|**2026-11-04**|**Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz for the Toda chain from inverse scattering transform**|Alberto Brollo|[[arxiv:2611.03857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03857)]|The paper addresses the lack of a consistent classical derivation of Thermodynamic Bethe Ansatz (TBA) equations for integrable classical field theories, particularly one that accounts for solitons, breathers, radiation, and their mutual interactions. The authors derive TBA equations for the Toda chain directly from its inverse scattering transform, obtaining solitonic modes that interact with radiation and thereby extending the soliton gas picture. They numerically verify their equations against established TBA equations formulated in terms of particle modes. The work provides a template expected to apply to KdV and purely radiative models such as defocusing NLS and Sinh-Gordon, while identifying current limitations for focusing NLS, Ablowitz-Ladik, and Sine-Gordon.|

Using gauge invariance for scalar and vector potentials, the authors derive explicit energy spectra and eigenfunctions, extend the solution to the electromagnetic Schrödinger equation with a "Dirac string" magnetic field generated by a singular vortex probability flux, and analyze superpositions producing various vortex and potential probability current fields within the Wigner-Vlasov formalism. The work also notes that the Aharonov-Bohm effect is inherent in the quantum Ψ-model. These results are significant because exact solutions are valuable for validating numerical algorithms and for methodological study of complex quantum systems, and they provide a rare analytically tractable example of a non-Gaussian system with positive phase-space quasi-probability.|
|**2026-11-04**|**Drinfeld J Presentation of Twisted Yangians**|Samuel Belliard, Vidas Regelskis|[[arxiv:1401.2143](https://arxiv.org/abs/1401.2143)]|The paper addresses the lack of a Drinfeld J presentation for twisted Yangians, which are important quantum group analogues of twisted half-loop algebras. |**2026-11-04**|**Phonon Bose-Einstein condensation in a Hubbard-phonon interacting system with infrared divergence**|Yoshitsugu Sekine|[[arxiv:1308.5589](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.5589)]|The paper studies grand canonical Gibbs states of a finite Hubbard model coupled to massless phonons with dispersion /k/^s (1≤s<d) at finite temperature, addressing whether phonons can undergo Bose-Einstein condensation in such an interacting system. Using a decoupling setting with a site-independent electron-phonon form factor, the authors apply an explicit unitary transformation that factorizes the Gibbs state into an electronic part and a free-phonon part up to a phase. They find that in the thermodynamic limit the phonons exhibit Bose-Einstein condensation with the critical density of the free Bose gas; assuming convergence as the infrared cutoff is removed, the condensed state is a mixture of mutually disjoint states with broken gauge symmetry described via expectations on the Weyl algebra. The work is significant for clarifying a mathematical/formal condensation mechanism, while noting that physically phonons are not expected to condense and that a suppression mechanism remains for future research.|
|**2026-11-04**|**Magnetism and infrared divergence in a Hubbard-phonon interacting system**|Yoshitsugu Sekine|[[arxiv:1008.2056](https://arxiv.org/abs/1008.2056)]|The paper studies a finite Hubbard model coupled to a massless phonon field through an infrared-singular form factor, and shows that the Hamiltonian has no ground state in the Fock representation. Using the Arai–Hirokawa unitary transformation, the authors define cutoff ground states on the Weyl algebra over the form domain of the inverse dispersion and prove they converge to a ground state in a representation inequivalent to Fock space. In this representation the ground state factorizes into a Hubbard ground state times the phonon vacuum, so the system exhibits ferromagnetic or unique spin-singlet order depending on the hopping matrix and electron number. The result clarifies how infrared divergence and electron-phonon coupling jointly determine magnetic ordering in correlated lattice systems.|

They find that cumulants follow power laws whose exponents vary continuously with the fractional order: at low order the mean defect number decays more slowly than Kibble-Zurek mechanism predictions, at intermediate order KZM holds with numerically extracted exponents, and at large order the free-fermion limit becomes increasingly accurate.
