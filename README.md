|**2026-10-15**|**Coherent states of neutrinos in rotating matter**|Alexander Breev, Maxim Dvornikov|[[arxiv:2610.19078](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19078)]|The paper investigates how neutrinos form coherent states while interacting with rotating matter, a problem relevant to astrophysical environments. By reducing the neutrino Hamiltonian to harmonic oscillator form in the massless limit with a specific matter velocity profile, the authors derive exact Dirac solutions and construct creation/annihilation operators to build generalized coherent states. These states reduce to plane waves for slow rotation, and the authors define an effective neutrino mass arising from the spin-matter interaction. Applying the results to neutrinos in a rapidly rotating neutron star, they compute effective masses for various neutrino energies using realistic stellar parameters.|

|**2026-10-15**|**On the Mathematical Structure of Helical Shear in Anelastic Continua**|Souhayl Sadik, Giuseppe Saccomandi, Luigi Vergori|[[arxiv:2610.19221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19221)]|The paper analyzes helical shear in a thick-walled tube of an incompressible hyper-anelastic solid containing radially distributed screw dislocations, modeled via the Bilby–Kröner–Lee decomposition and a Weitzenböck material manifold. Equilibrium reduces to an algebraic system per radius in which the two shear modes couple only through dislocation strength and the strain energy's dependence on the second invariant. Exact solutions are derived for several material models, showing that dislocations stiffen the tube, induce chiral mode coupling, and can restrict admissible dislocation strength or traction ranges. A weakly nonlinear expansion reveals that coupling appears only at second order, so every dislocated material behaves like a Mooney–Rivlin solid at that order.|
|**2026-10-15**|**Symmetries, Lax pair, and nilpotent Darboux transformation for the KdV-ghost system**|A. Restuccia, J. Rosales-G\'omez, A. Sotomayor|[[arxiv:2610.19192](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19192)]|The paper studies the KdV-ghost system derived from the BRST formulation of BF-type topological field theory, focusing on its symmetries, Lax pair, and Darboux formalism. The authors introduce a Grassmann-valued Lax pair, prove an isospectral theorem connecting the KdV and KdV-ghost spectral problems, and show that the ghost-extended spatial Lax operator is related to the KdV one by a nilpotent conjugation. They construct a nilpotent lift of the scalar KdV Bäcklund-Darboux transformation that closes on the local KdV-ghost system, yielding a recursive ghost extension of the bosonic Darboux chain with a new odd contribution at each step. This provides a Darboux-theoretic interpretation of the ghost sector that remains distinct from standard supersymmetric KdV hierarchies.|
|**2026-10-15**|**Spectral Theory of an Exactly Solvable Quantum-Optical Oscillator**|Sam Johar, Joseph Kraisler|[[arxiv:2610.18421](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18421)]|The paper analyzes a one-dimensional single-excitation model where a scalar photon field couples to a continuum of two-level atoms with quadratic density, producing an unbounded, non-semibounded matrix Hamiltonian with spatially varying coupling. The authors prove essential self-adjointness and derive the spectrum explicitly via a nonlinear spectral reduction, showing that the continuous spectrum is the half-line \((-\infty,\Omega]\) and that above the atomic resonance frequency \(\Omega\) there is a strictly increasing sequence of simple eigenvalues characterized by interlaced zeros of the Airy function and its derivative. These eigenvalues obey the asymptotic law \(\omega_n = (3\pi g n/4)^{1/2} + \Omega/4 + O(n^{-1/2})\). The result provides an exactly solvable quantum-optical oscillator whose spectral structure links to a confining massless relativistic oscillator above \(\Omega\) and an inverted oscillator below it, offering a rare explicit spectral solution for a non-semibounded, spatially varying coupling model.|
|**2026-10-15**|**The contrast between real and $p$ -adic integrability**|Luis Crespo, \'Alvaro Pelayo|[[arxiv:2606.24363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.24363)]|The paper asks whether an integrable Hamiltonian system behaves the same when its coefficients are taken over the reals versus the \(p\)-adic numbers, focusing on local normal forms. It proves that real and \(p\)-adic local normal forms of the same system are completely independent: any pairing of a real normal form with a \(p\)-adic one can occur. The authors develop computational techniques for these normal forms by combining Galois theory of \(p\)-adic fields with analytic estimates. This shows that \(p\)-adic symplectic geometry captures genuinely different information from real symplectic geometry, even though both contain \(\mathbb{Q}\) densely.|
|**2026-10-15**|**Work statistics of sudden quantum quenches: A random matrix theory perspective on Gaussianity and its deviations**|Miguel Tierz|[[arxiv:2509.09640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09640)]|For sudden quantum quenches in translation-invariant quadratic fermionic chains, the work distribution is reformulated as the statistics of traces of powers of Haar-random unitaries via a Toeplitz/unitary-matrix representation of the Loschmidt amplitude. Using multivariate central limit theorems for unitary traces, the authors show that the work statistics become Gaussian with variance \(\frac{1}{2}\sum_r r(a_r^2+b_r^2)\), with asymptotic independence across different powers. They further identify when non-Gaussian tails emerge, such as from numerous or slowly decaying interaction terms and Fisher–Hartwig singularities, illustrating these mechanisms in the XY chain. This provides a random-matrix framework for understanding Gaussianity and its breakdown in quantum quench work statistics.|
|**2026-10-15**|**A vectorial Darboux transformation for integrable matrix versions of the Fokas-Lenells equation**|Folkert M\"uller-Hoissen, Rusuo Ye|[[arxiv:2508.17534](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.17534)]|The paper addresses the construction of Darboux transformations for integrable matrix generalizations of the Fokas-Lenells equation and its associated negative Kaup-Newell flow. Using bidifferential calculus, the authors derive a vectorial binary Darboux transformation for the matrix system and show that a reduction yields an analogous transformation for the matrix Fokas-Lenells equation. This machinery produces matrix soliton solutions from a trivial seed and, more notably, yields systematic exact solutions of the two-component vector Fokas-Lenells equation on a plane-wave background, including breathers, dark solitons, rogue waves, and beating solitons. The results extend Darboux transformation techniques to vectorial and matrix settings, providing a unified route to a rich class of exact nonlinear wave solutions.|

|**2026-10-15**|**Physical limits to stochastic classifiers**|Timur Aslyamov, Massimiliano Esposito|[[arxiv:2610.20481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20481)]|Stochastic physical computers that use random state transitions for classification face fundamental limits on how many distinct tasks they can perform as their control parameters vary. The authors derive bounds on the number of binary classification tasks achievable at a fixed output margin by combining geometric packing arguments with fluctuation-response inequalities, expressing these limits in terms of control range, dimension, rate sensitivities, output fluctuations, and entropy production. For state and net-current observables, when controls alter kinetics without changing thermodynamic forces or energies, the task count depends on entropy production and output fluctuations, and if the product of their maxima is too small, the device can perform at most one task. An exactly solvable two-state switch demonstrates that sufficient driving and observation time allow barrier tuning to select between copying and inversion, illustrating the practical significance of these physical constraints.|
|**2026-10-15**|**Interplay of electric field and electronic correlations in pairing competition in bilayer nickelates**|Yanmei Cai, Yicheng Xiong, Ying Liang, Tianxing Ma|[[arxiv:2610.19809](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19809)]|Using constrained-path quantum Monte Carlo on a bilayer two-orbital Hubbard model, the authors examine how a perpendicular electric field (layer potential difference) tunes the competition between interlayer s± and intralayer d-wave pairing in bilayer nickelates. Increasing the layer bias enhances the d-wave pairing vertex and suppresses s±, while stronger onsite repulsion boosts both channels (especially d-wave) and crystal-field splitting reduces both while favoring s±. Hund's coupling favors s± at weak bias but strengthens d-wave at large bias, with the bias mainly redistributing charge between layers and increasing antiferromagnetic correlations. The results link charge redistribution and magnetic correlations to pairing competition, offering insight into how layer polarization and multiorbital interactions control pairing tendencies in bilayer nickelates.|
|**2026-10-15**|**Spectral signatures of spin textures and the emergence of a pseudogap**|Matteo Quinzi, Andrea Pintus, Tommaso Chiarotti, Massimo Capone, Nicola Marzari|[[arxiv:2610.19269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19269)]|Mean-field solutions that break translational symmetry can generate frequency- and momentum-dependent self-energies mimicking strong-correlation physics, challenging the assumption that such spectral features require nonperturbative treatment. Studying the 2D Hubbard model, the authors find that the doped antiferromagnet hosts many quasi-degenerate self-consistent solutions with antiferromagnetic textures forming antiphase domains separated by hole-rich metallic interfaces. These solutions produce spectral functions exhibiting hallmark pseudogap phenomena, including nodal-antinodal dichotomy, Fermi arcs, and a Luttinger surface. The work offers a conceptually simple real-space picture of the pseudogap as the spectral signature of interplay between antiferromagnetic domains and metallic interfaces, echoing spin-fluctuation mechanisms identified by nonperturbative methods.|
|**2026-10-15**|**On the Mathematical Structure of Helical Shear in Anelastic Continua**|Souhayl Sadik, Giuseppe Saccomandi, Luigi Vergori|[[arxiv:2610.19221](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19221)]|The paper analyzes helical shear in a thick-walled tube of incompressible hyper-anelastic material containing radially distributed axial screw dislocations, using the Bilby–Kröner–Lee decomposition to encode defects in a Weitzenböck material manifold. Equilibrium reduces to an algebraic system per radius in which the azimuthal and axial shear modes couple only when dislocations are present and the strain energy depends on the second invariant. Exact solutions are derived for several material models (neo-Hookean, Fung–Demiray, Gent, Knowles, Mooney–Rivlin) and numerical ones for Gent–Gent, revealing that dislocations stiffen the tube, couple the shear modes chirally, and can restrict admissible dislocation strength or traction regions. A weakly nonlinear expansion shows dislocation effects are absent at first order but produce chiral coupling at second order and stiffness corrections at third, extending Horgan–Saccomandi universal relations to dislocated tubes.|
|**2026-10-15**|**Selective Control and Imaging of Higgs and Goldstone Modes with a Quantum Simulator**|Connie Miao, Pranjal Praneel, Thomas G. Kiely, Eliott Rosenberg, Nicole S. Petukhov|[[arxiv:2610.19219](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19219)]|The paper realizes the 2D Bose-Hubbard model at unit filling on a transmon-qubit array to study collective Higgs (amplitude) and Goldstone (phase) modes of the superfluid order parameter. Higgs modes are excited via coupling modulation and detected through doublon density, while Goldstone modes are excited by spatially tailored chemical-potential modulation and detected via local density changes, with the two modes exhibiting distinct symmetries and no mutual coupling. Varying the system geometry shows that finite size opens spectral gaps, making the Higgs mode a coherent, long-lived excitation that would otherwise be obscured by decay into a low-energy continuum in the thermodynamic limit. This demonstrates that mesoscopic quantum simulators can isolate and probe phenomena that are hard to resolve at large scales.|
|**2026-10-15**|**Work statistics of sudden quantum quenches: A random matrix theory perspective on Gaussianity and its deviations**|Miguel Tierz|[[arxiv:2509.09640](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.09640)]|For sudden quantum quenches, the work distribution is shown to reduce to the statistics of traces of powers of Haar-random unitaries. These results connect quench work statistics to random matrix theory and clarify when Gaussian behavior should be expected or fail.|

|**2026-10-15**|**The Physics Is the Network: Fast, Training-Free Neural Emulation**|Luka Todorovic, Kaushal Gianchandani, Sai Ravela|[[arxiv:2610.20525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20525)]|The paper challenges the assumption that neural emulation requires training a learned surrogate for known physics. It proposes PolyNets, which combine a continuous-time neural representation of known polynomial dynamics with a simple recurrent network for discrete-time integration, requiring no training while preserving the prescribed numerical scheme for efficient hardware execution. Tested on the 1D Korteweg-de Vries equation against CPU/GPU Runge-Kutta solvers and three trained neural models, PolyNets match numerical solver accuracy—even at amplitudes outside the trained models' conditions—while delivering high, approximately resolution-independent GPU throughput and favorable ensemble scaling. This suggests training-free, physics-preserving network architectures can outperform learned emulators in both fidelity and efficiency.|

This yields explicit matrix soliton solutions from a trivial seed, and on a plane-wave background produces breathers, dark solitons, rogue waves, and "beating solitons" for the two-component vector Fokas-Lenells equation.
