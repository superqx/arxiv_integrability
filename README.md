## High Energy Theory

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-10-01**|**Full Eigenstate Thermalization in Integrable Spin Systems**|Tanay Pathak|[[arxiv:2510.05887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05887)]|The paper tests "full ETH," a generalization of the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis that predicts higher-order correlations of local operator matrix elements match thermal free cumulants. Using exact diagonalization of two integrable spin models, the Ising and XXZ Heisenberg models, the authors compare their behavior against predictions for chaotic systems. They find notable deviations from full ETH in these integrable models, including in the OTOC, whose late-time behavior is governed by the fourth-order free cumulant but with dynamics markedly different from nonintegrable systems. The results clarify how integrability alters thermalization beyond standard ETH.|
|**2026-10-01**|**Time-like Janus Solution -- holographic global quantum quench --**|Kenta Suzuki|[[arxiv:2509.01925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.01925)]|The paper constructs a time-like Janus solution in asymptotically AdS spacetime, mediated by a time-dependent dilaton field, which violates the null energy condition but serves as a toy model for a holographic global quantum quench. The dual CFT is described via conformal perturbation theory, where the scalar operator dual to the dilaton is coupled to a global-quench-type time-dependent source. The authors compute one-point functions of the scalar operator and stress-energy tensor, finding consistency with the proposed CFT picture, and evaluate late-time holographic entanglement entropy after the quench. A finite-temperature generalization interpretable as a time-dependent black hole is also discussed, highlighting the solution's utility for modeling out-of-equilibrium dynamics in holography.|

## Mathematical Physics

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-10-01**|**Half-space KPZ equation from a class of nonlinear SPDEs**|Kevin Yang|[[arxiv:2609.40352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40352)]|The paper addresses the problem of deriving the half-space Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) equation as a continuum limit from a broad class of Ginzburg-Landau stochastic partial differential equations under weak nonlinearity scaling. The authors establish this limiting procedure for general nonlinear SPDEs, rather than restricting to integrable models or their perturbations. The key result is the first half-space universality theorem for the KPZ equation beyond integrable settings. This significantly broadens the class of microscopic models known to converge to half-space KPZ, extending universality to non-integrable systems.|

## Condensed Matter

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-10-01**|**Efficient Calculation of Equilibrium Correlation Functions**|Yizhi Shen, Roel Van Beeumen, Wibe A. de Jong, Efekan K\"okc\"u|[[arxiv:2609.40349](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.40349)]|Computing equilibrium correlation functions conventionally requires a separate circuit per time point, leading to at least O(N_t) shots and O(N_t²) gates. The authors encode time into O(log N_t) ancilla qubits so a single circuit handles all time points, run only O(log N_t) times, cutting total resources to O(N_t log N_t). They prove this scaling is optimal and demonstrate it on the Green's function of a 1D spinless Hubbard model.|
|**2026-10-01**|**Weyl superconductivity from Feshbach resonance in the three-dimensional repulsive Hubbard model**|Taekyoung Kim, Pit Bermes, Giorgia A. Busin, Fabian Grusdt|[[arxiv:2609.39418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.39418)]|The paper addresses the superconducting phase of the three-dimensional repulsive Fermi-Hubbard model, motivated by recent experimental progress on its antiferromagnetic transition. The authors develop a 3D extension of the Feshbach mechanism, in which pairing is driven by near-resonant bound states of dopants whose internal structure differs qualitatively from the 2D case. This yields a time-reversal symmetry-breaking \(d_{x^2-y^2}+id_{z^2}\) pairing state that hosts gapless Weyl points, and the authors estimate its critical temperature. The results identify a novel Weyl superconducting state and provide testable predictions for quantum simulation and large-scale numerical studies.|
|**2026-10-01**|**Quantum quenches of scar states in the Affleck-Kennedy-Lieb-Tasaki model via Clifford augmented tensor network simulation**|Azar C. Nakhl, Ben Harper, Muhammad Usman, Thomas Quella|[[arxiv:2609.38848](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38848)]|The paper addresses how to efficiently simulate highly non-stabilizer (magical) quantum many-body states, focusing on the Affleck-Kennedy-Lieb-Tasaki (AKLT) model's ground and scar states. Using a qudit Clifford augmented tensor network method, the authors show these states have high Stabiliser Rényi Entropy yet remain expressible as low-bond-dimension Clifford augmented Matrix Product States, enabling time evolution via Clifford augmented TDVP. Quenching scar states from the Haldane phase to the antiferromagnetic Heisenberg Hamiltonian reveals a decay of string order that grows with increasing bimagnon number. This demonstrates that string order in AKLT scar states is sensitive to symmetry-preserving quenches even within the same topological phase, highlighting the utility of Clifford augmented methods for probing magic and dynamics in topological systems.|
|**2026-10-01**|**Symmetry-enabled tunable square-lattice Hubbard models in $\Gamma$ -valley moir\'e bilayers**|Rui Shi, Kejie Bao, Huan Wang, Jing Wang|[[arxiv:2603.23174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.23174)]|Square-lattice moiré systems offer a promising platform for simulating Hubbard physics, but electrically tunable control of the hopping ratio \(t'/t\) had been restricted to specific valley configurations, leaving \(\Gamma\)-valley systems unaddressed. The authors show that in \(\Gamma\)-valley twisted homobilayers at small twist angles, an emergent layer-exchange symmetry localizes low-energy states on nested square sublattices and suppresses inter-sublattice hopping, while an interlayer displacement field breaks this symmetry and enables continuous tuning of \(t'/t\) over a wide range. They further establish a formal correspondence between \(\Gamma\)- and M-valley moiré systems as different symmetry limits of a unified framework. This work identifies a general symmetry principle for displacement-field tunability and extends electrically controllable square-lattice Hubbard physics to a broad family of \(\Gamma\)-valley materials.|
|**2026-10-01**|**Full Eigenstate Thermalization in Integrable Spin Systems**|Tanay Pathak|[[arxiv:2510.05887](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05887)]|The paper tests the full Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis (ETH), which extends ETH to higher-order correlations, in two integrable spin models—the Ising and XXZ Heisenberg models—using exact diagonalization. It compares the numerically observed ETH free cumulants with the thermal predictions and contrasts them with the behavior expected in chaotic systems. The authors also decompose the out-of-time-ordered correlator (OTOC) into contributions from second- and fourth-order ETH free cumulants. They find that while the fourth-order term governs the late-time OTOC dynamics in these integrable models, its behavior differs significantly from that in nonintegrable systems.|

## Nonlinear Sciences

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-10-01**|**Initial-State Precision as a Predictive Resource: From Tori to Strange Nonchaotic Attractors and Chaos**|Song-Ju Kim|[[arxiv:2609.38674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.38674)]|The paper addresses how much initial-state precision is needed to predict a nonlinear system to a given accuracy over a finite horizon, framing this as an inverse prediction problem quantified by a resource \(B_N\) in binary refinement bits. Using a scale-separable error-growth regime and an exactly solvable circle/Chebyshev map as a chaotic calibration, the authors analyze the quasiperiodically forced logistic map under phase-only initial uncertainty. They find a three-level precision-resource hierarchy: bounded growth for smooth tori, logarithmic-like growth for strange nonchaotic attractors (SNAs), and linear-like growth for chaos, with the SNA case yielding a precision cost proportional to \(\log N\). The results establish SNAs as an intermediate finite-precision resource class and show that their operational manifestation can occur far later than derivative-based sensitivity suggests, with activation horizons exceeding \(10^6\) iterations near the torus–SNA fractalization point.|
|**2026-10-01**|**Duality of Hamiltonian and Lagrangian formulations for integrable systems**|Pierandrea Vergallo, Mats Vermeeren|[[arxiv:2604.19655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19655)]|The paper addresses the difficulty of formulating Lagrangian and variational structures for integrable systems that are naturally described by Hamiltonian operators, especially bi-Hamiltonian systems. It introduces "Hamiltonian potential variables" that map Hamiltonian operators into symplectic operators on a dual space, extending the classical potential-variable trick used for the KdV equation, and develops the Lenard scheme within this symplectic framework to construct Lagrangian multiforms. Applying this to the KdV equation, its dispersionless limits, polytropic gas dynamics, and the constant astigmatism equation yields pairs of Lagrangian multiforms, one of which is new for KdV, and the first known Lagrangian multiforms for the latter two models.|
