|**2026-08-31**|**Exact spectrum and anomalous relaxation in the open disorder-free Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev system**|Soshun Ozaki, Hironobu Yoshida, Hosho Katsura|[[arxiv:2606.08079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08079)]|This paper investigates a disorder-free version of the Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model that is coupled to an environment, using the Lindblad master equation formalism. The authors derive an exact, spectrum-resolved solution for the Liouvillian superoperator, valid for any system size, by leveraging the model's integrability. A key finding is that the spectral gap, which dictates the long-time decay rate of correlations, does not vanish as dissipation is turned off when the thermodynamic limit is taken first, a phenomenon termed anomalous relaxation. This result, along with observed spectral transitions and the convergence of the dissipative form factor, suggests the model is a valuable platform for connecting open-system dynamics to chaos and quantum resonances.|

The model's Hamiltonian is structured as a product of a zero-momentum occupation number and a quadratic pairing Hamiltonian, enabling a closed-form solution for the spectrum and partition function. Key findings include an extensive zero-energy degeneracy and a dynamics where the out-of-time-ordered correlator is time-independent at leading order in the large-N limit, with corrections emerging only at order 1/N. The work's significance lies in providing a controlled framework to isolate the consequences of breakdown interactions without complicating factors like disorder or spatial structure.|

The authors use an exactly solvable kinetic theory of a longitudinally expanding gluon plasma to show that the system's approach to this attractor can be described by the decay of specific excited states. They establish a direct mathematical correspondence between these decay modes and the quasinormal mode spectrum of the attractor itself, providing a general framework for understanding such dynamics. A key result is the derivation of analytic prescaling solutions, which could be applied to other strongly expanding systems.|

The method simplifies calculations and offers a potential path toward more efficient computation of higher-order cumulants through integrable hierarchies. Its significance lies in providing a unified framework that bypasses case-by-case analyses, streamlining the derivation process.|

## Condensed Matter

|**2026-08-31**|**Exact quantification of nonlocal magic**|Piotr Sierant|[[arxiv:2608.28563](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28563)]|This paper introduces the concept of "nonlocal magic" to quantify the quantum resource that is inherently distributed across the correlations of a bipartite system, rather than residing in its local components. The authors solve the long-standing optimization problem by proving that a state's nonlocal magic is exactly determined by the distance of its entanglement spectrum from that of Bell pairs. They find that this quantity is tied to the task of universal entanglement embezzlement and that its presence is governed by how spectral weights are distributed across factor-of-two rank windows, not by total entanglement. This offers a practical, operationally meaningful way to measure a fundamental resource in quantum systems, with accessibility via tensor-network simulations and entanglement spectroscopy.|
|**2026-08-31**|**Rapid Charge Stability Diagram Generation from Device-level Modeling of Semiconductor Quantum Dots**|Ron Nodel, David W. Kanaar, Connor Nasseraddin, Tim J. Wilson, Hong-Wen Jiang, Jason R. Petta, Chris Anderson, Mark F. Gyure|[[arxiv:2608.28263](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28263)]|This paper tackles the high computational cost of simulating charge stability diagrams for semiconductor quantum dot devices. The authors propose a method combining Multi-Domain Multi-Model (MDMM) simulations with automated tuning and Full Configuration Interaction (FCI) calculations to parameterize a Hubbard model. This model then generates charge stability diagrams much faster than traditional voltage sweeps, as demonstrated on an Intel Tunnel Falls device with qualitative agreement to experimental data. The work is significant for enabling predictive digital twin models that can accelerate the design and optimization of quantum dot architectures.|
|**2026-08-31**|**Field-tuned quasiparticles and electronic structure in heavy-fermion YbNi4P2**|Will Broad, Owen Moulding, Takaki Muramatsu, Manuel Brando, Alix McCollam, Femke Bangman, Gertrud Zwicknagl, Kristin Kliemt, Cornelius Krellner, Sven Friedemann|[[arxiv:2608.28119](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28119)]|This paper investigates how high magnetic fields alter the electronic structure of the heavy-fermion compound YbNi4P2. Using quantum oscillation measurements, the researchers found that quasiparticle mass decreases significantly with increasing field, aligning well with single-ion Kondo model predictions. At a field of 17 T, they observed an abrupt change in oscillation frequencies, indicating a sudden modification of the electronic structure, consistent with a Lifshitz transition. These findings highlight the tunability of correlated electron systems under extreme conditions and offer crucial data for refining theoretical models of such materials.|
|**2026-08-31**|**Mechanics and statistics of a solvable model of an autophoretic colloidal chain**|Arvin Gopal Subramaniam, Rajesh Singh|[[arxiv:2608.28041](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28041)]|This paper introduces a class of driven active colloidal chains with autophoretic interactions that can be solved exactly. The authors find that at a fixed chain geometry, the orientational dynamics obey detailed balance and derive from a scalar potential, resulting in zero entropy production in that sector, while all dissipation is confined to the translational motion. They solve the reduced dynamics exactly for dimers and semi-analytically for longer chains, deriving closed-form expressions for fluctuations and total entropy production. The study also shows that adding dipolar interactions destroys this equilibrium structure, producing a genuinely non-equilibrium state with no static attractor, whereas monopolar coupling is necessary and sufficient for the solvable polarized state. This work provides a rare exact analytical handle on far-from-equilibrium active matter, revealing how symmetry can persist in driven systems.|
|**2026-08-31**|**Vectorized Symmetric and Fermionic Tensor Network Implementations for GPU-Accelerated Variational Monte Carlo**|Si-Jing Du, Johnnie Gray, Ao Chen, Garnet Kin-Lic Chan|[[arxiv:2608.27861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27861)]|This paper addresses the inefficiency of existing tensor network variational Monte Carlo (TN-VMC) algorithms on GPUs, which stems from a lack of vectorized operations. The authors introduce a "flat" formalism for block-sparse tensors with abelian symmetries, enabling batched computation and high GPU utilization. They demonstrate speedups of up to 300× over single-core CPU implementations in simulations of the 2D Fermi-Hubbard model. This work provides a practical pathway for leveraging GPU acceleration in symmetry-aware tensor network simulations of strongly correlated quantum systems.|
|**2026-08-31**|**Exact spectrum and anomalous relaxation in the open disorder-free Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev system**|Soshun Ozaki, Hironobu Yoshida, Hosho Katsura|[[arxiv:2606.08079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.08079)]|This paper develops an exact solution for the Liouvillian spectrum of a disorder-free, dissipative Sachdev-Ye-Kitaev (SYK) model, leveraging the model's integrability to derive eigenvalues and projection superoperators for any system size. The authors find that the spectral gap governing long-time relaxation does not vanish in the dissipationless thermodynamic limit, a phenomenon termed "anomalous relaxation" that hints at connections to chaotic dynamics. They also observe spectral transitions from complex to real eigenvalues with increasing dissipation and a convergence of the dissipative form factor to the standard spectral form factor. The results establish this model as a valuable platform for studying nontrivial open-system dynamics in many-body quantum systems.|
|**2026-08-31**|**Fractionalization, emergent SU( $N$ ) symmetries, and fragmentation in layered quantum spin-orbital models**|Pedro M. C\^onsoli, Aayush Vijayvargia, Onur Erten|[[arxiv:2605.22908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22908)]|This paper introduces layered quantum spin-orbital models to explore fractionalization and symmetry-breaking order. By stacking Kitaev-type layers and coupling them with Ising interactions, the authors map the low-energy physics to an SU(N)-symmetric Fermi Hubbard model on a π-flux lattice, revealing emergent symmetries. Mean-field analysis of the N=3 case uncovers a rich phase diagram including intertwined orders and flavor-selective localization. The ground states exhibit magnetic fragmentation, where spins show conventional or string-like ordering while orbitals remain in a quantum liquid, offering a concrete framework for studying fractionalized quantum critical points.|
|**2026-08-31**|**Exactly Solvable Disorder-free Quantum Breakdown Model: Spectrum, Thermodynamics, and Dynamics**|Kinya Guan, Hosho Katsura|[[arxiv:2603.17379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17379)]|This paper introduces a disorder-free quantum breakdown model with all-to-all interactions that is exactly solvable due to its Hamiltonian factorizing into a product of a zero-momentum occupation number and a pairing-only quadratic term. The authors derive closed-form expressions for the spectrum and partition function, revealing an extensive zero-energy degeneracy, and analyze thermodynamics, spectral statistics, and correlation functions. Key results show that the out-of-time-ordered correlator (OTOC) is time-independent at leading order in the large-N limit, with time-dependent corrections first appearing at order 1/N and reducing to a simple two-point function at infinite temperature. This work provides a controlled framework to study breakdown interactions absent disorder, spatial structure, and environmental coupling, offering a rare exactly solvable example for exploring quantum dynamics.|
|**2026-08-31**|**Superconductivity in the repulsive Hubbard model on different geometries induced by density-assisted hopping**|Franco T. The authors analytically show this hopping generates attraction in the bonding band and repulsion in the antibonding band, which can overcome onsite repulsion and produce non-s-wave pairing. Using matrix product states, bosonization, and perturbation theory, they map the ladder’s phase diagram and identify a superconducting phase with a spin gap, reached via a Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless transition. The work highlights a mechanism for unconventional superconductivity in repulsive systems and provides a concrete numerical and analytical characterization of the transition.|
|**2026-08-31**|**Bad Metal Behavior and Lifshitz Transition of a Nagaoka Ferromagnet**|Jonas Arnold, Peter Kopietz, Andreas R\"uckriegel|[[arxiv:2510.01909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01909)]|This paper uses an extended fermionic functional renormalization group method to study the Hubbard model at infinite on-site repulsion, a regime where strong correlations require a projected Hilbert space. The method reveals a phase diagram for a square lattice with nearest-neighbor hopping, showing a progression from a paramagnetic Fermi liquid at low densities, through antiferromagnetic stripe order at intermediate densities, to an extended Nagaoka ferromagnet at high densities. In the ferromagnetic phase, the spectral function displays a flat, broad band characteristic of an incoherent non-Fermi liquid, and the authors identify two distinct ferromagnetic regimes separated by a Lifshitz transition. These findings are significant as they provide a microscopic understanding of bad metal behavior and complex phase competition in strongly correlated electron systems.|
