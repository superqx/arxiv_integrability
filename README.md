|**2026-07-29**|**Krylov-Space Memory Cores**|Mohsen Alishahiha, Mohammad Javad Vasli|[[arxiv:2607.26011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26011)]|The paper introduces Krylov-space memory cores as stationary, depth-resolved structures that organize anomalous initial-state memory in thermalizing nonintegrable systems. The method uses stationary occupation profiles, residual equilibration fluctuations, Gibbs deviation, and long-time current fluctuations to characterize memory regions. Key results show that anomalous states, such as those exhibiting weak thermalization or many-body scarring, develop compact memory cores with significant residual fluctuations and persistent current activity, unlike generic reference states. This work provides a stationary framework for identifying structured quantum memory and how it remains dynamically encoded, demonstrating that compact memory is state-selective rather than a generic outcome of integrability.|
|**2026-07-29**|**Discrete power-law decay of subsystem distance after a quantum quench**|Bin Sui, Jiaju Zhang|[[arxiv:2607.25661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25661)]|This paper investigates how a subsystem’s state approaches equilibrium after a sudden change in the transverse-field Ising chain, using the Bures distance to measure deviation from the stationary state. The authors find that the decay follows a discrete power law, with exponents taking only specific values such as 1, 5/4, 3/2, 7/4, 2, and 5/2. The exponent is determined by the pre- and post-quench fields and the properties of an excitation-fraction function, with the previously known \( t^{-3/2} \) decay emerging as a special case. This work reveals a universal discrete structure in how integrable systems locally equilibrate.|
|**2026-07-29**|**Electromagnetic, gravitational wave, and static gravitational transmission through throat spacetimes: a constraint-wave asymmetry**|Jeff Riley|[[arxiv:2604.14238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14238)]|This paper investigates how electromagnetic, gravitational wave, and static gravitational perturbations transmit through spherically symmetric throat spacetimes like wormholes. The authors find that propagating radiation modes with ℓ ≥ 1 are strongly suppressed at low frequencies due to a centrifugal barrier, while the static gravitational monopole (ℓ=0) exhibits no such barrier and only polynomial attenuation. This qualitative asymmetry between wave and static modes is shown to be universal across various throat geometries, including a Damour-Solodukhin wormhole. The findings reveal a fundamental structural asymmetry in constraint propagation independent of the specific matter content, as confirmed by comparing Numerov integration, WKB estimates, and exact analytical solutions.|
|**2026-07-29**|**Rotating Black Holes in a Viable Lorentz-Violating Gravity: Finding Exact Solutions Without Tears**|Deniz O. Devecioglu, Mu-In Park|[[arxiv:2402.02253](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.02253)]|The paper proposes a two-step method for constructing exact rotating black hole solutions in a Lorentz-violating gravity model derived from Hořava gravity. By applying this method, the authors find Kerr-type solutions with and without a cosmological constant, revealing a singular region extending inside the ring singularity and confirming the validity of black hole thermodynamics with two Killing horizons. Key results include electromagnetic charge solutions only when the speeds of light and gravity are equal, aligning the model with observed time delays in gravitational wave and gamma-ray burst signals. This work provides a systematic approach to generating rotating black holes in Lorentz-violating theories, offering potential consistency with observational constraints.|

|**2026-07-29**|**Krylov-Space Memory Cores**|Mohsen Alishahiha, Mohammad Javad Vasli|[[arxiv:2607.26011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.26011)]|The paper proposes Krylov-space memory cores as stationary, depth-resolved structures to identify how anomalous initial-state memory persists in thermalizing nonintegrable systems. The authors analyze occupation profiles, residual fluctuations, Gibbs mismatch, and current fluctuations to characterize these cores. In systems with weak thermalization, confinement, or many-body scarring, compact memory cores with strong signals form within broader stationary halos, while generic states lack such features. This framework reveals that quantum memory is state-selective and structurally organized, not a generic effect of integrability.|
|**2026-07-29**|**Superfluidity without charge order in the attractive Hubbard model on the kagome lattice**|Xiaodong Jin, Yingping Mou, Rubem Mondaini|[[arxiv:2607.25983](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25983)]|This paper uses auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo simulations to study the attractive Hubbard model on the kagome lattice. At 2/3 electronic filling, the authors discover a superfluid transition belonging to the chiral-XY universality class, which occurs without any accompanying charge order even at strong interactions. They further show that, contrary to mean-field predictions, superfluidity emerges for attractive interactions at densities with van Hove singularities, also without charge ordering. The results challenge previous mean-field studies and establish quantum Monte Carlo benchmarks for superconductivity in geometrically frustrated lattices.|
|**2026-07-29**|**Interacting hydrodynamic modes in spinless fermions with dephasing noise**|Fabian H. L. Essler, Patrik Penc|[[arxiv:2607.25938](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25938)]|This paper studies the non-equilibrium dynamics of spinless fermions subject to dephasing noise within a Lindblad equation framework. The authors map the problem to an exactly solvable Hubbard model and analyze quartic fermion operators in the Heisenberg picture to determine their hydrodynamic behavior. They find that these quartic operators can be understood as interacting pairs of bilinear hydrodynamic modes, leading to persistent diffusive late-time tails—a result that contrasts with translationally invariant bilinear operators. The key significance is that hydrodynamic tails depend not only on symmetry or coarse-grained fluctuations but also on the spatial structure and effective size of the eigenoperators.|
|**2026-07-29**|**Charge-6e superconductivity from doping SU(3) spin liquids**|Yan-Qi Wang, Boran Zhou, Hui Yang, Zhi-Qiang Gao|[[arxiv:2607.25909](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25909)]|This paper proposes a method for achieving charge-6e superconductivity by doping SU(3)-symmetric spin liquids, extending prior work on charge-4e superconductivity from doped SU(4) phases. The authors analyze a bilayer triangular-lattice Hubbard model with SU(3) symmetry using parton constructions, examining doped Z₃ and chiral spin liquids. Key results include the emergence of charge-6e superconductors from pairing charge-3e fermionic trions in the Z₃ case, and both Abelian and non-Abelian chiral charge-6e superconductors from doping SU(3)₁, SU(6)₁, and SU(3)₂ chiral spin liquids. The significance lies in identifying doped SU(3) spin liquids as a platform where symmetry, fractionalization, and topology combine to produce exotic higher-charge superconductivity.|
|**2026-07-29**|**Kinetically induced order from mobility-constrained excitations**|Aprem P. Joy, Urban F. P. Seifert|[[arxiv:2607.25806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25806)]|This paper introduces a kinetic mechanism for inducing long-range order in systems with mobility-constrained excitations, where coupling to an order-parameter field activates movement. The method is demonstrated through two models: a spin chain with dipole conservation and the Kitaev-Kondo model, both showing that condensation of the order parameter enables kinetic energy gain. Key results reveal that thermal activation of these constrained excitations can counterintuitively enhance order at higher temperatures. The significance lies in identifying a general pathway where constrained excitations, rather than hindering order, actively drive symmetry-breaking phases.|
|**2026-07-29**|**Discrete power-law decay of subsystem distance after a quantum quench**|Bin Sui, Jiaju Zhang|[[arxiv:2607.25661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25661)]|This paper numerically studies the decay of subsystem distance after a global quantum quench in the one-dimensional transverse-field Ising chain, using the Bures distance to compare the time-evolved state to its stationary ensemble. The authors find that the late-time decay follows a discrete power law, with exponents taking only specific values like 1, 5/4, 3/2, 7/4, 2, and 5/2. The exponent is determined by properties of the pre-quench Hamiltonian eigenstates, including continuity and boundary conditions of an excitation-fraction function. The results reveal a universal discrete structure in local equilibration dynamics, generalizing the known t^{-3/2} decay for initial ground states.|
|**2026-07-29**|**Tip-Tuned Renormalization-Group Spectroscopy Unmasks a False-positive Topological Superconducting Vortex**|Zhenhua Zhu, Qun Zhu, Yong-Wei Wang, Gu Zhang, Jihai Zhang, Xu-Cun Ma, Qi-Kun Xue, Can-Li Song, Dong E. Liu|[[arxiv:2607.25510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25510)]|The paper addresses the problem of distinguishing true Majorana zero modes (MZMs) from false-positive zero-bias peaks in scanning tunneling microscopy (STM). The method uses the STM tip's coupling to drive a local boundary renormalization-group flow, transforming dynamical Coulomb blockade into a definitive falsification test. Key results show that lowering the tip coupling transforms a clean vortex-core zero-bias peak into a dip, revealing it as a conventional vortex state rather than an MZM, and resolves two-gap superconductivity. This approach is significant because it tests dynamical protection against dissipation, overcoming the intrinsic ambiguity of static spectral measurements.|
|**2026-07-29**|**From weakly to strongly-interacting driven-dissipative bosons in one dimension**|Martina Z\"undel, Lo\"ic Herviou, L\'eonie Canet, Anna Minguzzi|[[arxiv:2607.25508](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25508)]|This paper studies a one-dimensional driven-dissipative Bose-Hubbard model with incoherent pump and losses, analyzing it using two-point space-time correlations. The authors combine stochastic semi-classical simulations, tensor network methods, and Keldysh perturbation theory to characterize the system across varying filling and interaction strengths. Key results show Kardar-Parisi-Zhang scaling in spectral linewidths at large filling and weak interactions, while at weak filling and strong interactions, the nature of excitations changes and remnants of the mean-field transition are identified. The work maps important regions of the phase diagram for driven-dissipative bosons, bridging weakly and strongly-interacting regimes.|
|**2026-07-29**|**Organizing Principles for Moir\'e Quantum Matter**|Qiaoling Xu, Yifan Gao, Tao Zhang, Ammon Fischer, Yi Jiang, Hanqi Pi, Zike Fan, Dongdong An, Kun Zhou, Yingjian Li, Yongqing Li, Yuhao Fu, Lei Wang, Lijun Zhang, B. They find a diverse set of flat-band models, including kagome-like and checkerboard lattices, as well as topologically non-trivial spin-orbit-coupled bands beyond the standard K-valley picture. The model reveals that extensile isotropic active pressure drives material from high- to low-activity regions, with density contrast governed by a single parameter proportional to compressibility. By tuning compressibility, the authors can steer active turbulence into low-activity zones and stabilize a one-dimensional chain of vortices at sharp activity interfaces. The significance lies in establishing compressibility as a control parameter for managing density variations and turbulent flow in active nematic suspensions.|
