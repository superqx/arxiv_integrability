|**2026-08-25**|**Inflationary Kicks and Coulomb Filtering of Spectator Dark Matter**|Martina La Rosa, Rasheshwari Paul Chowdhury, Gianmassimo Tasinato|[[arxiv:2608.23451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23451)]|This paper tackles the problem of excessive isocurvature perturbations generated by light spectator fields during inflation. To solve this, the authors introduce a novel filtering mechanism using a series of "kicks" (delta-function mass contributions) that, in a dense limit, create a Coulomb-like potential. This filter suppresses large-scale isocurvature while producing a characteristic spectrum that rises as k^3 before flattening, a shape directly linked to the dark matter mass. The work is significant for providing an exactly solvable, EFT-controlled model that can be realized in specific inflationary setups, offering a concrete path to test spectator dark matter with a low inflationary scale.|
|**2026-08-25**|**Kerr-Newman and genuine Kerr black holes in the Bertotti-Robinson magnetic field**|Hryhorii Ovcharenko, Jiri Podolsky|[[arxiv:2608.21672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21672)]|This paper introduces a new exact solution to the Einstein-Maxwell equations describing a rotating, charged black hole embedded in a uniform magnetic field. The authors clarify that a previously published "Kerr-BR" solution actually describes a black hole with a special charge, and they derive a new, genuinely uncharged Kerr black hole solution in this magnetic environment. They analyze the physical properties of the new family, including singularities, horizons, and thermodynamic quantities. The work is significant for providing a cleaner, more physically realistic model of a black hole in a magnetic field, which connects to the known Wald solution in the weak-field limit.|
|**2026-08-25**|**Machine-Learning Search for Lax Connections**|Osamu Fukushima, Tomohiro Shigemura, Ryosuke Suda, Norihiro Tanahashi, Kentaroh Yoshida|[[arxiv:2608.05146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05146)]|The paper introduces a machine learning framework to search for Lax connections in two-dimensional non-linear sigma models, using local current data without pre-defined spectral curves as training targets. For the SU(2) principal chiral model and the symmetric coset S^2, the method successfully reconstructs full spectral-parameter families, but for the non-symmetric coset T^{1,1}, it converges to a compact ansatz that is a "fake Lax" connection—flat on-shell but not encoding the equations of motion. Nonetheless, this candidate yields a genuine mechanical Lax pair in its point-particle reduction. The findings highlight that machine learning can propose useful ansätze and reveal spectral structures, but low flatness loss alone is insufficient to certify integrability, necessitating rigorous analytic verification.|
|**2026-08-25**|**Cherednik integrable system: eigenfunctions at generic eigenvalues**|A. Mironov, A. Morozov, A. They find that the generic eigenfunction here has an involved structure with N! branches, where each branch is a sum over Weyl chambers of Noumi-Shiraishi-type power series. The significance lies in providing explicit formulas for these generic eigenfunctions, with particular cases illustrated, thereby advancing the theory of DAHA-inspired integrable systems beyond polynomial solutions.|
|**2026-08-25**|**Entangling Power: A Probe of Symmetry and Integrability in Quantum Many-Body Systems**|Ian Low, Pallab Goswami|[[arxiv:2605.20661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20661)]|This paper introduces the time-averaged entangling power as a diagnostic tool for probing symmetry and integrability in quantum many-body systems. The authors compute this quantity for Heisenberg spin chains, finding that entangling power monotonically decreases with increasing symmetry in two-site models, and exhibits sharp dips at special symmetry points in finite-size XXZ chains. In the thermodynamic limit, they decompose the two-magnon S-matrix into quantum gates, showing that entangling power vanishes at SU(2) points but reaches a maximum at the free-fermion point—a reversal of the finite-size behavior. This establishes entangling power as an operator-level probe that can distinguish symmetry phases and integrability features in quantum simulations.|

They provide explicit formulas for specific cases to illustrate the construction. The significance lies in solving the eigenfunction problem for a broader class of integrable systems, offering a more intricate generalization of the known results and deepening the connection between DAHA theory and integrable models.|

The authors employ an asymptotically exact WKB analysis, leveraging an exact solution from the quantum mechanical Demkov-Osherov model to achieve this. Their method yields precise connection formulas for the system's solutions at different infinities, revealing a deeper connection between classical integrable systems and solvable multistate Landau-Zener models. The practical significance is demonstrated by applying these formulas to unstable vacuum decay, where they provide a precise scaling law for the number of excitations, including subdominant corrections.|

|**2026-08-25**|**Interaction-mediated Co-existence of Altermagnetism and Topology**|Jasmin Bedow, Nitin Kaushal, Marcel Franz|[[arxiv:2608.23442](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23442)]|The paper investigates whether Coulomb interactions can simultaneously generate altermagnetic order and a topological gap in a Lieb lattice Hubbard model. Using unrestricted Hartree-Fock theory and exact diagonalization, the authors show that extended Coulomb interactions self-consistently produce both altermagnetism and an effective Ising-type spin-orbit coupling, leading to a topological altermagnetic correlated insulator with a quantum spin Hall effect. The model exhibits a large region in the phase diagram where these phases coexist, and the inclusion of inversion-symmetry breaking terms generates second-order topological phases with corner modes.
