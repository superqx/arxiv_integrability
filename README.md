|**2026-08-10**|**Negative association of Busemann functions in exponential last-passage percolation**|Erik Bates, Xiao Shen|[[arxiv:2608.07236](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07236)]|This paper addresses the breakdown of product-form invariant measures in exactly solvable KPZ growth models, specifically focusing on exponential last-passage percolation (LPP). The problem is that Busemann increments, which are independent along a single down-right path, lose this independence when considering multiple asymptotic directions simultaneously due to non-product-form joint measures. The authors prove that this failure is one-sided, showing that Busemann increments across arbitrary directions are negatively associated, and they derive an exponential concentration inequality for their sums on the diffusive scale despite the lack of independence. The significance lies in establishing a general structural property that holds for arbitrary weights, with only the Burke property being specific to exponential weights, thereby extending insights beyond the exactly solvable case.|
|**2026-08-10**|**Fugacity-Resolved Stabilizer Entropy in Critical Quantum Chains: Discrete Selberg Sums and Exactly Solvable R\'enyi Indices**|Reyhaneh Khasseh, M. A. Rajabpour|[[arxiv:2608.06995](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06995)]|This paper introduces a fugacity-resolved partition function for stabilizer Rényi entropy in critical quantum chains, tracking the distribution of Pauli-string degrees in the balanced Majorana representation. The method maps the problem to a checkerboard-weighted discrete Selberg ensemble, yielding exact product formulas and an inverse Jack–Kostka representation for specific Rényi indices (α = 1/2, 1, 2, 4). Key results include exact binomial, Gaussian, and variance-scaling behaviors at these indices, with finite-size numerics showing a transition from unimodal to bimodal distributions as α grows. The work provides a unified framework linking nonstabilizerness to Shannon–Rényi entropies and offers exactly solvable models for quantum information measures in critical systems.|
|**2026-08-10**|**A causal magnetic black hole with finite self-energy**|Mohsen Fathi, Ariel Guzm\'an, J. R. Villanueva|[[arxiv:2608.06639](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06639)]|This paper introduces a new nonlinear electrodynamics model to describe a static magnetic black hole. Instead of assuming a regular center, the model is built by requiring physically motivated conditions such as a Maxwell weak-field limit, finite magnetic self-energy, and causal propagation. The authors find an exact solution for a minimal two-kernel model and analyze its properties, including horizons, singularities, and thermodynamic stability. Significantly, they compute observable features like photon spheres and simulated images, revealing branch-dependent shifts that could help distinguish this model from standard black holes.|
|**2026-08-10**|**Complex dynamics perspective for birational maps of the plane arising from cluster algebra mutations**|Andrei Grigorev, Krishna Chaitanya Kalidindi, Andres Quintero Santander, Roland Roeder|[[arxiv:2607.08125](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.08125)]|Applying holomorphic dynamics, the paper analyzes birational maps from cluster algebra mutations to determine whether they possess conserved quantities or invariant fibrations. By computing dynamical degrees and identifying superattracting periodic points, the authors show that many of these maps lack such invariants, resolving questions posed in prior work. The approach also yields algebraically stable models for the maps, enabling the application of ergodic theory to construct invariant measures with positive entropy and Lyapunov exponents. This establishes a rigorous dynamical framework for understanding complexity in cluster-algebra-driven systems.|
|**2026-08-10**|**Coordinate-energy transformation and the one-point function for the Heisenberg-Ising XXZ spin-1/2 chain on the ring**|Eric I. Corwin, Nikolaus Elsaesser, Axel Saenz|[[arxiv:2506.14171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.14171)]|This paper addresses the challenge of fully diagonalizing the Hamiltonian of the Heisenberg-Ising XXZ spin-1/2 chain on a discrete ring. The authors provide an explicit "inverse coordinate Bethe Ansatz" transformation, which maps the known eigenstates of the Hamiltonian back to the coordinate basis, complementing the standard forward Bethe Ansatz. They rigorously prove this inverse formula for the case of N=2 particles on an odd-length ring with small anisotropy, confirming it numerically for other parameters and proposing it as a conjecture. Assuming the conjecture holds, the method yields an exact expression for the one-point function using Izergin-Korepin determinant identities and implies completeness of the Bethe Ansatz for this system.|

|**2026-08-10**|**Entanglement Mpemba Effect**|Ruicheng Bao, Yue Liu|[[arxiv:2608.07465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.07465)]|This paper introduces the "entanglement Mpemba effect," where a less entangled initial state can become more entangled than a more entangled counterpart under identical open-system dynamics, enabling faster entanglement preparation. The authors derive a general criterion for this reversal based on the relaxation spectrum and use a deterministic LOCC-reachability preorder to provide a measure-independent proof of the effect. They demonstrate substantial time savings in exactly solvable models and propose a trapped-ion protocol for experimental realization. The findings offer a new strategy for accelerating dissipative entanglement generation without modifying the engineered reservoir.|
|**2026-08-10**|**Fugacity-Resolved Stabilizer Entropy in Critical Quantum Chains: Discrete Selberg Sums and Exactly Solvable R\'enyi Indices**|Reyhaneh Khasseh, M. A. The authors map the problem to a discrete Selberg ensemble and derive exact product formulas and a Jack–Kostka representation for indices α = 1/2, 1, 2, and 4, showing distributions are symmetric and converge to Gaussians after rescaling. Key results include exact binomial behavior at α=1, variance scaling linearly with system size at α=1/2, and an L log L enhancement at α=2, with numerics revealing transitions to bimodal and endpoint-dominated profiles for larger indices. This work provides a rigorous framework for resolving nonstabilizerness statistics in critical quantum chains, with implications for understanding quantum resources and their asymptotic behavior.|
|**2026-08-10**|**Extracting the pairing gap from van Hove singularities in rf spectra of the Fermi Hubbard model**|Chuping Li, Kaichao Zhang, Junru Wu, Yuxuan Wu, Dingli Yuan, Pengyi Chen, Lin Sun, Qijin Chen|[[arxiv:2608.06883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.06883)]|This paper introduces a method for determining the pairing gap in the 3D attractive Fermi Hubbard model by analyzing van Hove singularities (vHS) in rf spectra. The authors classify four distinct singularity types whose spectral positions are algebraically linked to the pairing gap and chemical potential, allowing both parameters to be extracted from just two spectral features. Simulations show these singularities remain detectable under realistic broadening even when conventional methods fail, and at half filling a single measurement suffices. The findings provide a practical experimental tool for probing pairing physics in quantum gas simulators.|
|**2026-08-10**|**Excitonic Magnetism in Ruthenium Pyrochlores**|Swetlana Swarup, Yang Yang, Natalia B. The authors develop a microscopic theory of Van Vleck excitonic magnetism, deriving superexchange interactions within a singlet–triplet manifold and analyzing the resulting Hamiltonian via triplon spectra and variational methods. They map a magnetic phase diagram, identifying triplon condensation instabilities and reproducing known orders while predicting a novel phase unique to this framework. Applied to Nd₂Ru₂O₇, the theory places it near an excitonic quantum critical point, offering a foundation for connecting excitonic magnetism to spectroscopic observations like Raman scattering.|
|**2026-08-10**|**Dynamics of one-dimensional Bose-Josephson Junction in a Box Trap: From Coherent Oscillations to Many-Body Dephasing and Dynamical Freezing**|Abhik Kumar Saha, L. F. The authors identify distinct regimes, including coherent Josephson oscillations, many-body dephasing with collapse-and-revival behavior, and, at strong interactions, dynamical freezing with suppressed tunneling and high fragmentation. They find that varying the initial imbalance alone can trigger a crossover between these regimes at intermediate interaction strengths. The results offer a unified framework for these phenomena and show that the Bose-Hubbard model becomes increasingly inaccurate as higher orbitals become relevant.|
|**2026-08-10**|**Scattering phase shift in quantum mechanics on quantum computers: non-Hermitian systems and imaginary-time simulations**|Peng Guo, Paul LeVan, Frank X. To solve this, the authors propose two alternative simulation methods: using imaginary time for Hermitian systems or real time for non-Hermitian systems. Both methods involve non-unitary evolution, which they handle by combining block encoding and the Hadamard test, an approach that requires no mid-circuit measurements or Hamiltonian adjustments. Numerical tests confirm both methods match exact solutions, with the non-Hermitian approach being more practical for scaling due to its lower demand for ancillary qubits.|

The study’s significance lies in providing a simple, analytically tractable framework for the EHM that captures multiple phenomena and complements more complex numerical methods like dynamical mean-field theory.|
