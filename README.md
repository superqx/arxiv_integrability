## High Energy Theory

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-09-17**|**Auxiliary Field Deformations of the Lambda Model**|Christian Ferko, Cian Luke Martin, Pranat Sharma|[[arxiv:2609.18831](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18831)]|The paper extends the auxiliary field deformation paradigm to the lambda model, which interpolates between the Wess-Zumino-Witten model and the non-Abelian T-dual of the principal chiral model. The authors construct an auxiliary field deformation of this seed theory and exhibit a Lax connection whose flatness reproduces the equations of motion after imposing the algebraic field equations. Through a Hamiltonian analysis, they verify that the deformed model satisfies the assumptions of known theorems guaranteeing Maillet structure (ensuring involution of conserved charges) and the existence of a classical Yangian. This shows that, as in other cases, the deformation alters the classical dynamics while preserving the underlying integrable structure.|
|**2026-09-17**|**Exact interpolation between Fick and Cattaneo diffusion in relativistic kinetic theory**|Lorenzo Gavassino|[[arxiv:2604.00984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00984)]|The paper addresses how relativistic kinetic theory can connect Fick's diffusive transport to Cattaneo's hyperbolic diffusion within a single solvable framework. It constructs exactly solvable (1+1)-dimensional relativistic kinetic models in which a parameter \(a \in [0,1]\) tunes scattering from infinitely soft and frequent to hard and finite-rate, interpolating between the two transport laws. The full quasinormal mode spectrum is obtained analytically for all \(a\), revealing how purely diffusive modes continuously deform into damped propagating modes. This provides a concrete microscopic realization of mixed diffusive-telegraphic behavior and clarifies the kinetic origin of the transition between parabolic and hyperbolic diffusion.|
|**2026-09-17**|**Echoes of the Maldacena-Milekhin-Popov traversable wormhole**|Rajdeep Mondal, Abhishake Sadhukhan|[[arxiv:2511.22671](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22671)]|The paper studies wave propagation on the four-dimensional traversable wormhole of Maldacena, Milekhin, and Popov, focusing on linear perturbations of massless scalar and vector test fields in the wormhole geometry. Because the two effective potential barriers lie an astronomically large distance (~10^35) apart, direct time-domain evolution is infeasible, so the authors compute single-barrier scattering amplitudes via Numerov integration and then sum multiple reflections analytically. They find that standard parabolic WKB fails (predicting 1/2 transmission at the barrier top versus the true 0.62, and incorrect low-frequency scaling), that echoes appear at times T_m=(2m+1)D with algebraically decaying energy E_m∝m^{-1}, and that the quasinormal spectrum splits into extremely long-lived cavity modes (quality factors up to 10^41) and rapidly damped photon-sphere modes. The key significance is that only the photon-sphere modes of a single mouth are observable in ringdown, whereas the echoes and trapped cavity modes characterize throat dynamics at time scales far beyond detectability.|

|**2026-09-17**|**Two-Parameter Family of Nonlinear Dirac Equations With Scalar-Scalar plus Vector-Vector Interactions**|Avinash Khare, Fred Cooper, John F. Dawson, Avadh Saxena|[[arxiv:2609.18170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18170)]|The paper constructs exact solitary-wave solutions to a (1+1)-dimensional nonlinear Dirac equation combining scalar-scalar and vector-vector interactions, introducing a two-parameter family controlled by the exponents κ and p. The authors analyze the resulting solution profiles and their energy-to-charge ratio E/Q as functions of ω/m, finding a transition from double-hump to single-hump solutions at κ=1 and a universal change in E/Q behavior at κ=2, which they link to the onset of instability. They also derive the nonrelativistic limit, yielding a modified nonlinear Schrödinger equation, and use it to assess the stability of single-hump solitary waves. The results clarify how the interplay of the two interaction types and their nonlinearity exponents governs soliton structure and stability in Dirac systems.|
|**2026-09-17**|**Exact interpolation between Fick and Cattaneo diffusion in relativistic kinetic theory**|Lorenzo Gavassino|[[arxiv:2604.00984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.00984)]|The paper addresses the lack of a microscopic kinetic framework that continuously bridges Fick (parabolic) and Cattaneo (hyperbolic) diffusion. It constructs a family of exactly solvable relativistic kinetic theories in 1+1 dimensions, parameterized by a single scattering parameter \(a \in [0,1]\), that interpolates between infinitely frequent soft scatterings and finite-rate hard scatterings. The full quasinormal mode spectrum is derived analytically for all \(a\), showing explicitly how purely diffusive modes deform into damped propagating modes. This provides a concrete microscopic realization of mixed diffusive-telegraphic transport and offers a tunable toy model for studying the transition between parabolic and hyperbolic hydrodynamic behavior.|

## Condensed Matter

|**2026-09-17**|**A first introduction to Matrix Product State algorithms for the integration of Lindblad equation**|Christophe Chatelain (LPCT)|[[arxiv:2609.18841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.18841)]|This review addresses the challenge of numerically integrating the Lindblad equation for one-dimensional open quantum lattice systems, where standard methods become intractable due to growing entanglement. It compares four Matrix Product State algorithms—two direct integrations of the vectorized Lindblad equation (one enforcing density matrix positivity) and two stochastic unravelings (quantum trajectories and quantum state diffusion)—all extending the TEBD approach. The methods are benchmarked against an exactly solvable free fermion model, with discussion of their principles, implementation, accuracy, and computational efficiency. The work provides an accessible entry point for researchers seeking to simulate dissipative quantum many-body dynamics.|
|**2026-09-17**|**Entropy spectroscopy of a tunable two-site Hubbard molecule**|Uhjin Kim, Seokyeong Lee, Gibum Yun, Dongsung T. Park, Soobeom Choi, Sangwoo Jeong, Donghoon Kim, V. Umansky, Yunchul Chung, Hyoungsoon Choi, H. -S. Sim, Hyung Kook Choi|[[arxiv:2609.17818](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17818)]|This paper addresses the challenge of measuring entropy in coupled quantum dot systems, where the thermodynamic requirements of the Maxwell relation do not generally align with the readout of a single charge sensor, limiting prior extensions from single to double quantum dots. The authors develop an entropy measurement protocol that shifts both dot levels together at fixed detuning, defining a δ-axis along which the Maxwell relation depends only on the total double-dot occupation measurable with a single charge sensor. Applying this to a tunable two-site Hubbard molecule in a GaAs double quantum dot, they observe entropy decreasing as interdot tunneling increases, reflecting suppressed occupation of antibonding orbitals and changing thermal contributions of hybridized singlet and triplet states, in quantitative agreement with a two-site Hubbard model. This work establishes a thermodynamically consistent framework for entropy spectroscopy of coupled quantum dots, offering a scalable route toward characterizing larger Hubbard simulators.|
