|**2026-09-18**|**Schwarzschild Black Hole in Uniform Magnetic Field in Einstein-Maxwell-Dilaton Theories**|Liang Ma, Peng-Yu Wu, H. Lu|[[arxiv:2609.19261](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19261)]|The paper addresses the lack of exact solutions for a Schwarzschild black hole embedded in a uniform magnetic field within general Einstein-Maxwell-dilaton (EMD) theories. Using a solution-generating technique starting from the Kaluza-Klein Kerr black hole in a magnetic field, the authors construct static solutions that embed the Kerr-Bertotti-Robinson solution into EMD theories and extend the framework to multiple Maxwell fields and dilatons. The resulting equations of motion reduce to Toda-like equations, yielding analytic electrovacua associated with all rank-2 Lie groups, and exact Schwarzschild black hole solutions immersed in these electrovacua are obtained. This work significantly generalizes previous results by providing a systematic method for generating black hole solutions in string-inspired dilatonic theories with external fields.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Unfolded Krylov complexity: universal chaotic dynamics without false positives**|Johanna Erdmenger, Kyoung-Bum Huh, Hyun-Sik Jeong, Juan F. Pedraza|[[arxiv:2609.02228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02228)]|Krylov (spread) complexity has been used as a chaos diagnostic, but it can produce false positives because integrable saddle-dominated systems also show prominent peaks despite lacking random-matrix level correlations. The authors propose unfolding the spectrum before constructing the Krylov dynamics, removing the smooth non-universal density of states while preserving microscopic spectral correlations. Numerical and analytical evidence shows that unfolding suppresses spurious peaks in integrable systems while retaining the universal signatures of chaotic ones; an exact treatment of the logarithmic model maps its spectrum to a uniform lattice and eliminates the false-positive peak. This establishes unfolded Krylov complexity as a more reliable probe of genuine many-body scrambling.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Boundary Quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov Equations and Integrability of Quantum Field Theories with Time-Dependent Bulk and Boundary Coupling Strengths**|Parameshwar R. Pasnoori|[[arxiv:2608.31080](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31080)]|The paper extends a generalized Bethe ansatz framework for quantum many-body systems with time-dependent couplings from periodic to open boundary conditions, where the boundary conditions themselves vary in time. For integrable time-dependent bulk couplings, the method identifies the compatible time-dependent boundary conditions and reduces the time-dependent Schrödinger equation to boundary quantum Knizhnik-Zamolodchikov (BqKZ) matrix difference equations, whose solutions give the exact wavefunction. The work further shows that the renormalization-group invariants of the corresponding static model coincide with the dynamical invariants of the time-dependent model, providing a systematic route to exact solutions and conserved quantities in such systems.|
|**2026-09-18**|**On non-relativistic integrable models and 4d SCFTs**|Rotem Ben Zeev, Anirudh Deb, Hee-Cheol Kim, Shlomo S. Razamat|[[arxiv:2604.19885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19885)]|The paper establishes a precise connection between generalized Schur indices of four-dimensional N=2 superconformal field theories and the non-relativistic limit of the elliptic Ruijsenaars-Schneider model, showing that class S theory indices can be expressed via elliptic Jack functions (e.g., Lamé equation eigenfunctions in the A1 case). Using this eigenfunction representation, the authors verify that generalized Schur indices of different theories in the Deligne-Cvitanović series map onto each other, yielding nontrivial identities among unrefined sums of non-relativistic elliptic Calogero-Moser eigenfunctions for different root systems. They further conjecture that non-relativistic limits of various integrable models generate generalized Schur-like limits of N=1 SCFTs, illustrating this with the Inozemtsev model, the non-relativistic van Diejen model, and rank Q E-string compactifications, and argue that N=1 Schur indices generally correspond to the free fermionic limit of a non-relativistic integrable model. This work provides a unifying integrable-systems framework for computing and relating supersymmetric indices across different dimensions and theory classes.|

|**2026-09-18**|**Integrability of the deformed Toda systems**|Mikhail Vasilev|[[arxiv:2609.20679](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20679)]|The paper addresses the open problem of whether the long-range deformation of the quantum open non-relativistic Toda system introduced by Mucciconi and Petrov in 2020 is integrable. The authors construct an explicit 2×2 Lax operator whose commutative family of differential operators contains the deformed Toda Hamiltonian, and they prove that this integrable deformation works on both classical and quantum levels for both non-relativistic and relativistic Toda systems. They also provide an n×n Lax matrix for the open non-relativistic case producing the same Hamiltonians, derive a van Diejen-type deformed Toda system, and show that the algebraic Bethe ansatz applies at the quantum level. These results establish the integrability of the deformed Toda systems and open the door to their exact solution via Bethe ansatz methods.|

|**2026-09-18**|**Where solitons are in a KdV soliton gas**|Benjamin Doyon|[[arxiv:2605.18093](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18093)]|The paper addresses the problem of defining individual soliton positions in a KdV soliton gas at finite times, where overlapping solitons cannot be identified unambiguously. The authors define soliton positions as explicit solutions to semiclassical Bethe equations and construct a fluid-cell projection that isolates solitons within mesoscopic regions. They prove that the projected field consists solely of the remaining solitons, unchanged and exponentially supported, and that conserved densities converge to the empirical density of these positions in the weak limit. This provides a rigorous kinetic theory of quasi-particles for the KdV equation, a first step toward deriving El's 2003 kinetic equation for soliton gases, with methods generalizable to other integrable solitonic models.|

|**2026-09-18**|**Non-Thermal Effects in Fermionic Atoms Coupled to Open Cavities**|Jules Sueiro, Marco Schiro|[[arxiv:2609.20434](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20434)]|The paper investigates how fermionic atoms in a Fermi-Hubbard model behave when coupled to an open, dissipative cavity mode, focusing on whether non-thermal steady states arise. Using Keldysh diagrammatics, the authors derive and solve quantum kinetic equations treating the cavity as a source of non-equilibrium noise and dissipation. They find that without Hubbard interactions the fermions reach a non-equilibrium steady state with a non-thermal distribution that exactly nullifies the heat current between fermions and cavity, and they identify regimes where an effective low-frequency temperature description emerges. Adding weak Hubbard interactions drives a crossover toward a full equilibrium distribution and modifies the mean-field superradiance phase diagram.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Khinchin's ergodicity and typicality in statistical mechanics**|Dario Lucente, Marco Baldovin, Giacomo Gradenigo, Angelo Vulpiani|[[arxiv:2609.20433](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20433)]|The paper addresses why equilibrium statistical mechanics works even when the strong ergodicity assumptions usually invoked are not satisfied. Following Khinchin, the authors argue that only a large number of degrees of freedom and the choice of extensive observables matter, not chaotic microscopic dynamics. Using analytical and numerical results for two classical integrable systems, the harmonic chain and the Toda model, they show that key statistical mechanics predictions such as the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution emerge even without chaos. This supports a typicality-based foundation of statistical mechanics that does not require ergodicity or dynamical chaos.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Hubbard models with ultracold shielded molecules in optical lattices**|Kevin P\'erez, Joseph W. Desroches, Kaden R. A. Hazzard, Tijs Karman|[[arxiv:2609.20286](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20286)]|Shielded ultracold molecules in optical lattices have been expected to realize only a hard-core Hubbard model with effectively untunable interactions because of their strong, long-range repulsive shielding cores. The authors non-perturbatively compute the on-site and nearest-neighbor Hubbard parameters for shielded bosonic and fermionic molecules, treating the shielding core and dipolar interactions on equal footing rather than via zero-range approximations. They find that lattice depth and microwave-induced dipolar interactions independently tune on-site and off-site interactions over roughly ±10 times the tunneling rate, with the required strong interactions achievable in shallow lattices that also permit fast tunneling. This establishes shielded molecules as a flexible platform for extended Hubbard models and enables an "interaction microscope" that can resolve inter-dopant interactions in doped Mott insulators, which quantum gas microscopy alone cannot distinguish.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Semiclassical scaling of eigenstate thermalization in single-particle chaotic systems**|Yaoqi Ye, Xiao Wang|[[arxiv:2609.20052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20052)]|The paper investigates off-diagonal matrix elements of real-space observables in single-particle chaotic systems to understand eigenstate thermalization. Using a semiclassical approach based on Berry's conjecture, the authors derive the banded structure of observable matrices and identify a characteristic bandwidth tied to a late-time timescale inversely proportional to particle velocity. They show that for steep-wall confined systems the predicted magnitude follows ETH entropy scaling with an extra kinetic-energy-dependent factor absent in conventional many-body ETH, verified numerically in a generalized quarter-Sinai billiard. This clarifies the dynamical implications of Berry's conjecture and links single-particle eigenstate thermalization to many-body ETH.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Odd/Even or Half ? Entanglement Anomaly in the Bose-Hubbard model**|Humberto E. L\'opez-Hern\'andez, Santiago F. Caballero-Benitez|[[arxiv:2609.19884](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19884)]|The paper examines how the geometry of a bipartition affects entanglement entropy in the 1D Bose-Hubbard model, comparing contiguous (first/second half) and alternating (odd/even sites) cuts that share the same number of sites but differ in boundary arrangement. Using perturbation theory plus a generalized slave-boson mean-field approach with quantum fluctuations, validated against exact diagonalization and DMRG, the authors derive analytical limits for the Mott insulator and superfluid phases. They find that in the Mott insulator the contiguous cut obeys an area law while the alternating cut violates it with a volume law S ∝ N_s (an "anomaly"), whereas in the superfluid both cuts saturate to the binomial value due to delocalization. Because the slave-boson cost scales with the local cutoff rather than Hilbert space dimension, the method accesses system sizes far beyond ED and establishes these partition-dependent scaling laws as a genuine many-body feature rather than a finite-size artifact.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Bad metallicity in the semi-quantum regime of the Hubbard model**|Evyatar Tulipman, Vadim Oganesyan, Thomas P. Devereaux, Steven A. Kivelson|[[arxiv:2609.19264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19264)]|Bad metals show T-linear resistivity exceeding the Ioffe-Regel limit across systems with very different ground states, suggesting strong local correlations as a common origin. The authors test this using exact diagonalization of the infinite-U Hubbard model at small hole densities, computing thermodynamics and transport on finite clusters. They identify an intermediate "semi-quantum regime" where compressibility is roughly T-independent yet resistivity is T-linear and violates the Ioffe-Regel limit, coinciding with quasi-local ferromagnetic "spin cages" forming around doped holes. The results indicate that bad-metal behavior can emerge from minimal strong-correlation physics, resembling transport in semi-quantum liquid helium and various real materials.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Intractable enumeration problems are like Russian nesting dolls: structural properties of monomer-dimer coverings on two-dimensional quadratic lattices**|Yong Kong|[[arxiv:2609.19231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19231)]|The paper addresses the computationally intractable problem of counting monomer-dimer coverings on two-dimensional quadratic lattices, which belongs to the #P-complete class. It derives an explicit exact formula for the number of coverings with \(s\) dimers, expressed as \(s-1\) nested sums, leading to an \(O(2^s)\) exponential-time algorithm. The solution is governed by a sequence exhibiting double-exponential growth, revealing structural properties of the problem's exact solution. This advances understanding of why such enumeration problems are inherently hard while providing a concrete closed-form characterization.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Interlayer pairing mechanism for bilayer nickelate superconductors**|Jiangfan Wang, Yi-feng Yang|[[arxiv:2606.15298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.15298)]|This review addresses the theoretical challenge of explaining superconductivity in Ruddlesden-Popper bilayer nickelates such as La₃Ni₂O₇, focusing on why interlayer pairing dominates in these multi-orbital systems. It synthesizes experimental constraints—the hybridized Ni-3d_x²−y² and 3d_z² electronic structure, orbital-dependent correlations, Hund's coupling, and strong interlayer magnetic coupling—and formulates them into bilayer two-orbital Hubbard and minimal t-J models. The authors elaborate strong-correlation interlayer pairing mechanisms, emphasizing a hybridization scenario in which local d_z² singlet pairs supply the pairing energy while their hybridization with itinerant d_x²−y² electrons establishes phase coherence, and they analyze the resulting pairing symmetry, T_c dependence on internal and external parameters, and nontrivial normal-state behaviors. The work is significant because it provides a unified two-component framework that connects the atomic-limit interlayer valence bond picture to realistic band structures, offering guidance for interpreting experiments and for engineering higher T_c in nickelate superconductors.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Large Deviation Functions of Open Quantum Systems with Strong Symmetries**|Jiayi Gu, Hailong Wang, Shanhe Su, Fei Liu|[[arxiv:2605.06244](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06244)]|Open quantum systems with strong symmetries have non-convex global rate functions, so the Gärtner-Ellis theorem cannot be applied directly to obtain them from the global SCGF. The authors instead apply the theorem locally within each symmetry sector, computing sector-resolved rate functions and reconstructing the global rate function as their minimum envelope. Applying this to an exactly solvable model and an open Heisenberg XXX chain, they find sector-dependent fluctuations spanning Poissonian to super-Poissonian statistics and emergent intermittency. The framework thus enables large-deviation analysis in symmetric open quantum systems where standard global approaches fail.|
|**2026-09-18**|**Entanglement Properties of the One-Dimensional Dimerized Fermi-Hubbard Model**|Min-Chul Cha, Hoon Beom Kwon, Ji-Woo Lee, Myung-Hoon Chung|[[arxiv:2602.20990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20990)]|The paper investigates entanglement in the one-dimensional dimerized Fermi-Hubbard model to distinguish its phases. Using matrix-product-state calculations, the authors identify two insulating phases (at 1/2- and 3/4-filling) and a metallic phase, and characterize them through entanglement spectra. They find that the 1/2-filling insulator has a charge gap from the band gap enhanced by repulsive interactions, while the 3/4-filling insulator has a Mott gap from interactions, a difference reflected in the half-chain entanglement entropy scaling and entanglement spectrum distribution. This shows entanglement measures can distinguish interaction-driven from band-driven insulating phases.|
