|**2026-08-13**|**K3 atoms of the cubic fourfold and the BPS structure of the Painlev\'e I determinant line**|Mark Raugas|[[arxiv:2608.12191](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12191)]|This paper addresses the compatibility of semiorthogonal decompositions with Bridgeland stability for cubic fourfolds, a key issue for the dynamical protection of K3 atoms. The authors analyze exactly solvable models from the noncommutative minimal model program, showing that in uncoupled Fano cases, the quantum cohomology path exits the geometric chamber and enters the gluing selection region without crossing a wall, with the $A_2$ quiver providing a coupled counterpart. They develop a tau-JLO dictionary linking the deformed cubic oscillator and Painlevé I, establishing a determinant identity with all-orders flatness and computing closed-form quantum periods. The significance lies in conjecturing that nonperturbative jumps of the determinant correspond to automorphisms of the underlying Joyce structure, providing new insights into the interplay between stability conditions and integrable systems.|
|**2026-08-13**|**Fortuity and fragility in supersymmetric SYK**|James Chryssanthacopoulos, David Vegh|[[arxiv:2608.12160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12160)]|This paper examines the properties of BPS states in the N=2 supersymmetric SYK model. The central problem is that these states are "fortuitous," existing only for specific ranges of fermion number N, and the authors investigate whether they can be continuously extended to larger system sizes. They introduce a mathematical "decoder" to quantify the cost of such uplift, finding that while some models allow perfect extension, the generic one-flavor model fails, and the decoder's spectrum exhibits quantum chaos. The significance lies in proposing a new framework, linking metric fragility and BPS chaos, to characterize and distinguish between chaotic and exactly solvable BPS sectors.|
|**2026-08-13**|**Electromagnetic, gravitational wave, and static gravitational transmission through throat spacetimes: a constraint-wave asymmetry**|Jeff Riley|[[arxiv:2604.14238](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14238)]|This paper investigates how perturbations transmit through geometric throats in spherically symmetric spacetimes, focusing on electromagnetic, gravitational wave, and static gravitational fields. Using the Ellis-Bronnikov background as a base, the authors show that propagating modes (ℓ≥1) are strongly suppressed below a frequency threshold due to a centrifugal barrier, while the static monopole (ℓ=0) experiences only polynomial attenuation with no such barrier. By extending results to a family of throat shapes and a Damour-Solodukhin-type wormhole, they establish this asymmetry as universal for static throats, independent of the matter sourcing the geometry. The significance lies in revealing a structural constraint-wave asymmetry inherent to multipole decomposition, confirmed through Numerov integration, WKB estimates, and exact solutions.|
|**2026-08-13**|**Analytic Properties of Infrared-Finite Amplitudes in Theories with Long-Range Forces**|Luke Lippstreu|[[arxiv:2505.04702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.04702)]|This paper addresses how infrared divergences obscure the analytic properties of scattering amplitudes in theories with long-range forces, such as quantum electrodynamics. Using a charged scalar particle in a fixed Coulomb background as an exactly solvable model, the authors canonically quantize the system and verify that unitarity and causality are preserved, while also examining necessary modifications to the LSZ reduction formula and the optical theorem. A key result is that the Coulomb phase divergence is linked to real radiative divergences via crossing symmetry, offering a constraint on the latter. In place of the Faddeev-Kulish approach, they propose eliminating infrared divergences by fully solving the quantum theory of the asymptotic Hamiltonian, which enables a systematic study of these novel analytic properties.|

## Mathematical Physics

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|

|**2026-08-13**|**Exact solution for stationary states of a closed memristor with mobile charged vacancies**|Irina V. Boylo, Konstantin L. Metlov|[[arxiv:2608.12264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12264)]|This paper presents an exact analytical solution for the stationary vacancy distributions in a closed memristor model that explicitly accounts for electrostatic interactions between mobile charged vacancies. The analysis reveals that these interactions cause the formation of an intermediate electrically neutral region between areas of reduced and increased vacancy concentration. A key finding is that the memristor's limiting resistances in its "on" and "off" states are independent of the interaction strength, at least to first order in vacancy concentration. This work provides a precise theoretical foundation for understanding how inter-vacancy forces shape the device's terminal states.|
|**2026-08-13**|**Quantum mixing for eigenfunctions of rational polygons in configuration space**|Kai Hippi, S{\o}ren Mikkelsen|[[arxiv:2608.11728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11728)]|This paper addresses the quantum mixing properties of eigenfunctions in rational polygons, a class of integrable systems where standard quantum ergodicity results fail. The authors extend the equidistribution results of Marklof and Rudnick to off-diagonal elements, specifically for rational polygons excluding the torus, by leveraging weak mixing of the directional billiard flow in almost all directions. They also provide an alternative proof for the special case of 2-tori. The significance lies in demonstrating that quantum mixing occurs in certain non-ergodic geometric settings, broadening the scope of quantum-classical correspondence beyond the standard ergodic framework.|

|**2026-08-13**|**Probing Impurity Quantum Criticality with Entanglement Witnesses**|Mateo C\'ardenes Wuttig, Andrew J. Millis|[[arxiv:2608.12317](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12317)]|This paper investigates the relationship between entanglement and quantum criticality in the two-impurity Kondo model, a system realizable with coupled quantum dots or magnetic impurities on surfaces. Using density-matrix and numerical renormalization group calculations, the authors reveal that the non-Fermi-liquid critical point—which separates independent Kondo screening from an inter-impurity singlet—corresponds to a shift in each local moment's primary entanglement partner from conduction electrons to the other impurity. This transition is marked by singular behavior in impurity-bath entanglement and inter-impurity susceptibility. Crucially, they demonstrate that quantum Fisher information of collective spin and charge operators captures this same structure, making the entanglement signature accessible through experimentally measurable dynamical response functions at both zero and finite temperatures.|
|**2026-08-13**|**Exact solution for stationary states of a closed memristor with mobile charged vacancies**|Irina V. Boylo, Konstantin L. Metlov|[[arxiv:2608.12264](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12264)]|This paper presents an exact analytical solution for the stationary vacancy distributions in a nonlinear memristor model that accounts for electrostatic interactions between charged mobile vacancies. The analysis reveals that electrostatic repulsion creates an intermediate electrically neutral region separating zones of depleted and enriched vacancy concentration. Key findings show that the limiting resistances in the memristor's "on" and "off" states remain independent of the interaction strength to first order in vacancy concentration, despite the interaction's significant effect on the distribution shapes. This work provides a rare exact solution for a nonlinear, interacting system and clarifies the conditions under which electrostatic effects can be ignored in predicting device resistance states.|
|**2026-08-13**|**Fortuity and fragility in supersymmetric SYK**|James Chryssanthacopoulos, David Vegh|[[arxiv:2608.12160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12160)]|This paper investigates the properties of BPS states in the supersymmetric SYK model, focusing on how these states exist only for specific system sizes (fortuity) and can potentially be extended to larger systems (uplift). The authors introduce a "decoder" operator to quantify the cost and success of this uplift process, coining the term "metric fragility" for when uplift becomes expensive or fails. They find that while some models exhibit perfect uplift, generic cases eventually fail, and the decoder's spectrum reveals chaotic behavior consistent with random matrix theory. The significance lies in establishing a connection between the fragility of BPS states across system sizes and quantum chaos, offering a new tool to distinguish chaotic sectors from exactly solvable ones.|
|**2026-08-13**|**Two routes to quantum anomalous Hall states in altermagnets**|Makoto Naka, Shuntaro Sumita, Yukitoshi Motome, Hitoshi Seo|[[arxiv:2608.12124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12124)]|This paper proposes two theoretical methods to induce quantum anomalous Hall (QAH) states in altermagnets, starting from a topologically trivial altermagnetic model on a square lattice. The first method uses a staggered potential to break sublattice symmetry, yielding a Chern number C=1 and quantized Hall conductivity of \(e^2/h\). The second applies an out-of-plane magnetic field, creating a metastable topological state with Chern number C=2 and conductivity \(2e^2/h\), stabilized within a hysteresis loop. The work identifies distinct gap-closing mechanisms at the Brillouin-zone boundary and confirms chiral edge states with unique spin polarizations, offering experimentally feasible routes to achieve quantized Hall effects in altermagnetic materials.|
|**2026-08-13**|**Thermodynamic and structural behavior of one-dimensional divalent patchy hard rods: Wertheim's first-order thermodynamic perturbation theory vs exact results**|Ana M. Montero, Andr\'es Santos, P\'eter Gurin, Szabolcs Varga|[[arxiv:2605.21003](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21003)]|This paper analyzes a model of one-dimensional divalent patchy hard rods using exact solutions rather than approximations. The researchers compare Wertheim's first-order perturbation theory with exact results, showing the theory becomes exact in a sticky limit but fails for finite-range interactions. Key findings reveal richer structural behavior for finite-range sites, including distinct correlation length regimes separated by Fisher-Widom and Widom lines, which vanish in the sticky limit. The work clarifies limitations of perturbation theories and provides exact benchmarks for understanding association-driven fluids in confined geometries.|
|**2026-08-13**|**Starvation suppression in dense scale-free metabolic networks: Dynamical mean-field analysis of catalytic reaction networks**|Kota Mitsumoto, Shuji Ishihara|[[arxiv:2603.19850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19850)]|This paper investigates how the scale-free topology of metabolic networks affects their dynamic behavior, specifically regarding starvation responses. Using dynamical mean-field theory, the authors derive an exact solution for a catalytic reaction model on dense random networks with arbitrary degree distributions. They find that scale-free out-degree distributions eliminate the metabolic-starvation transition seen in homogeneous networks, and that power-law in-degree distributions generate a matching power-law in biomolecular abundances. The results, validated by numerical simulations, establish a theoretical link between network topology and metabolic dynamics, highlighting a potential evolutionary advantage of scale-free structures.|
|**2026-08-13**|**Emergent gauge flux and spin ordering in magnetized triangular spin liquids: Applications to Hofstadter-Hubbard model**|Jiahao Yang, Hao Tian, Si-Yu Pan, Gang v. Chen|[[arxiv:2601.12898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12898)]|This paper investigates how external magnetic fields and electron interactions shape the internal magnetic order of frustrated spin liquids, specifically in triangular lattices relevant to moiré materials. The authors develop a spin model and show that an orbital magnetic field promotes a chiral spin liquid with staggered internal flux, while a Zeeman field drives a transition to a state with uniform internal flux and conical spin ordering. They identify distinct thermal Hall and magnetic signatures for these competing phases. The findings provide a framework to distinguish these exotic states experimentally, with direct implications for understanding moiré Hofstadter-Hubbard systems and triangular-lattice antiferromagnets.|
