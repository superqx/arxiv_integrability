|**2026-07-27**|**Lifts of partial cohomological field theories and examples of bi-Hamiltonian structures in the non-semisimple case**|Guido Carlet, Dimitrios Makris, Sergey Shadrin|[[arxiv:2607.22084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22084)]|This paper introduces a method to lift partial cohomological field theories using a Frobenius algebra, extending prior work by Della Vedova, Lorenzoni, and Savoldi. The authors apply this lift to systematically construct non-semisimple homogeneous partial cohomological field theories whose associated integrable systems exhibit a second Hamiltonian structure. The key result is the verification of a conjecture by Buryak et al., providing an explicit formula for the second Poisson bracket in these new non-semisimple cases. Additionally, the study connects these lift constructions to Morimoto’s theory of lifting geometric structures to the Weil bundle of infinitely near points.|

## Condensed Matter

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-07-27**|**Exact Neural-Network Representations of the Motzkin States**|Runde Zha, Yuntian Gu, Chaohui Fan, Jia-lin Chen, Hai-Jun Liao, Tao Xiang|[[arxiv:2607.22522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22522)]|The paper addresses the challenge of representing Motzkin spin chain ground states, which exhibit logarithmic or square-root entanglement scaling beyond the area law, using neural networks. The authors construct exact, training-free neural-network representations for colorless and colorful Motzkin states via recurrent, feedforward, convolutional, and transformer architectures. Their method introduces causal prefix-sum modules and position-selective rectified linear gates to enforce height constraints, along with a causal stack module for color matching in colorful states. The results provide prototypic benchmarks demonstrating that neural networks can capture entanglement features inaccessible to tensor networks, offering a constructive design framework for strongly entangled quantum systems.|
|**2026-07-27**|**Exactness of Symmetry-Broken Self-Interaction Correction in the Strongly-Correlated or Classical Limit: Harmonium as a Demonstration**|Cody Woods, Yunzhi Li, Wenqing Yao, Chen Li, John Perdew|[[arxiv:2607.22488](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22488)]|The paper addresses the failure of standard density functional theory in strongly-correlated and classical limits. It demonstrates that symmetry-broken self-interaction correction (SIC) yields exact ground-state energies in the classical limit (Planck’s constant approaching zero) for the harmonium model—a two-electron system in a harmonic trap. The method remains usefully accurate across all values of Planck’s constant, from zero to the physical value. Additionally, the symmetric ground-state density can be recovered by spherically averaging the broken-symmetry density, confirming the consistency of the approach.|
|**2026-07-27**|**BCS states and D-wave condensates in the 2D Hubbard model**|Kazue Matsuyama, Jeff Greensite|[[arxiv:2507.01280](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01280)]|This paper studies BCS-type states in the 2D Hubbard model by relaxing trial wavefunctions from Hartree-Fock ground states to local energy minima within the BCS ansatz. The method reveals numerous nearly degenerate local minima, similar to Hartree-Fock, but uniquely identifies a parameter region in the underdoped regime where these minima correspond to \(d_{x^2-y^2}\) condensates. Additionally, the results suggest \(d_{xy}\) condensation in the overdoped regime and condensates on triangular lattices. These findings are significant because they demonstrate that BCS states can capture d-wave pairing in the Hubbard model beyond conventional Hartree-Fock approximations.|

## Nonlinear Sciences

|**2026-07-27**|**Mersenne Representation, the Conolly Sequence, and Soliton Profiles over Finite Fields**|Fumitaka Yura|[[arxiv:2607.22202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22202)]|This paper introduces a novel "Mersenne representation" of nonnegative integers, decomposing numbers into binary and nonbinary sides. The authors use this representation to prove a previously conjectured relationship between two integer sequences, A055938 and A080578, and identify the binary-side counting function with a shifted Conolly sequence. They then apply this combinatorial framework to the finite-field Box and Ball System (BBS), showing that the Mersenne representation directly reconstructs global integer-valued traveling-wave soliton profiles. The work is significant because it demonstrates how the structure of a number system can directly solve and reconstruct solutions for an integrable system, connecting number theory with soliton theory.|
|**2026-07-27**|**Lifts of partial cohomological field theories and examples of bi-Hamiltonian structures in the non-semisimple case**|Guido Carlet, Dimitrios Makris, Sergey Shadrin|[[arxiv:2607.22084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22084)]|The paper introduces a method for lifting partial cohomological field theories using a Frobenius algebra, extending prior work on lift procedures. By applying this technique to local polyvector fields, the authors systematically construct non-semisimple homogeneous theories whose integrable systems exhibit a second Hamiltonian structure. These results confirm a conjecture by Buryak et al., providing an explicit formula for the second Poisson bracket in previously unsolved non-semisimple cases. The work also connects the lift constructions to Morimoto’s theory of geometric structure lifts on Weil bundles, broadening the theoretical framework.|
