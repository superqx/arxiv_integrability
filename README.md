|**2026-11-13**|**Going beyond the standard quasiparticle picture: Entanglement spreading after quenching from highly entangled states**|Elisa Sabadini, Vincenzo Alba|[[arxiv:2611.15409](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15409)]|The paper addresses the limitation of the standard quasiparticle picture, which assumes entangled pairs and only applies to integrable initial states, by studying quenches from $n$-rainbow states in the XXZ spin chain—homogeneous but non-integrable states (for $n>1$) built from long-range Bell pairs. Using analytic arguments for the noninteracting XX chain and tDMRG simulations for the interacting XXZ chain, the authors show that in the XX chain the quench generates entangled multiplets of $2n$ quasiparticle species whose interspecies correlations, though vanishing as $1/n$, strongly suppress entanglement growth, a behavior captured by a generalized quasiparticle picture. In the interacting XXZ chain, however, the entanglement entropy grows at the rate of thermodynamic entropy exchange across the cut, with no signature of multiplet suppression on accessible time scales, both for homogeneous quenches and for joining the $n$ -rainbow state to an infinite-temperature state (for which a von Neumann entropy formula is conjectured). These results extend the quasiparticle framework beyond integrable initial states and reveal that interaction qualitatively alters entanglement spreading from highly entangled states.|
