|**2026-09-30**|**Quenched Cosmological Collider Physics: Random fields & white noise**|Matheus Curado Ferreira|[[arxiv:2609.30095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30095)]|The paper studies how a massive spectator field in de Sitter space responds to a linearly coupled, spatially quenched random source with a power-law time profile, aiming to understand disorder effects in cosmological collider signals. The fixed-realization problem is solved exactly via Lommel functions and a Mellin–Barnes representation, and after Gaussian disorder averaging the propagator's spectral function and heavy-field poles remain unchanged while the seed factorizes into hypergeometric sectors. Results show that a persistent source generates a late-time logarithm in the bispectrum, decaying sources make the Schwinger–Keldysh endpoint integrable, and fast decay makes the nonanalytic massive clock dominant in the squeezed limit; white noise removes explicit exchanged-scale dependence and permits destructive interference with the collapsed trispectrum branch. The significance lies in showing that the temporal profile controls the clock envelope, amplitude, and phase while its logarithmic frequency stays fixed by the heavy-field mass, refining predictions for cosmological collider observables under disorder.|

|**2026-09-30**|**Boolean Cumulants and Exact Reduced Descriptions of Renewal-Driven Systems**|Marco Bianucci, Riccardo Mannella|[[arxiv:2609.33621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.33621)]|The paper addresses reduced descriptions of systems driven by memoryless step noise with finite correlation times, where Gaussian-based approximations fail due to the underlying renewal structure. It introduces two exact complementary representations: a kernel description governed by Boolean cumulants of the jump distribution and a frozen-noise formulation adapted to stationary densities, with the memory kernel exactly resummed as a Boolean generator for exponential waiting times. Key results show that second-order closure is exact only for symmetric Bernoulli jumps (with leading error scaling as $(b_4/b_2)\lambda^2$), and that $N$-point Gauss quadrature surrogates preserve multi-time correlations up to order $2N-1$ , yielding exact support boundaries and Kesten tail indices for linear multiplicative systems.
