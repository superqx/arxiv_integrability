|**2026-10-19**|**Time-dependent integrable quantum field theories with dynamical boundaries**|Pradip Kattel|[[arxiv:2610.03870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03870)]|The paper addresses the construction and solution of time-dependent integrable chiral quantum field theories on finite intervals, a setting where standard autonomous methods do not directly apply. The authors develop a general framework that builds such theories from autonomous scattering and reflection data, showing that spatial homogeneity forces right- and left-moving spectral labels to be affine with a common slope, so bulk and boundary couplings sample a single spectral trajectory. A complete round trip yields boundary quantum Knizhnik–Zamolodchikov equations, with Yang–Baxter and reflection equations ensuring local consistency and boundary-qKZ flatness ensuring global consistency; the local Hamiltonian is reconstructed via Cayley inversion of bulk and boundary matching matrices. They apply the construction to rational SU(N) scattering with fundamental boundary impurities and an O(3) model with a spin-1/2 impurity, and for the SU(2) impurity they construct explicit boundary-qKZ solutions as lattice sums of off-shell boundary Bethe vectors, recovering static open-chain Bethe equations when the drive rate vanishes.|
|**2026-10-19**|**Fortuity and fragility in supersymmetric SYK**|James Chryssanthacopoulos, David Vegh|[[arxiv:2608.12160](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12160)]|In the N=2 supersymmetric SYK model, BPS states at fixed charge exist only over a finite range of N, and the authors ask whether raising the charge can extend BPS classes to arbitrarily large N. They introduce a decoder D whose spectrum measures exact uplift and its metric cost, defining "metric fragility" as the failure or rising cost of uplifting harmonic representatives, and test this in Chen's single-matrix model, the two-flavor SYK protected tower, and the generic one-flavor model. Exact uplift eventually fails in the generic one-flavor case, while the operator D†D shows Gaussian unitary ensemble level statistics, with its eigenvalues quantifying metric continuity across system size and their correlations probing chaos in the BPS sector. The work shows that metric fragility and BPS chaos are complementary observables of the same operator, distinguishing chaotic BPS sectors from exactly solvable towers.|

|**2026-10-19**|**Uniform restriction lower bounds and nongeodesic common nodal curves in integrable systems**|Xianchao Wu, Xiao Xiao|[[arxiv:2610.23836](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23836)]|The paper addresses whether a curve shared by infinitely many nodal sets of Laplace eigenfunctions must be geodesic, a question raised by Bourgain–Rudnick and by Toth–Zelditch and Han. The authors prove uniform L² lower bounds for joint eigenfunctions of quantum completely integrable systems under transverse crossing and isolation of a tangential characteristic branch, extending these to the multi-branch setting, and use them to characterize possible joint nodal curves on Liouville tori. They then construct real analytic metrics on the two-torus for which a fixed embedded nongeodesic circle is a nodal component of infinitely many Laplace eigenfunctions, giving a negative answer to the Toth–Zelditch/Han question. The work is significant for clarifying restriction obstructions in integrable systems and narrowing the possible nodal curves that can persist.|
|**2026-10-19**|**Folding to the Bessel-Clifford system, and Equivariant Topological Recursion**|Mohamad Alameddine, Alexander Hock|[[arxiv:2610.23320](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23320)]|The paper studies how symmetry reductions of meromorphic connections relate to folding and topological recursion, using the Bessel-Clifford system as a motivating example realized as a folding of a symmetry-invariant subspace of the 0-member of the Painlevé IV hierarchy. The authors introduce equivariant topological recursion for spectral curves with Z2×Z2 (Klein four-group) symmetry and prove that equivariant correlators fold to ordinary topological recursion correlators on the quotient spectral curve. They further show compatibility of this folding with wave functions and quantum curves, and in the Bessel-Clifford case obtain an explicit relation between equivariant and folded wave functions while recovering the quantum curve reduction. The work provides a general framework linking symmetry reductions of integrable systems to folding operations in topological recursion.|
|**2026-10-19**|**Time-dependent integrable quantum field theories with dynamical boundaries**|Pradip Kattel|[[arxiv:2610.03870](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03870)]|The paper addresses the construction and solution of time-dependent integrable chiral quantum field theories on finite intervals, a setting where dynamical boundaries and time dependence complicate standard integrability methods. It introduces a generalized framework that combines wavenumber rescalings, spectral perturbations, and higher-order phase modulations within a single small parameter, with coefficients tuned so lower-order Taylor terms vanish. Applied to the Kadomtsev–Petviashvili I equation, the method yields explicit second-order two- and three-lump solutions and third-order three- through six-lump solutions, whose long-time asymptotics reveal anomalous scattering of peak positions and amplitudes. |**2026-10-19**|**Mean-Field Dynamics of the Bose-Hubbard Model in High Dimension**|Shahnaz Farhat, Denis P\'erice, S\"oren Petrat|[[arxiv:2501.05304](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.05304)]|The paper rigorously validates a mean-field approximation for the dynamics of the Bose-Hubbard model on a square lattice in the limit of high spatial dimension. Unlike conventional mean-field approaches that average out interactions, the approximation here is taken in the hopping amplitude, yielding a nontrivial mean-field equation that retains the full on-site interaction. The authors prove a trace-norm estimate showing that the one-site reduced density of the Schrödinger dynamics converges to the mean-field dynamics as dimension grows.
