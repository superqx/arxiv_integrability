## High Energy Theory

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-11-02**|**Massive Scalar Quasinormal Modes and Quasibound States of Charged Black Holes with Logarithmic corrections**|Prosenjit Paul, Rickmoy Samanta|[[arxiv:2610.33883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33883)]|The paper investigates how a logarithmic deformation of the Reissner-Nordström metric, sourced by nonlinear electrodynamics, affects the dynamics of a neutral massive scalar field, focusing on both quasinormal modes (relaxation) and quasibound states (trapped modes with weak horizon absorption). Using first-order and sixth-order WKB methods, global matching, a generalized Leaver method, and time-domain evolution, the authors obtain consistent QNM estimates and show that even a small scalar mass shifts the potential barrier outward, raises it, and reduces its curvature, leading to weaker damping at fixed multipole and overtone. For QBSs, global matching and Leaver agree closely, and analytic checks show a local Padé approximation captures binding energy to about 0.04% error while leading Gamow tunnelling underestimates damping by roughly 25%; increasing scalar mass or nonlinear coupling strengthens binding but shortens lifetime via enhanced horizon absorption. The work also demonstrates orthogonality of an independently computed QNM–QBS pair using a relativistic bilinear framework with appropriate horizon regularization, supporting the consistency of the mode analysis in this deformed black-hole geometry.|
|**2026-11-02**|**Monodromy defects in ABJM theory and integrability**|Charlotte Kristjansen, Andrey Shusharin|[[arxiv:2608.10840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10840)]|The paper addresses how supersymmetric monodromy defects (vortex loops) in ABJM theory can be described within the integrability framework of the underlying alternating SU(4) spin chain. Using the KT relation and the algebraic Bethe ansatz, the authors derive a closed-form expression for the leading-order one-point functions of non-protected operators in the presence of these defects. They show that, as a special case, the result reproduces the spin-chain overlap controlling three-point functions of two maximal giant gravitons and one tiny graviton, and they also compute the first quantum correction to the one-point functions in the simplest setting. The work establishes vortex loops as integrable boundary states and provides a foundation for extending overlap formulas to more general defect configurations.|

## Mathematical Physics

|**2026-11-02**|**Dirac Equations With All Possible Combinations of Scalar-Scalar, Vector-Vector and Pseudoscalar-Pseudoscalar Interactions**|Avinash Khare, Fred Cooper, John F. Dawson, Avadh Saxena|[[arxiv:2610.41084](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41084)]|The paper addresses the nonlinear Dirac equation in 1+1 dimensions with all possible combinations of scalar, vector, and pseudoscalar interactions at the nonlinearity parameter κ=1, corresponding to the most general four-fermion interactions. The authors seek exact stationary solutions with frequencies 0 < ω < m and show that every such model can be reduced to suitable combinations of scalar-scalar and vector-vector interactions, with two combinations mapping onto known SS−(1/p)VV and VV−(1/q)SS cases. For the remaining six combinations, they find that the allowed frequency range and the solitary wave profile (single or double hump) depend on the parameters, and they compute the charge Q and energy E, showing that E/Q is independent of the coupling constant g. A key result is that all solitary waves in these models are bound states satisfying E/Q < m, which unifies the treatment of scalar, vector, and pseudoscalar self-interactions.|

## Condensed Matter

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-11-02**|**Solvable coupled cluster states in $d=\infty$ with prescribed momentum distribution**|Izak Snyman, Serge Florens|[[arxiv:2610.41976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41976)]|The paper connects the exponential coupled cluster ansatz with dynamical mean-field theory (DMFT) by showing that in the infinite-dimensional hypercubic lattice, static expectation values of coupled cluster wave functions can be evaluated exactly in closed form when cluster amplitudes admit a low-rank decomposition. This reduces the environment of a lattice site to a Gaussian Grassmann kernel, yielding a static "impurity problem" — a wave function counterpart of DMFT in which errors from evaluation are separated from limitations in state expressivity. For rank-one doubles, the momentum distribution is obtained in closed form, any momentum distribution can be realized as the exact and unique ground state of an explicit parent Hamiltonian with a gapless tunnelling spectrum, and the states variationally recover low-order perturbation theory for the Hubbard model. Their failure near the Mott transition exposes a fundamental limitation of coupled cluster doubles, while the family also contains a gapless state with zero quasiparticle weight and a smooth momentum distribution at the Fermi energy.|
|**2026-11-02**|**Airy distribution of the quenched spectral form factor in non-interacting fermions**|Oscar Martin, Satya N. Majumdar, Laura Foini|[[arxiv:2610.41393](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41393)]|The paper studies the late-time statistics of the scaled spectral form factor (SFF) in non-interacting fermion systems, where prior work on chaotic many-body systems and the random energy model found that the SFF logarithm is self-averaging and follows a reflected-Gumbel distribution. Considering N non-interacting fermions on a lattice of size L with an incommensurate single-particle spectrum, the authors show analytically/numerically that the rescaled logarithm of the SFF obeys an Airy distribution instead of reflected-Gumbel statistics. They further find that the SFF logarithm loses self-averaging, since its mean and standard deviation are of the same order. The results establish that non-interacting fermions violate the universal late-time statistics seen in chaotic models, while an interacting integrable model still exhibits reflected-Gumbel behavior, highlighting a sharp distinction between non-interacting and interacting integrable dynamics.|
|**2026-11-02**|**Exact Electron Dynamics in Linear and Chiral Cavities: Complete Field versus Single-Helicity Reduction**|Patrick Barron, Kalman Varga|[[arxiv:2610.40986](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40986)]|The paper asks whether a chiral cavity can induce Landau-like orbital dynamics for a free electron, and answers by exactly solving three quadratic electron–cavity models in the dipole limit at arbitrary coupling. A single linearly polarized mode renormalizes the electron mass, delaying its flight, slowing wavepacket spreading, and entangling it with the cavity, whereas the complete transverse field with both helicities preserves two gapless translations, keeping the electron ballistic with no synthetic magnetic field. Reducing to a single helicity yields two inequivalent models depending on whether the reduction occurs before or after the Power–Zienau–Woolley transformation, differing by a uniform field B = sλ²/(2ω_c); in the full theory the partner helicity's frequency determines which behavior applies, with Landau-like dynamics arising only at the soft-partner point. The results show that single-helicity projection alone does not create synthetic magnetism—the cavity's mode content does.|
|**2026-11-02**|**Theory of Convective Diffusion**|\v{Z}iga Krajnik, Takato Yoshimura, Alvise Bastianello, Enej Ilievski|[[arxiv:2610.40862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40862)]|The paper develops a hydrodynamic theory for diffusive-scale dynamics in one-dimensional systems with interacting sound modes but no self-interactions. It identifies three Gaussian relaxation processes—normal diffusion from microscopic noise, convective diffusion from sound-mode collisions (an effective multiplicative temporal noise), and an effective additive noise from surrounding hydrodynamic modes—with integrable systems described by generalized hydrodynamics as a pure case of convective diffusion. For convective diffusion, the authors derive an exact closed-form expression for higher-point dynamical correlation functions and show they differ structurally from those of normal diffusion. They further demonstrate that each sound mode exhibits a universal non-Gaussian charge-transfer distribution in its comoving frame, establishing anomalous fluctuations as a hallmark of diffusive-scale dynamics in integrable systems.|

|**2026-11-02**|**An abundance of periodic orbits in two-dimensional turbulence revealed by a diffusion-based generative model**|Jeremy P Parker, Tobias M Schneider|[[arxiv:2602.23181](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.23181)]|The paper addresses the scarcity of known short periodic orbits in two-dimensional Kolmogorov turbulence at Re=40, which limits periodic-orbit-based reconstructions of chaotic statistics. The authors train a diffusion generative model on nonperiodic vorticity sequences from direct numerical simulation, then modify the architecture—without retraining—to generate symmetry-aware periodic trajectories, which an iterative solver refines into exact relative periodic orbits. This yields 111 new orbits with periods T < 3, including 40 with T < 2, spanning all recurrence-symmetry classes. The abundance and diversity of these orbits indicate that converged periodic-orbit descriptions of spatiotemporal chaos may require far larger orbit libraries than previously assumed.|
