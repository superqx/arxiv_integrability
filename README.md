|**2026-07-28**|**The loop-nilpotent cohomological Hall algebra**|Shivang Jindal, Andrei Negu\c{t}|[[arxiv:2607.23544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23544)]|This paper constructs an explicit shuffle algebra model for the loop-nilpotent cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA) of a tripled quiver with a cubic potential. The authors use this model to prove several key results, including that the loop-nilpotent CoHA becomes supercommutative at \(\hbar=0\) and that it provides generators for the full preprojective CoHA. A significant consequence is a new characterization of the BPS Lie algebra and a novel formula for Kac polynomials based on dimensions of polynomial vector spaces. The findings also confirm a conjecture on spherical generation and, for ADE quivers, establish the loop-nilpotent CoHA as the positive half of a Drinfeld-Gavarini dual Yangian.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Horava Stars Revisited: New Phases of Incompressible Stars and Black Holes, and Buchdahl's Theorem**|Mu-In Park|[[arxiv:2607.23172](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23172)]|This paper revisits exact solutions for incompressible static stars in non-projectable Horava gravity, using z=3 black-hole metrics as the exterior spacetime. The author derives a modified Buchdahl bound on compactness that ranges from 4/9 to 1 for vanishing cosmological constant, and discovers Ultra-Compact Objects (UCOs) with compactness exceeding 1 that satisfy all four standard energy conditions despite having negative pressure. These UCOs include regular black-hole solutions with matter localized near a timelike core, enabled by Birkhoff's theorem in Horava gravity. The study also identifies negative-mass stars with positive pressure and proves Buchdahl's theorem for this gravity model, revealing new phases of stellar structure beyond general relativity.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Cherednik integrable system: eigenfunctions at generic eigenvalues**|A. Mironov, A. Morozov, A. The method involves analytically continuing the non-symmetric Macdonald polynomials, which yields a more complex structure than in the Ruijsenaars-Schneider case. Key results show that the generic eigenfunction consists of \(N!\) branches, each a power series of the Noumi-Shiraishi type, rather than a single series. The work clarifies the fundamental structure of eigenfunctions for this Cherednik system, illustrated with explicit formulas for special cases.|
|**2026-07-28**|**A Horizon-to-Boundary Dictionary Linking Smooth Horizon Continuation, Pole-Skipping, and \(SL(2,\mathbb R)\) Lowest-Weight Structure**|Yoon-Seok Choun, Ki-Seok Kim|[[arxiv:2607.03800](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03800)]|This paper addresses the mathematical ambiguity of "pole-skipping" near black hole horizons, where resonant frequencies make the field's retarded response indeterminate. The authors solve a scalar field in a two-dimensional black hole and show that pole-skipping occurs when two distinct boundary branches both yield smooth horizon continuations. They find that a branch-preserving continuation, governed by spacetime conformal symmetry, uniquely selects the causal response, and they introduce a strict criterion to distinguish true resonances from spurious points. The work builds a rigorous dictionary linking horizon regularity, causal continuation, and conformal symmetry, clarifying how local horizon physics constrains holographic observables.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Eigenfunctions of deformed Schr\"odinger equations**|Matijn Fran\c{c}ois, Alba Grassi, Tommaso Pedroni|[[arxiv:2511.10636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10636)]|This paper addresses the spectral problem for finite-difference operators with arbitrary polynomial potentials, which can be seen as solvable deformations of standard Schr\"odinger operators. The method leverages the open topological string/spectral theory correspondence to construct exact generalized eigenfunctions that are valid for all eigenvalues and describe both bound and resonant states. Key results show these eigenfunctions are entire functions in position space that become square-integrable only for a discrete subset of eigenvalues, and that special parameter loci yield spectral degeneracies or real energy spectra. The work is significant as it provides a rare exact, analytic solution for both bound and resonant states in a quantum-mechanical spectral problem.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Fate of entanglement in quadratic Markovian dissipative systems**|Fabio Caceffo, Vincenzo Alba|[[arxiv:2406.15328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15328)]|This paper addresses the problem of understanding how entanglement negativity evolves in open quantum systems described by quadratic Lindblad equations. The authors develop a hydrodynamic description by dividing the system into mesoscopic cells, where correlations are generated locally at early times and then transported via ballistic quasiparticle propagation under environmental influence. They show that in the hydrodynamic limit, negativity can be reconstructed from correlations between independently propagating quasiparticles. The approach is validated through quenches in fermionic and bosonic chains with gain/loss dissipation, offering a framework for tracking genuine mixed-state entanglement in driven-dissipative systems.|

|**2026-07-28**|**Coulomb branches, quantized zastavas, Kac polynomials, and shuffle algebras**|Dinakar Muthiah, Alex Weekes|[[arxiv:2607.24711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24711)]|This paper resolves a quantization problem for embeddings of Kac-Moody affine Grassmannian slices. While the original embeddings, constructed using fundamental monopole operators, do not quantize generally, the authors show that the quantized version exists for zastava spaces, a variant of the Braverman-Finkelberg-Nakajima Coulomb branch construction. By taking a limit, they construct algebras $\mathcal{A}$ and $\mathcal{A}^+$, analogous to the Borel and unipotent Yangians, and prove their Hilbert series match a version of Hua's formula for Kac polynomials. The work establishes an isomorphism between $\mathcal{A}^+$ and a shuffle algebra, leading to a proof of Negut's conjecture on the spherical generation of localized shuffle algebras.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Derangetropy Operators**|Masoud Ataei, Sepideh Forouzi|[[arxiv:2607.24705](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24705)]|This paper introduces "derangetropy operators," which reweight probability densities based solely on their own cumulative distribution function ranks. The authors prove that these operators are uniquely equivariant under monotone transformations and that their iteration condenses any distribution onto its median, following a universal law given by the Koenigs limit. A key result is that when balanced with diffusion, the distribution function obeys an overdamped sine-Gordon equation, with the hyperbolic secant distribution emerging as a globally stable steady state. The paper's significance lies in establishing a deep connection between probability theory, exactly solvable dynamics, and a range of mathematical structures, including Sturm-Liouville theory, the Virasoro algebra, and conformal invariance.|
|**2026-07-28**|**The loop-nilpotent cohomological Hall algebra**|Shivang Jindal, Andrei Negu\c{t}|[[arxiv:2607.23544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.23544)]|This paper develops an explicit shuffle algebra model for the loop-nilpotent cohomological Hall algebra (CoHA) of a tripled quiver with a cubic potential. Using this model, the authors establish its connection to quantized Coulomb branch algebras and prove it becomes supercommutative at a specific parameter value. They also derive explicit generators for both the loop-nilpotent and the full preprojective CoHA, leading to a new characterization of the BPS Lie algebra and a formula for Kac polynomials. The work's significance lies in providing concrete algebraic structures and confirming a conjecture on spherical generation, while also showing that for ADE quivers, this CoHA corresponds to the positive half of a Drinfeld-Gavarini dual Yangian.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Cherednik integrable system: eigenfunctions at generic eigenvalues**|A. Mironov, A. Morozov, A. Key results show that generic eigenfunctions are more complex, consisting of \( N! \) distinct branches, each of which is a power series of the Noumi-Shiraishi type. This work advances the theory of integrable systems by providing explicit formulas for previously inaccessible eigenfunctions.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Eigenfunctions of deformed Schr\"odinger equations**|Matijn Fran\c{c}ois, Alba Grassi, Tommaso Pedroni|[[arxiv:2511.10636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.10636)]|This paper studies the spectral problem of finite-difference operators with polynomial potentials, which are deformations of standard Schrödinger operators arising from supersymmetric Yang-Mills theory. The method extends these formulas to the general case of Y(glₙ) for arbitrary n in a related work. Key results include explicit, constructive expressions for these vectors that simplify their computation. The significance lies in advancing the combinatorial understanding of Bethe vectors, which are fundamental in the algebraic Bethe ansatz for solving quantum integrable models.|

|**2026-07-28**|**Self-energy pole optimization of superconductivity in the bilayer Hubbard model**|Taka-Shi Fujiwara, Shiro Sakai, Ryotaro Arita|[[arxiv:2607.24549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.24549)]|The paper investigates the bilayer Hubbard model at half filling, where a crossover from Mott insulator to band insulator induces a rearrangement of self-energy poles between bonding and antibonding bands as interlayer hopping is varied. Upon doping, this pole structure generates a band-selective pseudogap and enhances s±-wave superconductivity, with the order parameter peaked near the insulator-insulator boundary. Key results show low-energy anomalous self-energy poles develop simultaneously in both bands, cooperatively boosting pairing and acting as emergent fermionic excitations similar to single-layer systems. The significance lies in demonstrating that controllability of these poles via interlayer hopping offers a tunable platform for optimizing strongly correlated superconductivity.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Many-Body Protection of Topological Edge Memory in Strong Interacting Quenches**|Yuxiao Hang, Stephan Haas, Rishabh Jha|[[arxiv:2606.19437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19437)]|This paper investigates whether topological edge memory survives a quantum quench in a non-integrable, interacting system, specifically the bond-alternating XXZ chain. By analyzing quenches across various initial and final Hamiltonians, the authors establish a key distinction: the dynamics and memory retention depend on whether the post-quench Hamiltonian is free or genuinely interacting. Key results show that for free final Hamiltonians, edge memory decays as a power law, while for interacting final Hamiltonians, finite-time stability bounds and simulations demonstrate that interactions can protect boundary memory over long timescales. The significance lies in revealing a many-body protection mechanism that prevents fast scrambling, allowing topological edge memory to persist in strongly interacting regimes.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Cavity Control of Strongly Correlated Electrons Beyond Resonant Coupling**|Lukas Grunwald, Xinle Cheng, Emil Vi\~nas Bostr\"om, Michael Ruggenthaler, Marios H. Michael, Dante M. The authors develop a consistent cavity-QED formalism in the Coulomb gauge that includes both dynamical dressing from the vector potential and static screening from the dielectric environment. Key results show that at half-filling, the magnetic exchange interaction is controlled by a generalized Purcell factor, with polaritonic surface cavities enabling a net enhancement of magnetism observable via Raman spectroscopy; at weak doping, a surface cavity can reverse the nodal-antinodal dichotomy of the polaron dispersion, detectable with ARPES. |**2026-07-28**|**Entanglement Properties of the One-Dimensional Dimerized Fermi-Hubbard Model**|Min-Chul Cha, Hoon Beom Kwon, Ji-Woo Lee, Myung-Hoon Chung|[[arxiv:2602.20990](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20990)]|This paper investigates entanglement in the one-dimensional dimerized Fermi-Hubbard model using a matrix-product-state approach. The authors compute the ground state to identify two distinct insulating phases at 1/2- and 3/4-filling, alongside a metallic phase. They find that the 1/2-filling insulator has a band-gap-driven charge gap enhanced by repulsion, while the 3/4-filling insulator is a Mott insulator, with their differences revealed through entanglement entropy and spectrum scaling. |**2026-07-28**|**Strongly correlated Josephson junction: proximity effect in the single-layer Hubbard model**|Don Rolih, Rok \v{Z}itko|[[arxiv:2602.14796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14796)]|This paper examines the proximity effect in a single-layer Hubbard model within a Josephson junction, using dynamical mean field theory to analyze strong electronic correlations. The results reveal two distinct phases: a Mott-insulating phase with a large charge gap that strongly suppresses critical current, and a proximitized superconducting phase that behaves as a 0-junction with a gap closing at a phase bias of π. Key findings include a first-order transition between these phases with hysteresis, and the identification of sub-gap resonances in the self-energy of the Mott phase that explain its phase insensitivity. The significance lies in demonstrating that phase bias and junction transparency can serve as tuning knobs to switch between insulating and conducting regimes in strongly correlated systems.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Frictional work and entropy production in integrable and non-integrable spin chains**|Vishnu Muraleedharan Sajitha, Matthew J. Davis, L. A. For non-integrable chains, frictional work is shown to be proportional to diagonal entropy production, with deviations at low temperatures where quantum relative entropy provides a better description. In integrable chains, frictional work decomposes into contributions from independent subspaces with distinct effective temperatures. The findings reveal that integrability breaking improves work extraction in adiabatic regimes but hinders it under strongly non-adiabatic conditions, highlighting the role of system integrability in quantum thermodynamics.|

|**2026-07-28**|**Fate of entanglement in quadratic Markovian dissipative systems**|Fabio Caceffo, Vincenzo Alba|[[arxiv:2406.15328](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.15328)]|This paper develops a hydrodynamic model to describe the evolution of entanglement negativity in open quantum systems following a quantum quench. Benchmarking on Kitaev, tight-binding, and harmonic chains with linear dissipation shows that the negativity can be accurately reconstructed from quasiparticle correlations in the hydrodynamic limit. This work provides a powerful, general framework for understanding genuine mixed-state entanglement in driven-dissipative systems.|
|**2026-07-28**|**Impact of disorder and phonons on the Hubbard bands of Mott insulators in strong electric fields**|Tommaso Maria Mazzocchi, Daniel Werner, Enrico Arrigoni|[[arxiv:2310.11833](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.11833)]|This paper investigates the non-equilibrium steady state of a Hubbard model under a strong electric field, focusing on the interplay of disorder and phonons. When optical phonons are included, they provide dissipation pathways and introduce states within the band gap, which significantly enhance the stationary current. The key finding is that phonons, not disorder, are essential for facilitating relaxation and current at off-resonant fields by enabling in-gap states.|
