|**2026-11-18**|**Non-integrability of open strings via Pohlmeyer reduction**|Divania Carelse, Laila Tribelhorn, Konstantinos Zoubos|[[arxiv:2611.22570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22570)]|The paper investigates whether boundary conditions for classical open strings preserve integrability, using Pohlmeyer reduction to map string dynamics onto simpler field theories. The authors first verify that strings ending on Y=0 non-maximal giant gravitons reduce to a sine-Gordon model satisfying the Ghoshal-Zamolodchikov boundary integrability criterion. They then apply this criterion to Pohlmeyer-reduced models of open strings on AdS₃, successfully distinguishing integrable from non-integrable boundary conditions in agreement with recent analytic and numerical findings. The work establishes Pohlmeyer reduction as a practical framework for diagnosing open-string non-integrability.|
|**2026-11-18**|**Gravitational Memory Beyond Null Infinity through Finite-Distance Carrollian Screens**|Felipe Diaz, Sercan H\"usn\"ugil, Oriana Labrin, Leonardo Sanhueza|[[arxiv:2607.18675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18675)]|The paper addresses how gravitational memory can be characterized at finite distances rather than only at null infinity. It develops a Carrollian geometric framework on finite-distance null screens, using Robinson–Trautman spacetimes as an exactly solvable radiative setting to extract asymptotic Bondi data and compare finite-screen and asymptotic memory. The leading large-radius tracefree component of the finite-screen memory reduces to standard Bondi displacement memory, while finite-distance corrections retain additional focusing, embedding, angular drift, Coulombic, and near-zone information. This establishes Bondi memory as the universal asymptotic projection of a broader quasilocal Carrollian response, and shows that late-time screens near the final Schwarzschild horizon retain only isotropic null Brown–York stress.|
|**2026-11-18**|**Exact solution of two-dimensional Palatini Gauss-Bonnet theory on a strip**|M\'aximo Ba\~nados, Marc Henneaux|[[arxiv:2604.15471](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15471)]|The paper studies two-dimensional Palatini Gauss-Bonnet theory on an infinite strip, a setting in which the theory possesses only boundary degrees of freedom. Its phase space is identified with the cotangent bundle of the SL(2,R) group manifold, subject to a first-class constraint quadratic in momenta. Choosing the simplest gauge-invariant boundary Hamiltonian (H = 0), the authors show the dynamics reduce to geodesics on the SL(2,R) group manifold, with a mass set by the Gauss-Bonnet coupling, and identify left- and right-translation symmetries acting on the two ends of the interval. These exact results clarify the boundary structure and symmetries of the model and provide a basis for commenting on its quantum theory.|

## Mathematical Physics

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|

|**2026-11-18**|**Orbital Stability and Instability of Slow and Fast Solitary Waves of gILW**|Diego Yupanqui|[[arxiv:2611.21989](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21989)]|The paper studies orbital stability of solitary waves for the generalized Intermediate Long Wave equation (gILW) with power nonlinearity, a problem where explicit solitary-wave profiles are unavailable for p>1. The authors establish existence of even, positive, radially decreasing solitary waves via concentration-compactness and symmetric rearrangement, prove convergence to gBO and gKdV solitary waves in the limits of infinite and zero depth, and then use perturbation theory to transfer spectral information from these limiting equations to gILW. For slow waves (small δc) they obtain stability for p=1,2,3 and instability for p≥5, while for fast waves (large δc) they find stability for p=1 and instability for p≥3, so that for p=3 stability changes between regimes. These results extend the classical Albert–Bona–Henry work for p=1 to all p>1 and, notably, avoid any use of explicit profile formulas.|
|**2026-11-18**|**Baxter Q-operators from a Schwinger-Boson construction of the master T-operator and the mKP hierarchy**|Zengo Tsuboi|[[arxiv:2607.17642](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17642)]|The paper addresses the conventional Schur-function definition of the master T-operator for rational inhomogeneous gl(M) spin chains and proposes an alternative construction. By realizing gl(M) on an auxiliary Fock space of finitely many Schwinger bosons, the authors build a monodromy matrix and define the master T-operator as its Fock-space trace, with Howe duality recovering the Schur-function expansion. They show that in a suitable limit where Miwa variables approach inverse boundary-twist eigenvalues, the surviving terms reproduce the degenerate Yangian L-operators used in a separate Baxter Q-operator construction, yielding an explicit residue formula for the Q-operator; an independent Holstein-Primakoff contraction gives the same result. This unifies two previously distinct constructions of Baxter Q-operators and clarifies their relation to the master T-operator and the mKP hierarchy.|

## Condensed Matter

|**2026-11-18**|**Photon condensation driven by scalar spin chirality**|Dimitrije Stepanenko, Daniel Loss, Mircea Trif|[[arxiv:2611.23178](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23178)]|The paper addresses the challenge that gauge invariance forbids equilibrium photon condensation under spatially uniform electric-dipole coupling, so alternative mechanisms are needed. Starting from the half-filled Hubbard model in the Mott regime, the authors derive an effective Hamiltonian in which virtual ring exchange on triangular molecular magnets couples cavity flux directly to emergent scalar spin chirality. Using mean-field theory, collective-fluctuation calculations, and finite-size exact diagonalization, they find a phase with spontaneous chirality ordering, a macroscopic cavity displacement, and hybrid chirality-photon excitations, with a condensation threshold tunable electrically via molecular chirality splitting. The work establishes frustrated-magnet orbital degrees of freedom as a route to equilibrium light-matter ordering beyond conventional dipolar cavity QED.|
|**2026-11-18**|**Memory kernel and closure for systems driven by intermittent forcing with power-law waiting times**|Marco Bianucci, Riccardo Mannella|[[arxiv:2611.23092](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23092)]|The paper addresses systems driven by intermittent control variables with random amplitudes and power-law waiting times, where the memory kernel in the governing equation involves time-ordered G-cumulants. It derives an exact decomposition of these coefficients separating jump statistics from renewal statistics, and shows that for power-law waiting times with finite mean, long-delay coefficients converge across all orders to ordinary moments of the jump law, yielding a closed-form kernel as a superposition of frozen deterministic flux kernels. For 2<μ<3, second-order closure retains a finite error even with slow dynamics, while the frozen-flow equation gives an exact stationary mean; the telegraphic process is the only renewal case where second-order results are exact. An exactly solvable model with additive and correlated multiplicative noise confirms these predictions.|
|**2026-11-18**|**Strong eigenstate thermalization in Singer-cycle Clifford circuits**|Naoto Tsuji|[[arxiv:2611.22391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22391)]|The paper addresses the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH) in periodically driven quantum circuits, asking whether all eigenstates—not just almost all—can be thermal. The authors construct brickwork Clifford circuits on L qubits whose Pauli-operator dynamics is governed by a Singer cycle, an irreducible symplectic matrix over F₂ of maximal order 2^L+1. They prove a strong form of infinite-temperature ETH: every Floquet eigenstate is thermal, with uniform bounds on both diagonal deviations and off-diagonal matrix elements for observables supported on a fixed number of sites. They further show that temporal fluctuations are exponentially suppressed in the thermodynamic limit, so long-time averages approach infinite-temperature values for arbitrary initial states and each observable stays near that value at almost all times.|
|**2026-11-18**|**Exact solution of the two-dimensional one-component plasma on the truncated Beltrami pseudosphere**|Josep Batle|[[arxiv:2611.21652](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21652)]|The paper solves the two-dimensional one-component plasma at coupling Γ=2 exactly on a truncated Beltrami pseudosphere, a constant-negative-curvature surface bounded by two parallels. A conformal map to a flat cylinder reduces the problem to cylinder interactions with curvature entering only via the area element, making the partition function a product of incomplete gamma functions with explicit density and determinantal correlations. Working at fixed shape in the large-N limit, the free energy contains a bulk (1/2)N ln N term, a linear term, the flat hard-wall surface tension times total boundary length, and a shape-dependent constant, with no ln N term—matching the conformal-anomaly prediction for Euler characteristic zero and agreeing term by term with the cylinder result of Shen and Forrester. The density approaches the flat contact value n_b ln 2 at both walls despite differing circumferences, and comparisons with exact results up to N=4096 and Monte Carlo simulations at Γ=1, 4, 6 show how boundary layers evolve with coupling.|
|**2026-11-18**|**Quantum vacuum inversion and multi-kink unbinding in the non-degenerate double sine-Gordon model**|Jonathan Lozano-Mayo|[[arxiv:2609.24861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24861)]|The paper investigates whether quantum fluctuations can unbind a composite soliton (multi-kink) even when the correction to its mass remains small, focusing on the non-degenerate double sine-Gordon model. Using one-loop analysis, tensor-network calculations of the lattice theory, and the Gaussian effective potential, the authors show that the vacuum at the multi-kink's center becomes the true vacuum at a coupling of order the classical vacuum lifting. This vacuum inversion survives beyond one loop, persists in the continuum limit, and is captured by the Gaussian effective potential; on finite chains, the multi-kink unbinds when the tension of the central-vacuum string between its halves drops through zero. The results establish a nonperturbative mechanism for soliton unbinding with only small mass corrections, highlighting tensor-network methods as a tool for studying such quantum soliton phenomena.|
|**2026-11-18**|**A Correlated Route to Antiferromagnetic Spintronics**|Joel Bobadilla, Alberto Camjayi|[[arxiv:2603.16552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16552)]|The paper addresses the limited role of antiferromagnets in spintronics, where spin-compensated electronic structure has confined them to passive functions. It identifies a symmetry selection rule requiring simultaneous breaking of particle-hole symmetry and sublattice equivalence to generate a spin-polarized dc charge current, and demonstrates this in the doped antiferromagnetic Hubbard model using dynamical mean-field theory. Doping and a uniform magnetic field each break one symmetry, with electronic correlations converting the resulting asymmetries into a sizable, field-tunable current polarization that peaks deep in the ordered phase and reverses at an emergent compensation point. The work unifies this mechanism with altermagnetic symmetry breaking and establishes electronic correlations as an active ingredient for spintronics in conventional collinear antiferromagnets.|
|**2026-11-18**|**Moir\'e Engineering of Square-Lattice Hubbard Models for Copper- and Iron-Based Superconductors**|Toshikaze Kariyado, Yusuf Wicaksono, Ashvin Vishwanath, Pavel Volkov, Zhu-Xi Luo|[[arxiv:2603.11153](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11153)]|The paper proposes twisted Γ-valley square-lattice homobilayers, exemplified by ZnF₂, as a tunable platform for emulating Hubbard-type models relevant to high-temperature superconductors. Using parameters derived from first-principles calculations, the authors show that the lowest moiré band realizes a single-orbital Hubbard model for cuprates, while the next two bands form a pₓ, p_y model capturing the minimal symmetry structure of pnictide d_xz, d_yz Hamiltonians. A Hartree-Fock analysis predicts an antiferro-orbital ferromagnetic insulator at quarter filling. The work establishes moiré engineering as a practical route to simulating both copper- and iron-based superconductor physics in a single controllable system.|
|**2026-11-18**|**Interference-caged quantum many-body scars: Local ETH-violation witnesses and deformation stability**|Tao-Lin Tan, Yi-Ping Huang|[[arxiv:2504.07780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.07780)]|The paper introduces "interference caging," a Fock-space mechanism in which escape amplitudes cancel exactly across the active boundary of a set of product configurations, producing exact eigenstates that are locally nonthermal. The authors give sufficient conditions for reducing this configuration-dependent cancellation to a bounded local caging operator, whose positive square vanishes on the caged eigenstate and serves as a local witness of ETH violation when its microcanonical expectation stays bounded away from zero. Using a chiral-shell search that requires no preselected wavefunction or target subspace, they recover the known scar tower in the spin-1 XY chain—proving interference-caged scars throughout an open interval of exchange couplings in the thermodynamic limit—and construct exact caged eigenstates with local witnesses in the square-lattice quantum dimer model under a checkerboard phase deformation. The work provides a construction-independent route to quantum many-body scars, with local ETH-violation witnesses and demonstrated deformation stability.|
