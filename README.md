|**2026-08-05**|**Krylov Complexity in Non-Inertial Quantum Systems**|Ming-Qi Ma, Shi-Cheng Liu, Lei-Hua Liu, Hai-Qing Zhang|[[arxiv:2606.29770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29770)]|This paper develops a framework for studying Krylov complexity in non-inertial quantum systems, focusing on how this measure of quantum state spreading depends on the observer's motion. The authors first show that for a single Rindler pair, the Krylov complexity exactly equals the mean number of correlated pairs, and that the dynamics are controlled by a balance between system parameters, yielding three distinct regimes including a localized phase. Extending to multimode quadratic Hamiltonians, they find this direct correspondence breaks down, meaning the simple single-pair model is a uniquely solvable benchmark. The work offers new insights into how complexity measures behave under observer-dependent transformations, which could have implications for quantum information in curved spacetime.|
|**2026-08-05**|**Exact solutions to complex Type IIB supergravity for complex superalgebra $F(4)$ and its real forms**|Eric D'Hoker, Michael Gutperle, Christoph F. The authors systematically classify which reality conditions can be imposed, yielding real-form solutions across various Type IIB theories with different numbers of time-like directions, including both known solutions (e.g., AdS₆×S²×Σ) and new ones like dS_{1,5}×S²×Σ in Type IIB*. They also prove that no real forms exist with so(7;ℝ)⊕so(3;ℝ) symmetry, confirming the consistency of their classification with real forms of F(4). The work provides the first exhaustive classification of real-form solutions from complex F(4), with implications for holography, particularly in relation to the polarized IKKT model.|

|**2026-08-05**|**Non-Abelian Hirota-Miwa Equations for the KPZ Universality Class**|C. Alexander Rodriguez|[[arxiv:2608.02772](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02772)]|This paper presents a new algebraic framework for deriving explicit, closed matrix equations for 18 models within the solvable sector of the KPZ universality class, spanning four different scaling regimes. The method organizes Fredholm determinant data into an overdetermined linear system on a directed lattice, leading to a compatibility condition known as the "diamond equations," which are then compressed into finite-dimensional matrix observables via a Darboux transformation. The authors show that these observables satisfy the same nonlinear structure as the seed data, so verifying a closed equation for a specific model reduces to checking a few linear conditions. The framework generalizes to produce variable-coefficient Hirota-Miwa equations, unifying prior results and linking the models to a central system in classical integrability theory.|

|**2026-08-05**|**Accelerated quantum Monte Carlo simulations of the attractive Hubbard model on the kagome lattice**|Jie Zhang, Xiang Li, Yu Wang|[[arxiv:2608.03894](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03894)]|This paper presents a computational acceleration scheme for determinant quantum Monte Carlo (DQMC) simulations, specifically targeting the attractive Hubbard model on the kagome lattice. The method combines fast Fourier transform (FFT) for propagator multiplications with a delay-update algorithm, enabling simulations on systems twice as large as previously possible. Using this approach, the authors reliably characterize superfluid quantum criticality at the Dirac filling and show that a proposed charge-density-wave order is likely a finite-size artifact. They also demonstrate that their scheme reduces computational cost scaling from cubic to approximately N^2.49, making it more feasible for sizes relevant to current optical lattice experiments.|
|**2026-08-05**|**Large scale neural quantum states reveal the interplay between superconductivity and quantum criticality in the Hofstadter-Hubbard model**|Christopher Roth, Andrew Millis, Tomohiro Soejima|[[arxiv:2608.02753](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02753)]|This paper investigates how the parent insulating state influences superconductivity in the triangular-lattice Hofstadter-Hubbard model. Using neural quantum states on large systems, the authors find a continuous topological phase transition between a quantum Hall insulator and a chiral spin liquid. Upon doping, they observe a topological superconductor where the pairing strength is unaffected by the parent state, but the superfluid stiffness is significantly enhanced near the critical point. This work reveals that proximity to the quantum critical point, rather than the nature of the parent insulator, sets the superconducting energy scale, demonstrating the power of neural quantum states for studying strongly correlated systems.|
|**2026-08-05**|**Two-Parameter Ansatz for the Violation of Eigenstate Thermalization**|Kohei Ohgane, Lev Vidmar|[[arxiv:2608.02744](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02744)]|This paper introduces a two-parameter ansatz to describe the continuous transition from eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH) compliance to its violation. The framework distinguishes between two breaking mechanisms: "fading ergodicity," a known route to ergodicity breaking, and "trapped ergodicity," a novel scenario where ETH is violated in finite systems but restored in the thermodynamic limit. Testing this on disordered and linearly-potential spin-1/2 chains, the authors find that observed ETH violations align with trapped ergodicity. This work provides a unified theoretical framework for classifying ergodicity-breaking mechanisms, with implications for understanding the thermodynamic limit of quantum systems.|
|**2026-08-05**|**Spin-charge separation in the triangular-lattice Hofstadter-Hubbard model**|Yuntian Gu, Hui Yang, Zhehao Dai, Yantao Wu|[[arxiv:2608.02727](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02727)]|This paper investigates the possibility of a chiral spin liquid phase in the triangular-lattice Hofstadter-Hubbard model, motivated by recent moiré material experiments. Using variational Monte Carlo with neural quantum states (NQS) and projected entangled pair states (PEPS), the authors directly observe spin-charge separation in real space via numerical spin-pumping and real-time spin/charge motion simulations. They also probe conjectured anyonic superconductivity, finding a positive two-electron binding energy that vanishes with increasing system size. The work establishes NQS and PEPS as complementary, powerful methods for detecting topological order and fractionalized excitations in strongly correlated 2D systems.|
|**2026-08-05**|**1+1d Lattice Dirac Fermions from Non-Onsite Vector and Axial Symmetries**|Tabin Dharanikota, Lukasz Fidkowski|[[arxiv:2608.02722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02722)]|This paper presents a new lattice model for a 1+1 dimensional Dirac fermion that maintains exact vector and axial U(1) symmetries at the microscopic level. To reconcile the quantum mechanical mixed anomaly between these symmetries, the model employs non-on-site symmetry actions within a Hilbert space built from graded tensor products and rotor degrees of freedom. The model is exactly solvable via a unitary transformation to a fermionic Villain formulation, and it can be extended to describe interacting Luttinger liquids. Notably, the construction shows that the free Dirac fixed point inherently contains irrelevant interactions, which the authors compute to leading order, providing a new tool for studying fermionic systems.|
|**2026-08-05**|**Periodic orbits and quantum many-body scars in integrable spin chains**|Elena Petrova, Maksym Serbyn, Marko Ljubotina|[[arxiv:2607.15132](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15132)]|The paper addresses the unclear relationship between quantum many-body scars and integrability in the context of the integrable XYZ/XXZ spin-1/2 chain. They analytically reconstruct scarred eigenstates and their towers, explaining the equidistant spacing from the decoupling of these magnon pairs, and show the structure persists under a transverse field that breaks integrability. This work establishes a direct analytic link between periodic-orbit scarring and the algebraic structure of integrable models, providing a microscopic foundation for scar phenomenology.|
|**2026-08-05**|**Engineering correlated Dirac fermions and flat bands on SiC with transition-metal adatom lattices**|Henri Menke, Niklas Enderlein, Roland Gillen, Yi-Ting Tseng, Michel Bockstedte, Janina Maultzsch, Giorgio Sangiovanni, Philipp Hansmann|[[arxiv:2410.17165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.17165)]|The paper proposes using transition-metal adatom lattices on silicon carbide as a platform for studying strongly correlated physics. Employing advanced computational methods, it analyzes the electronic structure of Ti, V, and Cr adatom systems, which feature narrow bands near the Fermi level. The results reveal distinct ground states: V forms a paramagnetic Mott insulator, Cr sits near a phase transition to a flat-band fermi liquid, and Ti realizes a heavy Dirac semimetal. This work identifies a versatile new platform for engineering and investigating exotic correlated quantum phases.|

|**2026-08-05**|**Painlev\'e Integrability, Auto-B\"acklund Transformation and the exact solutions of (1+2) Kudryashov-Sinelshchikov (KS) equation**|Apeksha Patil, Amlan Kanti Halder, Rajeswari Seshadri|[[arxiv:2608.03455](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.03455)]|The paper investigates a fourth-order (1+2)-dimensional Kudryashov-Sinelshchikov (KS) equation, which models pressure wave propagation in liquids with gas bubbles. |**2026-08-05**|**Non-Abelian Hirota-Miwa Equations for the KPZ Universality Class**|C. The method involves recasting Fredholm determinant data as an overdetermined linear problem on a directed lattice graph, leading to a compatibility system of "diamond equations," which is then compressed via a Darboux transformation into a finite-dimensional matrix observable. The key result is that this observable obeys the same nonlinear structure as the initial seed data, meaning verification of a closed nonlinear equation for any model reduces to simple linear checks on kernel data. The framework unifies known results by showing the diamond equations are gauge-equivalent to the non-abelian Hirota-Miwa system, offering a significant structural link between KPZ fluctuations and classical integrability.|

This equality, along with a non-triviality condition, confirms that the system is superintegrable and that the torus action's momentum map provides the action variables. The work offers a fresh perspective on collective superintegrability, building on and complementing prior results in the field.|
