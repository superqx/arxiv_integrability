The authors prove that for every fixed nonzero dimensionless field, these quantities satisfy no algebraic differential equation in the coupling, ruling out Onsager-type formulas and broad classes of special-function representations. They close the countable set of previously unresolved exceptional fields by combining Galois conjugation with an analysis of the large-order growth of reversed-spin droplet coefficients, showing that near-square droplets at conjugate fugacities outside the unit circle produce growth that no cancellation can remove. The results extend to the Lieb lattice and decorated square lattices, and yield partial results in all dimensions d ≥ 2, establishing a sharp contrast between the solvable zero-field case and the nonzero-field model.|
|**2026-11-06**|**Discrete quadrature spectrum of composite bosons: Weyl relation, intensity correlations, and binned homodyne detection**|Itzhak Roditi|[[arxiv:2611.07776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07776)]|The paper addresses how a composite boson built from two fermions can appear elementary or composite depending on the measurement performed, using an exactly solvable paired-mode model. It compares classical drives, periodically binned quadrature statistics, and intensity correlations, deriving an exact product formula for the Weyl defect and showing that binned observables are piecewise constant and sensitive to single Schmidt eigenvalues crossing bin edges. Using a spectral filter and polar correction, the authors recover an exact Pauli algebra and herald a logical singlet from two independent copies, with finite-mode examples (Schmidt number ~3) succeeding at the percent level despite order-one Weyl defects, while near-unit g^(2) fails to control the binned algebra. The results are significant for clarifying which observables genuinely certify composite-boson behavior and for establishing convergence properties of the construction across flat and infinite spectra.|
|**2026-11-06**|**Lee-Yang theorem for fermions**|Chaithanya Rayudu, Takahiro Misawa, Andrew Zhao, Jun Takahashi|[[arxiv:2609.23942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.23942)]|The paper addresses the absence of a Lee-Yang zero-freeness result for interacting fermion systems, a gap that limits both phase-transition analysis and algorithmic applications. The authors prove a Lee-Yang theorem for the partition function of a broad class of interacting fermion models, including the attractive Hubbard model, the repulsive Hubbard model on bipartite graphs, and the interacting Hofstadter model. This zero-freeness implies a provably efficient quantum algorithm for estimating ground-state energies and rigorously rules out phase transitions in these models when a nonzero local external field is present. The work extends Lee-Yang techniques to fermionic many-body systems and connects them to quantum algorithm efficiency.|
|**2026-11-06**|**New exact solutions of the 3D Schr\"odinger equation**|E. E. Perepelkin, B. I. Sadovnikov, N. G. Inozemtseva, A. S. Medvedev|[[arxiv:2506.04725](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.04725)]|The paper addresses the scarcity of exact analytical solutions to the 3D Schrödinger equation by constructing a complete set of solutions for a two-parameter "quadratic funnel" potential within the Ψ-model, extending earlier work that found only a single ground-state wave function yielding a positive gauge-invariant Weyl-Stratonovich quasi-probability density. Using gauge invariance for scalar and vector potentials, the authors derive explicit energy spectra and eigenfunctions, obtain a solution to the electromagnetic Schrödinger equation with a "Dirac string" magnetic field from a singular vortex probability flux, and analyze superpositions producing various vortex and potential probability current fields. These results are examined within the Wigner-Vlasov formalism, and the exact solutions provide valuable benchmarks for testing numerical algorithms simulating complex quantum systems.|

|**2026-11-06**|**Galois conjugation, droplet growth, and the differential transcendence of the Ising model in nonzero field**|Ran Luo, Jiahua Tian|[[arxiv:2611.08033](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08033)]|The paper addresses whether the square-lattice Ising model in a nonzero magnetic field admits exact closed-form expressions, showing that its free energy and magnetization satisfy no algebraic differential equation in the coupling at any fixed nonzero field. The authors rule out the remaining exceptional fields—where the fugacity is an algebraic integer—by combining Galois conjugation with analysis of the large-order growth of reversed-spin droplet coefficients. They prove that at conjugate fugacities outside the unit circle, near-square droplets generate coefficient growth that cannot be cancelled in the full pressure series. This establishes differential transcendence of the model for all nonzero fields, extends the free-energy result to the Lieb lattice and decorated square lattices, and resolves analogous fields in every dimension \(d \ge 2\).|
|**2026-11-06**|**Masked diffusion as random sequential adsorption: an exactly solvable model for parallel decoding**|Adrian Baule|[[arxiv:2611.07845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07845)]|Masked diffusion models can decode many tokens in parallel, but jointly revealed tokens are sampled independently, creating an error that the paper characterizes physically. The authors prove that for any data this error equals the entropy production of generation relative to the reversed masking process, and for Markov data they map generation onto random sequential adsorption with labelled gaps, solving it exactly for any number of steps. Taking many steps yields a slowly driven system whose error is set by a closed-form thermodynamic metric on the mutual information between tokens, with optimal schedules as its geodesics; longer correlations concentrate error at the start of generation, and schedules beginning with small steps reduce both path error and perplexity while needing fewer steps to reach asymptotics.|
|**2026-11-06**|**Discrete quadrature spectrum of composite bosons: Weyl relation, intensity correlations, and binned homodyne detection**|Itzhak Roditi|[[arxiv:2611.07776](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07776)]|The paper asks whether a composite boson of two fermions can pass as elementary, and how that appearance depends on the measurement used. Working in an exactly solvable paired-mode model, it compares classical drives, periodically binned quadrature statistics, and intensity correlations, deriving an exact product formula for the Weyl defect plus a controlled dilute expansion in Schmidt moments, while binned observables turn out piecewise constant and sensitive to single eigenvalues crossing bin edges. Finite-mode examples with Schmidt number around three succeed at the percent level despite order-one Weyl defects, and near-unit g^(2) fails to control the binned algebra, whereas flat spectra converge strongly to the oscillator counterpart. The results are significant because they show that compositeness is probe-dependent and that standard nonclassicality indicators like g^(2) do not certify the effective Pauli algebra needed for treating composite bosons as logical qubits.|
|**2026-11-06**|**Universal Phase Locking from Slowly Varying Inhomogeneity in Tunnel-Coupled One-Dimensional Condensates**|Bence Fitos, G\'abor Tak\'acs|[[arxiv:2611.07657](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07657)]|The paper addresses the discrepancy between experimental observations of phase locking in tunnel-coupled one-dimensional Bose condensates and the predictions of the homogeneous integrable sine-Gordon model. Using the truncated Wigner approximation combined with asymptotic analysis, the authors show that a slowly varying density profile drives the relative phase near the density maximum toward zero through a universal mechanism. This drift appears already in the linearized theory, with a timescale set solely by the density curvature and independent of tunnel coupling, and rescaling time by this relaxation scale collapses the dynamics across different system sizes and density profiles. The results establish inhomogeneity as the origin of phase locking and provide a general framework for understanding relaxation in experimentally relevant condensate systems.|
|**2026-11-06**|**Single-shot ground-state preparation via strongly coupled baths**|Liang-Hong Mo, Jerome Lloyd, Yuxuan Zhang, Dmitry A. Abanin, Biao Lian|[[arxiv:2611.07021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07021)]|The paper addresses the slow cooling of most dissipative state-preparation protocols, which stems from their reliance on weak system-bath coupling. It constructs cooling protocols at strong coupling that combine dynamically ramped ancilla levels with time-dependent couplings, and for free-fermion systems reduces the dynamics exactly to a two-level scattering problem in which ground-state cooling becomes a control problem of maximizing resonant excitation transfer while suppressing non-adiabatic heating. The resulting "Möbius-Ingham" protocols achieve optimal error scaling for gapless spectra and almost-exponential suppression of leading heating error for gapped spectra, lie on the Pareto frontier of steady-state error versus cooling time in benchmarks, and remain effective for interacting Ising and t-V chains and the 2D Fermi-Hubbard model. The work is significant because it enables accurate ground- and Gibbs-state preparation in as little as a single cooling cycle, extending dissipative preparation to strong-coupling regimes.|
|**2026-11-06**|**On Entanglement Dynamics in Holographic Theories**|Hanzhi Jiang|[[arxiv:2611.07010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07010)]|This thesis addresses the difficulty of computing entanglement entropy (EE) in interacting, time-dependent quantum field theories by using the AdS/CFT correspondence to study real-time entanglement dynamics in strongly coupled systems. It first analyzes EE evolution in an exactly solvable two-dimensional CFT with an inhomogeneous sine-square deformation, interpreting the unusual evolution as a change of spacetime foliation that creates new horizons in the dual bulk, with holographic calculations matching CFT results. It then develops an effective entanglement membrane description for chaotic many-body systems, showing that reflected entropy in 2d CFTs requires an additional membrane degree of freedom, that a conventional non-degenerate tension emerges under a relevant deformation dual to a hairy BTZ black hole with an interior Kasner universe, and extends the membrane theory to higher dimensions. Applying these membrane theories to multipartite entanglement reveals that scrambling dynamics generate multipartite entanglement with rich structure, offering new insight into how entanglement propagates and organizes in strongly coupled quantum systems.|
|**2026-11-06**|**Ground state of the Hubbard model with spin-dependent linear potential**|Jacek Dobrzyniecki, Thomas Busch|[[arxiv:2604.24068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24068)]|The paper studies how a spin-dependent linear potential competes with attractive spin-spin interactions in the ground state of a one-dimensional Fermi-Hubbard model with open boundaries. Using DMRG simulations, the authors map density distributions and doublon numbers as functions of the potential gradient and interaction strength, identifying three regimes separated by critical thresholds: robust pairing at small gradients, a phase-separated regime with a staircase-like stepwise loss of doublons, and complete spin separation at large gradients. They support these findings with a phenomenological model and local-density approximation that yield closed-form estimates of the threshold gradients, and confirm the staircase persists under harmonic confinement. The results are directly relevant to cold-atom experiments and show that spin-dependent linear potentials allow integer-level control over the number of bound fermion pairs.|
|**2026-11-06**|**Singlet-only always-on gapless exchange (SAGE) spin qubits: Charge noise effects and two-qubit gates**|Nathan L. Foulk, Katharina Laubscher, Silas Hoffman, Sankar Das Sarma|[[arxiv:2604.15419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15419)]|SAGE qubits encode a single qubit in the spins of four electrons across four tunnel-coupled quantum dots, offering inherent protection against magnetic-gradient-induced Pauli errors through singlet-only encoding and always-on exchange couplings, though this always-on operation increases sensitivity to charge noise. Starting from a Hubbard model of the coupled dots, the authors characterize SAGE performance under 1/f charge noise affecting both dot chemical potentials and interdot tunnel couplings. They find that realistic CPMG-like pulse sequences significantly extend idle coherence times at experimentally relevant noise strengths, and that a simple refocusing strategy mititgates noise in two-qubit gates, with longer entangling-pulse ramp times suppressing leakage into noncomputational states. The work establishes practical noise-mitigation protocols for making SAGE qubits viable despite their enhanced charge-noise sensitivity.|
|**2026-11-06**|**Deep-layered machines have a built-in Occam's razor**|Thomas Fink|[[arxiv:2603.01217](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.01217)]|The paper addresses why input-output maps in complex systems tend to favor simple outputs, a phenomenon known as simplicity bias. Using an exactly solvable deep-layered machine, the authors prove that this bias strengthens with each additional layer, with output probability approaching Pr(F) ∝ 2^{-K(F)}, where K is Kolmogorov complexity. The key result is that hierarchical composition alone can generate an intrinsic Occam's razor, suggesting a general mechanism underlying simplicity bias in learning and evolution.|
|**2026-11-06**|**The generic Mott transition in the sine-Gordon model through an embedded worm algorithm**|Oscar Bouverot-Dupuis, Laura Foini, Alberto Rosso|[[arxiv:2510.20901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.20901)]|The sine-Gordon model with a tilt describes the generic Mott transition in one-dimensional quantum systems, but its distinct topological sectors restrict standard local Monte Carlo methods to very small system sizes. The authors introduce the smooth worm (SmoWo) Monte Carlo algorithm, which combines worm updates with event-chain Monte Carlo moves to enable transitions between topological sectors, and they prove its validity and quantify its performance. This acceleration allows simulations of large system sizes, yielding a precise characterization of the model's phases and critical behavior. The method provides a practical route to studying generic Mott transitions beyond the reach of conventional Monte Carlo schemes.|
