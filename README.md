## High Energy Theory

|**2026-09-03**|**Unfolded Krylov complexity: universal chaotic dynamics without false positives**|Johanna Erdmenger, Kyoung-Bum Huh, Hyun-Sik Jeong, Juan F. Pedraza|[[arxiv:2609.02228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02228)]|This paper addresses the problem of false positives in using Krylov (spread) complexity to diagnose quantum chaos, where integrable systems can mimic chaotic signatures. The proposed method is to "unfold" the energy spectrum before constructing Krylov dynamics, which removes the smooth, non-universal density of states while retaining microscopic spectral correlations. Key results show that this unfolding suppresses spurious peaks in integrable models, preserves universal chaotic signatures, and is analytically exact for a logarithmic model. This establishes unfolded Krylov complexity as a more reliable probe of genuine many-body scrambling, distinguishing it from kinematic spectral effects.|

|**2026-09-03**|**Fractional oscillators with fractional damping in the presence of external forces**|Fernando Olivar-Romero, Oscar Rosas-Ortiz|[[arxiv:2609.01883](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01883)]|This paper presents an exact solution for a fractional oscillator that incorporates a fractionally damped dynamic law, modeled by a three-parametric Caputo differential equation. The method applies Laplace transforms, which use the transform of the external force to determine the system's response profile. Key results show that with a constant force, the oscillator settles to a fixed point, while a sinusoidal force produces sustained oscillations. The significance is that the external force dictates the long-term behavior of the system, linking the fractional dynamics to the Newton-Scott-Blair viscoelastic model.|
|**2026-09-03**|**Lie symmetry analysis and similarity reductions for the tempered-fractional Keller Segel system**|Ghorbanali Haghighatdoost, Mustafa Bazghandi|[[arxiv:2509.11690](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11690)]|This paper develops a Lie symmetry analysis for the tempered-fractional Keller-Segel (TFKS) system, a chemotaxis model that includes anomalous diffusion. The authors introduce a new method to manage the nonlocal characteristics of tempered fractional operators, enabling the derivation of all Lie point symmetries and optimal subalgebras. Through this, they reduce the system's partial differential equations to ordinary differential equations, producing new exact solutions. The work provides insights into the model's long-term behavior and aggregation patterns, while offering a generalizable technique for other tempered fractional differential equations.|

## Condensed Matter

Patti, Natalia Ares|[[arxiv:2609.02736](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02736)]|QArray+ presents a physics-informed, GPU-accelerated simulator for quantum dot arrays, addressing the limitations of existing tools that rely on simplified models and fail to capture non-equilibrium dynamics. The method incorporates gate-dependent tunnel coupling and an open-system quantum description to simulate coherent charge-state hybridization and essential latching effects. Key results demonstrate significant speedups, computing a 100x100 gate voltage grid for 64 dots in ~0.17 seconds on multiple GPUs, with scalability to larger devices due to short-range interactions. This enables high-throughput synthetic dataset generation for training robust machine-learning models for automated tuning of large-scale quantum devices.|
|**2026-09-03**|**Prominent Dimensional Effects on High-order Harmonic Generation in Strongly Correlated Electron Systems**|Kento Uchida, Shintaro Takayoshi, Yuta Murakami, Dongjoon Song, Alannah M. Hallas, Masayuki Watanabe, Takashi Konishi, Aiko Nakano, Koichiro Tanaka|[[arxiv:2609.02466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02466)]|This paper investigates how the dimensionality of strongly correlated materials affects high-harmonic generation (HHG). Through experiments on quasi-1D SrCuO₂ and quasi-2D Pr₂CuO₄, combined with Hubbard model simulations, the authors show a stark contrast: the 1D material produces a robust, temperature-insensitive harmonic plateau, while the 2D material exhibits weaker harmonics with strong thermal suppression. The simulations attribute these differences to dimensionality-dependent doublon–holon dephasing driven by spin–charge coupling. The findings establish dimensionality and spin–charge coupling as crucial factors for controlling nonlinear optical responses in correlated systems.|
|**2026-09-03**|**Phase synchronization of fish schools through spatial gaps**|Elena G. de Lamo, \'Oscar S\'anchez, M. Carmen Miguel, Romualdo Pastor-Satorras|[[arxiv:2609.01754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01754)]|The paper investigates how visual signals coordinate the collective motion of two groups of fish separated by a transparent barrier, preventing direct physical interaction. The authors find that each school's collective heading switches unpredictably between two opposite directions, but turning events in one school influence the other, leading to synchronized behavior that depends on the distance between groups. A model using two coupled stochastic oscillators accurately reproduces the observed phase synchronization. This work provides a quantitative link between active matter systems and coupled oscillator theory, with implications for understanding information transfer across spatial boundaries in animal groups.|
|**2026-09-03**|**Athermality of generalized Gibbs ensembles**|Riccardo Senese, Bruno Bertini, Katja Klobas, Pasquale Calabrese|[[arxiv:2606.30762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.30762)]|This paper analyzes how generalized Gibbs ensembles (GGEs), which describe the steady states of integrable quantum systems, differ from standard thermal equilibrium states. The authors introduce "athermality," defined via quantum resource theory as the relative entropy to the closest thermal state, to quantify this difference. Using integrability techniques, they compute athermality for quenches in several models, such as the XY and XXZ spin chains and the Lieb-Liniger model, discovering that it becomes anomalously small near critical points of the post-quench Hamiltonian. The significance lies in proving that this behavior stems from a singularity in GGE entropy, providing a quantitative link between non-equilibrium dynamics and quantum criticality.|
|**2026-09-03**|**Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis and Random Matrix Theory Universality in Few-Body Systems**|Jiaozi Wang, Hua Yan, Robin Steinigeweg, Jochen Gemmer|[[arxiv:2506.09011](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.09011)]|This paper investigates quantum chaos in few-body systems using the Feingold-Peres model, a standard testbed. Through semiclassical analysis and numerical simulation, the authors examine whether the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH) holds in such systems and how its form scales with the effective Planck constant. They also test if observables restricted to small microcanonical energy windows exhibit universal random matrix theory statistics. The findings deepen the understanding of few-body quantum chaos and connect it with modern formulations of ETH.|
