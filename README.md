|**2026-10-21**|**Mass Deformations and Sheets in Logahoric Higgs Moduli**|Georgios Kydonakis, Lutian Zhao|[[arxiv:2610.27934](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27934)]|The paper studies how tame defects in six-dimensional N=(2,0) superconformal theories behave under mass deformations. Using the logahoric Hitchin integrable system and Lie algebra sheets, it shows that varying fixed-dimension leaves of the Hitchin system corresponds to varying fixed-dimension residue orbits. The authors verify the period and dimension conditions for mass-like deformations proposed by Balasubramanian–Distler, thereby realizing Lie algebra sheets on the Poisson moduli space of logahoric Higgs torsors. This connects the sheet structure of Lie algebras to the geometry of Higgs moduli and supports the proposed mass-deformation framework.|

|**2026-10-21**|**A twisted Marchenko lemma and applications**|Jun-Yang Zhang, Shi-Hao Li, Shou-Feng Shen|[[arxiv:2610.28233](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28233)]|The paper addresses the lack of a Marchenko-type lemma for non-commutative two-component integrable systems. It introduces a twisted differential equation and an associated two-component Wronski matrix, showing that under Marchenko lemma conditions its logarithmic derivative satisfies nonlinear equations whose projections yield a non-commutative two-component KP equation, with single-component reductions recovering the non-commutative Burgers, KP, and DS equations. Using a direct approach based on two-component quasi-Wronskians, the authors construct a higher-order non-commutative two-component KP equation with matrix-valued solutions, and show that reductions of seed functions produce the one-component hierarchy as well as the non-commutative DS and NLS equations. This extends the Marchenko method to the non-commutative multicomponent setting and unifies several known integrable equations within a single framework.|

## Condensed Matter

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-10-21**|**Acceleration of CTQMC via Denoising Diffusion Models with Auxiliary Spectral Functions**|Rishi Rao, Li Zhu|[[arxiv:2610.28542](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28542)]|CTQMC impurity solvers for DMFT are numerically exact but expensive, and statistical noise from undersampling destabilizes self-consistency, especially at low temperatures. The authors train a diffusion-model denoiser on synthetic data that predicts positive, normalized auxiliary spectral functions consistent with noisy imaginary-time Green's functions, then refine these via autodifferentiation to match the self-energy, its first two high-frequency moments, and impurity occupation, yielding causal Green's functions and self-energies by design. The framework reduces required CTQMC measurements by a factor of 10–100, reproducing the Mott transition in a half-filled Bethe-lattice Hubbard model with 5000 measurements, the pseudogap transition in a two-patch DCA of the 2D Hubbard model with 5×10^5 measurements, and the SrVO3 spectral function with 50× fewer measurements than reference. Because auxiliary spectral functions are generated, approximate real-frequency spectra are obtained without analytic continuation.|
|**2026-10-21**|**Floquet ground state order**|Suman Mondal, Paul M. Schindler, Marin Bukov|[[arxiv:2610.28336](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28336)]|Clean, interacting, periodically driven systems are generally expected to heat to featureless infinite-temperature states, precluding stable order. The authors introduce Geometric Floquet theory, decomposing evolution into geometric (holonomy) and average-energy parts, and derive a variational principle plus matrix product state algorithm to compute the Floquet ground state of the average-energy operator in large interacting systems. Simulations of driven nonintegrable spin chains with over 100 spins reveal gapped, area-law entangled Floquet ground states hosting both dressed equilibrium order (Floquet antiferromagnets) and nonequilibrium order (ground-state time crystals), protected by a finite average-energy gap that closes at transitions. They further identify quasilocal counterdiabatic driving as a heating-stability mechanism and argue these states evade the Eigenstate Thermalization Hypothesis, enabling engineering of strongly correlated nonequilibrium quantum matter.|
|**2026-10-21**|**Microscopically informed self-consistent multi-resolvent closure for dense many-body spectra**|Zhiqiang Huang|[[arxiv:2610.28031](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28031)]|The paper addresses the challenge of predictively closing the exact multi-resolvent hierarchy of dense many-body spectra for interacting Hamiltonians. It introduces multi-resolvent self-consistency (MRSC) equations that separate an exact algebraic hierarchy from a finite-parameter approximate closure, using a connectivity basis, a causal Stieltjes representation, and a moment-field identity to reduce moment matching to a finite-dimensional fixed-point problem. On a free-fermion benchmark the method reproduces exact identities and recovers the Faddeeva closure at r=1, and in a blind test on the interacting Ising chain it predicts held-out two-resolvent probes to within 0.35%–0.97%. The framework also provides a sign-correct energy-resolved correction to the single-resolvent ETH variance baseline and a hierarchical error analysis, offering a finite-order, diagnostically transparent approach to spectral-weight correlations rather than a proof of universal emergence.|
|**2026-10-21**|**Spin fluctuations and nearly isotropic quasiparticles in the doped three-dimensional Hubbard model**|Pengcheng Hou, Kun Chen, Youjin Deng|[[arxiv:2610.27779](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27779)]|The paper addresses the lack of controlled momentum-resolved results for the doped three-dimensional Hubbard model, a paramagnetic metal with antiferromagnetic spin fluctuations above its Néel temperature. Using connected-determinant diagrammatic Monte Carlo, the authors compute the momentum-resolved self-energy and full-zone spin susceptibility over a range of densities, temperatures, and interactions, focusing on U/t = 4. They find that spin fluctuations become strong toward half filling, enhancing the susceptibility peak up to sevenfold and raising the momentum-dependent scattering rate to four to five times its second-order value, yet the fluctuations remain short ranged and quasiparticles near the Fermi surface stay nearly isotropic without hot spots. These results benchmark extensions of DMFT and spin-fluctuation theories, showing that strong spin fluctuations can coexist with nearly isotropic quasiparticles.|
|**2026-10-21**|**QMBL: the Quantum Many-Body Leaderboard. A live record of published ground-state energies for lattice Hamiltonians and the electron gas**|Tristan Zerweck, Juan Carrasquilla|[[arxiv:2610.22387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22387)]|The paper addresses the problem that published ground-state energies for quantum many-body systems are scattered across the literature with fragmented conventions, making systematic comparison difficult. The authors introduce QMBL (the Quantum Many-Body Leaderboard), a maintained, interactive, open-source database that aggregates published ground-state energies for six lattice Hamiltonians and the homogeneous electron gas, using AI agents to read papers, convert conventions, and verify numbers against their sources. Version 0.5.0 contains 2511 energies across 532 instances, of which 285 have no exact solution and thus serve as targets for a companion open-problems benchmark. The leaderboard provides a live alternative to the static VarBench dataset and records provenance, error bars, and compute-cost estimates, supporting FAIR data principles.|
|**2026-10-21**|**Engineering tunable $p$ -wave magnetism in antiferromagnetic bilayers**|Yu-Han Lin, Jin-Wei Dong, Ziqiang Wang, Sen Zhou|[[arxiv:2609.00277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00277)]|The paper addresses the challenge of generating odd-parity spin splitting (p-wave magnetism) in fully compensated antiferromagnetic systems without spin-orbit coupling. It proposes a symmetry-guided design using a bilayer of two AB-stacked antiferromagnetic square lattices with nonparallel collinear moments, where an in-plane relative shift between layers selectively breaks spin-degeneracy-protecting symmetries while preserving time-reversal constraints. The authors show that the direction and magnitude of the p-wave spin splitting and associated spin responses can be continuously tuned by this in-plane displacement, and by mapping the system onto an effective bond-modulated square lattice, they find a mean-field phase diagram with extended regions of spontaneous p-wave magnetism exhibiting coplanar or chiral spin textures. This establishes a minimal equilibrium platform for realizing and manipulating tunable p-wave magnetism in two-dimensional systems.|
|**2026-10-21**|**N\'eel Ordered Magnetic Phases in Bipartite Quasicrystals**|Jia-Heng Ji, Zhi-Yan Shao, Yu-Bo Liu, Wei Li, Fan Yang|[[arxiv:2511.22204](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.22204)]|The paper investigates whether altermagnetism (AM) can exist in two-dimensional bipartite quasicrystals, a claim that previously lacked strong evidence. Using sign-problem-free projector quantum Monte Carlo simulations of half-filled Hubbard models, the authors consistently find Néel ordered states and show that these can be antiferromagnetic, altermagnetic, or ferrimagnetic depending on the symmetry relating the two sublattices. They derive a general criterion linking the sublattice symmetry operation to the magnetism class, and identify symmetry-protected AM in D4-symmetric Thue-Morse quasicrystals and geometry-enforced AM in the D5-symmetric Penrose quasicrystal. These results establish quasicrystals as a viable platform for unconventional magnetism and provide guidance for future experiments.|

|**2026-10-21**|**The gauge action on semi-discrete Lax representations, its invariants, and Lax-Miura type constructions**|Sergei Igonin|[[arxiv:2604.20830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.20830)]|The paper addresses the problem of determining when semi-discrete matrix Lax representations for evolutionary differential-difference equations are equivalent under local gauge transformations, and when parameters in such representations can be removed by gauge. The authors construct explicit gauge invariants for these semi-discrete Lax representations and derive sufficient conditions for parameter nonremovability as well as necessary conditions for gauge equivalence. They apply the theory to Lax representations of Volterra–Yamilov, Cherdantsev–Yamilov, and Toda type equations and compare their invariants with known results for continuous zero-curvature representations. Additionally, they develop procedures for constructing Miura-type transformations for discrete equations on the two-dimensional integer lattice via gauge simplifications of discrete Lax pairs, yielding new transformations for certain non-evolutionary 4-point and 6-point equations.|
