|**2026-09-23**|**Exactly solvable Russian-doll model: Renormalization-group cycles meet fractality**|Ilya Liubimov, Alexander Gorsky|[[arxiv:2603.17674](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17674)]|The paper studies the Bethe-ansatz-integrable Russian Doll model of superconductivity with broken time-reversal symmetry, whose renormalization group (RG) flow is cyclic rather than monotonic. By exactly solving the RG flows, the authors analyze the one-pair sector and identify localized, fractal, and delocalized phases, showing that the Bethe-ansatz quantum number Q counts RG cycles and parametrizes towers of states. They demonstrate that Q acts as an order parameter, revealing a new mechanism for fractal-phase formation in deterministic systems and illustrating the interplay between fractality and cyclic RG.|

|**2026-09-23**|**Neural Network Backflow with Low-Rank Multi-Determinant Updates**|Tianshu Huang, Xiaowei Ou, Vidvuds Ozolins|[[arxiv:2609.26544](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26544)]|The paper addresses the challenge of simulating strongly correlated fermions, where exponential Hilbert space complexity and complex sign structures hinder accurate modeling. It introduces a variational framework combining a neural network backflow transformation with variational Monte Carlo, using a multi-determinant expansion with low-rank shifts to capture non-local correlations. Applied to the 2D Hubbard model, the method reaches energies within 0.45% of auxiliary-field quantum Monte Carlo at half-filling and reproduces intertwined charge- and spin-density stripe patterns at 1/8 doping. This demonstrates a scalable, interpretable approach for variational simulation of strongly correlated fermionic systems.|
|**2026-09-23**|**Counterions between walls with surface charge modulations: Exact Poisson-Boltzmann solutions**|Ladislav \v{S}amaj|[[arxiv:2609.26356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.26356)]|The paper examines how position-dependent (modulated) surface charge on two parallel walls affects the pressure between them in the Poisson-Boltzmann limit. Using a general solution of the two-dimensional Liouville equation, the authors construct exact electrostatic potentials and inversely derive the corresponding surface charge densities, restricting to one-directional modulations. Their analytical results show that charge modulation can either increase the pressure at small wall separations or decrease it at large separations, contradicting earlier Monte Carlo predictions that modulation always reduces pressure. This work provides exact benchmarks that clarify when and how surface charge patterning alters inter-wall forces.|
|**2026-09-23**|**Fractional Quantum Geometry in Topological Mott Regime**|Junyu Tang, Hongquan Lv, Gang v. Chen|[[arxiv:2609.25922](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25922)]|The paper addresses how to probe quantum geometry in a topological Mott insulator, specifically a chiral spin liquid whose charge-neutral spinons carry nontrivial band topology and quantum geometry. It proposes that virtual polarization of the gapped charge sector mediates an emergent U(1) gauge field, yielding a tensor Ioffe-Larkin response and enabling two optical measurement protocols. In the low-frequency off-shell regime, a self-calibrated ratio of longitudinal to Hall conductivity yields the spinon Chern number without microscopic knowledge of the charge response, while in the resonant on-shell regime, combining the inverse Ioffe-Larkin response with the Kramers-Kronig relation reconstructs the quantum-metric and Berry-curvature spectral densities. The framework applies to the triangular lattice Hofstadter-Hubbard model in Moiré systems and materials like Nb $_3$Br$_8$ , establishing a unified means to quantitatively probe quantum geometry of fractionalized quasiparticles.|
|**2026-09-23**|**Efficiency of Continuous-Time Quantum Monte Carlo Updates in the Ferromagnetic State of the Doped SU(3) Fermi-Hubbard Model**|Juntaro Fujii, Kazuki Yamamoto, Akihisa Koga|[[arxiv:2609.25494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25494)]|The paper addresses poor Monte Carlo sampling efficiency when simulating the doped SU(3) Fermi-Hubbard model via segment-based continuous-time hybridization-expansion quantum Monte Carlo within dynamical mean-field theory, focusing on the low-temperature, strong-coupling ferromagnetic regime. The authors compare update schemes, specifically testing the addition of double-flip and flavor-permutation updates to a basic local update. They find that the double-flip update minimizes the integrated autocorrelation time of the majority-flavor occupation in the ferromagnetic state, while the flavor-permutation update is most effective in the paramagnetic regime. The significance lies in identifying efficient update strategies for different magnetic phases and explaining the difference through analysis of the characteristic occupation changes induced by accepted updates.|
|**2026-09-23**|**Coherent lattice dynamics distinguish Holstein and Su-Schrieffer-Heeger electron-phonon coupling**|Debshikha Banerjee, Jinu Thomas, G. Alvarez, T. P. Devereaux, T. A. Maier, S. Johnston|[[arxiv:2609.25418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25418)]|This paper investigates how different microscopic electron-phonon (e-ph) coupling mechanisms—Holstein (local electrostatic) versus Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH, hopping-modulating)—affect the nonequilibrium response of a pumped one-dimensional Mott insulator. The authors perform time-dependent density matrix renormalization group simulations on both the Hubbard-Holstein and Hubbard-SSH models. They find that while the electronic responses are qualitatively similar and governed by the same Hubbard-model excitations, the lattice dynamics differ substantially: the Holstein model shows long-lived coherent phonon oscillations when the pump resonates with the bare phonon frequency, whereas the SSH model shows none. This contrast is robust across phonon frequencies and pump parameters, establishing coherent lattice dynamics as a direct nonequilibrium diagnostic of the microscopic form of e-ph coupling.|

|**2026-09-23**|**Dynamical Correlation of the Post-quench Non-thermal Equilibrium State**|Yang-Yang Chen, Song Cheng|[[arxiv:2601.06987](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06987)]|The paper addresses how correlations behave in the non-thermal equilibrium state (NTES) that an integrable system reaches after a quantum quench, a regime usually described by the generalized Gibbs ensemble. By combining quench action methods with the form factor approach, the authors compute the field-field correlation function for the NTES produced by an interaction quench in the Lieb-Liniger model. They find that the spectral distribution differs qualitatively from that of a thermal equilibrium state, exhibiting a new dispersion branch linked to the algebraically decaying tail of the root density distribution. This suggests a broader class of NTES sharing similar spectral features, refining understanding of how quenched integrable systems relax.|
