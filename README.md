The authors show analytically that a normalizable localized zero mode opens an additional interaction channel whose decay is parametrically slower than that from exchanging massive fluctuations, even though the continuum remains gapped, with the massive fluctuations providing the local dressing of each object's coupling to the zero mode. The sine-Gordon kink supplies an exactly solvable realization in which the Casimir interaction exhibits the predicted zero-mode-dominated large-distance behavior, localized around the kink. This identifies localized zero modes as a mechanism for enhancing long-range Casimir forces in gapped systems.|

Testing against combined HERA data yields good fits (χ²/dof = 71.811/77 across 19 virtuality slices, and 123.608/106 for the compact HERA-I F₂ surface), and the fixed-coupling three-particle normalization is evaluated as g₃(∞) = 0.589320245 with verified numerical convergence. |**2026-11-05**|**A local Yang-Baxter obstruction for the hard-square model**|Gabriel Ortega, Eliecer Pe\~na-Ancavil|[[arxiv:2611.06780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06780)]|The hard-square model lacks a known closed-form solution despite the neighboring hard-hexagon model being solved via the Yang-Baxter equation. The authors classify height alphabets that encode hard squares without hidden heights, finding exactly two connected encodings: the occupation variables and the four ABF heights. They show that no analytic Yang-Baxter family of face weights passes through hard squares at nonzero activity on either encoding, because the exclusion rule converts certain Yang-Baxter equations into polynomial identities forcing auxiliary weights to vanish. This rules out the standard integrability route for hard squares while leaving open the possibility of solutions via hidden heights, alternative encodings, or families beyond a single analytic Yang-Baxter family.|
|**2026-11-05**|**Rough-to-frozen transition in non-integrable dimer models**|Alessandro Giuliani, Vieri Mastropietro, Bruno Renzi, Fabio Toninelli|[[arxiv:2611.05516](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05516)]|The paper addresses the Pokrovsky-Talapov law governing the rough-to-frozen transition in random surface models, which had previously been proven only for exactly solvable cases like the classical 2D dimer model. The authors prove this law for a non-integrable dimer model on the hexagonal lattice with a weak plaquette interaction, using multiscale analysis via constructive fermionic renormalization group. They identify two regimes with distinct scaling properties—one with effective quadratic dispersion at smaller distances and one with effective linear (Luttinger liquid) dispersion—explaining the universality of the 3/2 exponent despite interaction-dependent critical exponents in the rough phase. As a byproduct, they control the scaling limit uniformly in the distance from the frozen phase.|

Using variational Monte Carlo, the authors study a single-band Hubbard model with on-site interaction U and ASOC strength λ, comparing Gutzwiller- and particle-number-projected BCS trial wave functions for several pairing symmetries. At U/t = 10, the f-wave state is energetically favored without ASOC (λ/t = 0), but a chiral p-wave state becomes lowest in energy once λ/t = 0.1. These results show that ASOC can strongly reshape the competition among pairing states in the presence of strong correlations.|

Using finite-temperature determinant quantum Monte Carlo simulations of a minimal single-orbital bilayer Hubbard model at strong interlayer coupling (t⊥/t ∼ 1), the authors show that spin stripes develop spontaneously with hole doping and are accompanied by a crossover of the dominant pairing symmetry from d-wave to s±-wave. This crossover tracks the changing relative strengths of intralayer versus interlayer antiferromagnetic correlations, with interlayer correlations overtaking intralayer ones, and the dynamical spin structure factor exhibits a momentum-dependent reorganization of magnetic excitations consistent with fluctuating stripes. The results identify the structure of spin fluctuations as a key link between spin stripes and pairing competition, offering a minimal-model perspective for understanding La₃Ni₂O₇.|
|**2026-11-05**|**Resonantly interacting $2e/3$ anyons: a controlled microscopic theory of anyon superconductivity**|Qingchen Li, Pavel A. For a three-valley gas, they identify a resonance governed by an exactly solvable Aharonov-Casher anyon Hamiltonian, and using its ground-state wavefunction they demonstrate Cooper-pair off-diagonal long-range order directly in electron coordinates, determining the pairing symmetry and vortex-lattice structure, with a relevant pair detuning and marginal three-body contact controlling compressibility and sound velocity. The work shows that binding thresholds between competing anyon composites host universal resonant regimes invisible to the lowest-energy anyon species alone, opening a new setting for controlled theories of anyon matter.|
|**2026-11-05**|**Zero noise extrapolation of transport coefficients**|Hansveer Singh, Pieter Claeys, Sarang Gopalakrishnan, Anushya Chandran|[[arxiv:2611.05481](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05481)]|The paper addresses the failure of standard zero-noise extrapolation (ZNE) when estimating transport coefficients of conserved quantities, since noise that violates conservation laws makes naive extrapolation unreliable. The authors analyze noisy one-dimensional non-integrable spin chains, defining effective transport coefficients from the leading momentum dependence of Fourier-mode decay rates and showing that universal nonlinear hydrodynamic corrections can make these coefficients non-analytic in the noise strength γ. Specifically, they predict that the effective spin diffusion coefficient of a dissipative Heisenberg chain and the effective subdiffusion coefficient of a dipole-conserving fracton scale toward their noiseless values as γ^(1/2), and they confirm these predictions numerically. This work reveals a fundamental limitation of naive ZNE and provides corrected scaling laws for extracting transport coefficients on noisy quantum devices.|
|**2026-11-05**|**Run-and-tumble particles in a harmonic external field: constructing the solution of the Fokker-Planck equation from the equations of motion**|Aoran Sun, Fangfu Ye, Jeff Z. Y. The authors develop a method to integrate the Langevin equations, constructing moments and correlation functions conditioned on the particle's final state, both with and without a harmonic external force field. For the one-dimensional harmonic case, they derive exact steady-state distributions for the tumble state and for the run state conditioned on velocity direction. These results establish equivalence with the exact solutions of the corresponding Fokker-Planck equations, providing a rigorous link between the microscopic equations of motion and macroscopic distribution descriptions.|
|**2026-11-05**|**$d$-wave superconductivity in quintuple-layer bulk superconductor La$_6$Ni$_5$O$_{12}$**|Zhan Wang, Yuxin Wang, Hui-Ke Jin, Kun Jiang, Jiangping Hu|[[arxiv:2610.22212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22212)]|The paper addresses the unresolved pairing symmetry of superconductivity in the quintuple-layer nickelate La₆Ni₅O₁₂.
