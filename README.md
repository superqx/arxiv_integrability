Clerk|[[arxiv:2609.07830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07830)]|This paper introduces a new theoretical framework by applying fermionic gauging to the Bose-Hubbard model, which results in an exact dual description using fermionic composites. This duality extends prior mappings between hard-core bosons and fermions to encompass soft-core interactions, effectively linking the sine-Gordon and Thirring models at low energies. The authors verify this exact duality through DMRG calculations, confirming the system's properties and demonstrating that the fermionic correlations provide a direct manifestation of Luttinger's theorem. This work is significant because its construction generalizes to other bosonic systems and higher dimensions, offering a new tool for investigating strongly correlated quantum systems.|
|**2026-09-09**|**Constructing the Monopole Formula for $A$ -Type Good Quivers via Quiver Yangians**|Tiantai Chen|[[arxiv:2609.06711](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06711)]|This paper explores the conjectured correspondence between quiver Yangians and Coulomb branch algebras in the context of 3D \(\mathcal{N}=4\) supersymmetric gauge theories. The authors focus on \(A\)-type good quiver gauge theories, specifically \(T_\rho(SU(N))\) theories, and aim to derive the monopole formula from the quiver Yangian perspective. By reorganizing the algebra's generators into boundary-adapted ones, they recover classical relations equivalent to \(U(N)\) Casimirs on the Slodowy slice. They demonstrate that these generators and relations correctly reproduce the fugacity structure of the Hall-Littlewood monopole formula, thereby reconstructing it as the Hilbert series of the truncated shifted quiver Yangian and providing strong evidence for the proposed correspondence.|
|**2026-09-09**|**Chaos in Systems with Quantum Group Symmetry**|Victor Gorbenko, Aleksandr Zhabin|[[arxiv:2510.23247](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.23247)]|This paper investigates whether quantum group symmetries can exist in chaotic, non-integrable systems. The authors construct an explicit example: a non-unitary but PT-symmetric spin chain with next-to-nearest neighbor interactions that possesses quantum group symmetry. Through eigenvalue statistics, they demonstrate that this system exhibits chaotic behavior, challenging the traditional link between quantum groups and integrability. The work also reveals an additional transition where eigenvalues become complex, expanding the understanding of such symmetric, non-Hermitian systems.|

The method involves formulating the equation on graphs with discrete edges and deriving exact eigenstate and spectral solutions, exemplified through star graphs with three edges. Key results show that these discrete solutions align well with continuum-case results, as demonstrated in tabulated comparisons. The significance lies in offering a systematic framework for analyzing relativistic effects in branched quantum devices, where continuum models may be insufficient.|
|**2026-09-09**|**Big Crunch on Julia and Anti-Julia Sets in the Integrable Limit**|Masatomi Iizawa, Satoru Saito, Yasuhito Narita, Yasuyuki Maeda, Hiromitsu Harada, Akira Shudo|[[arxiv:2609.07308](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07308)]|This paper investigates why the fractal Julia set structure, characteristic of chaotic systems, disappears in integrable (non-chaotic) limits. The authors introduce a new concept called the "anti-Julia set," defined as the closure of divergences in periodic equations, along with their pre-image sets, and study a complex map with a deformation parameter to bridge integrable and non-integrable dynamics. They analytically show that as the parameter approaches the integrable limit, the pre-anti-Julia set's boundary contains the Julia set, leading to a "Big Crunch" where these structures collapse. The significance lies in analytically characterizing this transition with numerical visualizations, providing a mechanistic explanation for the absence of Julia sets in integrable systems.|

The method links such perturbations to massive Euclidean free fields, enabling a Feynman-Kac representation and a probabilistic expression for the relative entropy, which is then analyzed via a Polchinski equation in an abstract Wiener space framework. Building on techniques from Bauerschmidt, Bodineau, and Dagallier, the authors derive a probabilistic entropy bound that translates into operator-algebraic terms. As a key result, they prove that the Lorentzian massive Sine-Gordon model in the ultraviolet finite regime satisfies this bound, offering a rigorous relative entropy estimate for interacting QFTs.|
|**2026-09-09**|**Tropicalization of Fock's inverse spectral transform**|Terrence George|[[arxiv:2609.06241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.06241)]|This paper addresses the inverse spectral transform for cluster integrable systems, specifically examining its behavior in the tropical limit where spectral curves degenerate. The authors analyze Fock's explicit inverse formula and determine the leading-order asymptotics of its classical components, showing they correspond to tropical analogues. They establish that Fock's transform commutes with tropicalization and derive a tropical version of Fay's trisecant identity. The results provide a rigorous tropical inverse spectral transform on degenerate spectral data, deepening the link between algebro-geometric integrable systems and tropical geometry.|
|**2026-09-09**|**St\"ackel and Eisenhart lifts, Haantjes geometry and Gravitation**|Ond\v{r}ej Kub\r{u}, Piergiulio Tempesta|[[arxiv:2509.19950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19950)]|This paper introduces the "Stäckel lift" as a unified geometric framework for constructing new integrable Hamiltonian systems. A key result is that these systems naturally possess a symplectic-Haantjes structure, which is demonstrated through applications to magnetic systems and the generation of Platonic-wave geometries. This work is significant for its potential to model modified gravity and momentum-dependent metrics in theoretical physics.|
|**2026-09-09**|**Six-Vertex, Loop and Tiling models: Integrability and Combinatorics**|Paul Zinn-Justin|[[arxiv:0901.0665](https://arxiv.org/abs/0901.0665)]|This paper reviews the author's work on exactly solvable two-dimensional statistical models, specifically the six-vertex model, loop models, and lozenge tilings. Its significance lies in unifying these diverse areas through the lens of integrability and showcasing how physical models can solve complex mathematical problems.|

Horvath, Bruno Bertini|[[arxiv:2609.09139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09139)]|This paper extends the Ballistic Macroscopic Fluctuation Theory (BMFT), previously limited to near-equilibrium systems, to model large-scale dynamics after integrable quenches. The authors formulate the full-counting-statistics problem as a path integral and solve it via a saddle-point approximation, yielding nonlinear partial differential equations for charge fluctuations. They validate their approach by solving these equations for both free fermions and the interacting Rule 54 cellular automaton, matching known exact results in both cases. They find that the system's moments form rotating multiplets, with only the Hamiltonian emerging as a conserved quantity in the quadratic sector. The work offers a precise microscopic realization of the model's isochronous dynamics and correctly reduces to the free-streaming case when the trap is removed.|
