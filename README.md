|**2026-10-02**|**On-shell renormalization of sine-Gordon by the quantum inverse scattering method**|Francesco Beccarini, Claudio Conti|[[arxiv:2610.01571](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01571)]|The paper addresses the challenge of defining a consistent renormalization scheme for the sine-Gordon model regularized on a lattice via the quantum inverse scattering method, where continuum and infinite-volume limits only commute after rescaling the box length. It shows that this rescaling, combined with an on-shell condition fixing the lightest breather mass to that of the linearized boson, yields a renormalization scheme in which all cutoff dependence is absorbed into a single factor \(Z_L\) multiplying the box length, with no renormalization of the field or coupling \(\beta\). Soliton and breather masses then follow directly from monodromy operator eigenvalues expressed in terms of non-running parameters, reproducing classical and semiclassical results as \(\beta \to 0\) and yielding mass ratios without ever computing the lattice-to-physical scale relation. The scheme clarifies the interplay between integrability and renormalization, is compared with Coleman's normal ordering and conformal perturbation theory, and is argued to extend to other ultralocal integrable theories with massive spectra.|
|**2026-10-02**|**Microcanonical Operator Spectrum, BPS Chaos and Free Probability Theory**|Alexandre Belin, Yichao Fu, Federico La Rocca|[[arxiv:2609.22422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22422)]|The paper addresses how the matrix elements of simple operators behave in chaotic quantum many-body systems, focusing on the eigenspectrum of operators projected onto narrow microcanonical energy windows—a characterization especially relevant when the Hamiltonian is degenerate. Assuming the operator eigenbasis is Haar-randomly oriented relative to the Hamiltonian's, the authors prove via the induced probability distribution and Free Probability Theory that the projected operator spectrum follows Gaussian random matrix statistics. These results clarify spectral fluctuations in microcanonical windows and carry implications for BPS chaos and eigenstate thermalization.|
|**2026-10-02**|**Exact fermionic dual of the Bose-Hubbard model**|Lei Su, Ivar Martin, Aashish A. Clerk|[[arxiv:2609.07830](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.07830)]|The paper derives an exact fermionic dual description of the Bose-Hubbard model with global U(1) symmetry by applying fermionic gauging, which realizes generalized Jordan-Wigner transformations. This dual is expressed in terms of composite fermions built from bosons and fermions, and in 1D it generalizes the mapping between the extended hard-core Bose-Hubbard model and the spinless Fermi-Hubbard model to soft-core bosons, reducing at low energies to the known sine-Gordon–Thirring equivalence. Using DMRG calculations, the authors confirm the duality and show that the gapless phase and transition are governed by compact boson conformal field theory, with the composite correlation oscillation wave vector fixed by density—a novel manifestation of Luttinger's theorem. The construction extends to generic bosonic systems and higher dimensions, offering new routes for studying Bose-Fermi mixtures in optical lattices.|
|**2026-10-02**|**Factorization in deep inelastic scattering at Bj\"{o}rken limit: Reduction to (1+1)D integrable models**|H. Babujian, M. Karowski, A. The authors fit the data with a local power law whose running exponent γ(Q²) is described by a renormalization-group crossover form, extracting an asymptotic value C ≈ 0.431. This value identifies the sine-Gordon power-law universality class, corresponding to coupling β²/π ≈ 6.23, and yields a quantitatively accurate fit with reduced χ² near unity. The results suggest that small-x growth in deep inelastic scattering is organized by sine-Gordon-type universality, with finite-Q² dependence governed by a smooth crossover to the asymptotic exponent.|

|**2026-10-02**|**Quadratic symmetry algebras and Sturm algebraization of superintegrable systems with magnetic fields**|Shams Ara, Md Fazlul Hoque, Ian Marquette|[[arxiv:2610.01869](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01869)]|The paper addresses two three-dimensional quantum superintegrable systems in axially symmetric magnetic fields, where the goal is to understand how hidden algebraic structures govern their solvability. The authors derive quadratic symmetry algebras with Casimir operators and deformed-oscillator representations, and independently recover the spectra through separation of variables in circular parabolic coordinates, where the equations become sextic biconfluent-Heun equations with finite polynomial sectors. They show that each separated equation admits an sl(2) algebraization reducing to a finite tridiagonal matrix, and they construct a pre-separation Sturm representation in which the transformed integrals belong to the universal enveloping algebra U(sl(3)). The significance lies in obtaining three complementary algebraic descriptions at different stages of the same multiseparable system, and in explaining that while fixing the axial integral reduces both distinct magnetic Hamiltonians to a common singular 2:1 oscillator normal form, this does not imply equivalence of the original systems.|
|**2026-10-02**|**Dissipation-Enabled Operator-Norm Locality Bounds for Bose-Hubbard Hamiltonians**|Marius Lemm, Tim M\"{o}bus|[[arxiv:2610.01669](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01669)]|Closed bosonic lattice systems generally lack operator-norm Lieb-Robinson bounds uniform in the initial state because information propagation speed can grow with local boson occupancy. The authors show that on-site $\ell$-photon loss ($\ell > 2$ ) in a dissipative Bose-Hubbard Lindbladian restores such a bound, proving an almost-ballistic Lieb-Robinson bound by using dissipation to deplete highly occupied sites and regularize the state via Sobolev-type local particle moment bounds. They extend the approach to cat-code dissipation, obtaining a volume-uniform local adiabatic approximation for code-space states, and derive consequences including local channel approximation, a thermodynamic limit, and efficient digital quantum simulation. The significance is that these locality-based tools become available uniformly in the input state, as in quantum spin systems but in contrast to closed Bose-Hubbard models.|
|**2026-10-02**|**On the instability of some upward propagating, exact, nonlinear mountain waves**|Christian Puntini|[[arxiv:2604.03620](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03620)]|The paper studies the linear stability of an exact solution for upward-propagating nonlinear mountain waves in dry, adiabatic compressible flow. Using the short-wavelength instability method, the authors reduce the problem to a system of ODEs with periodic coefficients along fluid trajectories and analyze it via Floquet theory. They find that stability depends on a single discriminant, with instability occurring when wave steepness exceeds 1/3 for neutral stratification, and this threshold is lowered by weakly stable stratification. The instability is a subharmonic parametric resonance, and the results identify an unstable region in the upper laminar layer beneath the tropopause where the wave is steepest.|

|**2026-10-02**|**On thermalization in random free fermions: Statistical origins and energy-dependent ETH structure**|Xiao-Kan Guo, Zhiqiang Huang|[[arxiv:2610.01414](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.01414)]|The paper investigates how random free fermion systems reach thermal equilibrium, using random-matrix methods to analyze the observable \(\operatorname{Tr}(A\rho(t))\) for single-particle operators. The authors derive a thermalization function with a characteristic timescale \(\tau_\lambda = \hbar/(2\eta\sqrt{N})\), prove its \(/t/^{-3}\) decay, and show fluctuations vanish as \(O(1/N)\) in the thermodynamic limit, while also establishing a factorization theorem for energy-shell eigenstate statistics. Under a controlled eigenvector-eigenvalue correlation deformation, both the diagonal energy-resolved slope and the off-diagonal ETH spectral function become energy-dependent, distinguishing this model as an analytically solvable realization of weak ETH-type self-averaging where eigenbasis chaos can be separated from spectral chaos.|
|**2026-10-02**|**Krylov complexity of a tilted extended Bose-Hubbard chain with Rydberg-dressed interactions**|Yifan Chen, Tianyi Yan, Lu Qin, Weibin Li|[[arxiv:2610.00712](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00712)]|The paper studies Krylov state complexity in a tilted extended Bose-Hubbard chain with both on-site (U) and nearest-neighbor (V) interactions, a setup realizable via Rydberg-dressed interactions in optical lattices. Using exact diagonalization and Lanczos recursion from three Fock states, the authors compute time-dependent complexity, its long-time saturation, and Lanczos coefficient fluctuations for an open chain at unit filling. They find a pronounced quasi-chaotic regime near U ≃ V where complexity saturation shows a sharp ridge even though spectral level-spacing statistics remain only partially chaotic, indicating that state-sensitive Krylov diagnostics capture information about many-body chaos missed by spectral measures alone.|
|**2026-10-02**|**The half-filled optical Su-Schrieffer-Heeger-Hubbard model with uniaxial strain**|Jonah Huang, James Neuhaus, Benjamin Cohen-Stead, Steven Johnston, Richard Scalettar|[[arxiv:2610.00681](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00681)]|Strain is known to modify hopping and lattice symmetry in electron-phonon coupled systems, but its effect on the half-filled Su-Schrieffer-Heeger-Hubbard model was unclear. The authors model uniaxial strain via anisotropic hopping and use quantum Monte Carlo simulations to map the phase diagram in the electron-phonon and Hubbard coupling plane at half filling. They find that strain breaks the 90° rotational symmetry, reducing the fourfold degenerate bond-order-wave (BOW) phase to two patterns, and they determine the BOW critical temperature along with directionally dependent gap formation in the spectral function. These results show how strain can be used to control and lift degeneracies of bond-order phases in correlated electron-phonon systems.|
|**2026-10-02**|**Emergence of electronic modes and triplet pairing from spin-1 antiferromagnetic insulators in the Kanamori-Hubbard model**|Masanori Kohno|[[arxiv:2610.00110](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00110)]|This paper addresses how electronic band structures of spin-1 antiferromagnetic (Mott) insulators evolve under doping or heating, a question previously studied mainly for spin-1/2 Hubbard and Kondo insulators. Using the two-orbital Kanamori-Hubbard model combined with weak-hopping effective theory, selection-rule analysis, and numerical calculations, the authors show that emergent in-gap electronic modes inherit momentum-shifted spin-mode dispersions, with doping-induced and temperature-induced modes reflecting different spin excitations across orbitals. They further find that small inter-orbital repulsion allows onsite spin-triplet pairing after doping, producing a pair-breaking gap, while temperature-induced modes remain gapless. These results clarify the interplay of spin, charge, and orbital degrees of freedom in large-spin correlated insulators and highlight routes toward unconventional quantum phenomena and triplet pairing.|

|**2026-10-02**|**Prediction Accuracy Can Select the Scaling Law of Required Precision**|Song-Ju Kim|[[arxiv:2610.00352](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00352)]|The paper asks how much precision is needed, and along which uncertainty channels, to satisfy a given prediction accuracy. It formulates a minimum one-shot specification cost over initial states and forcing parameters, showing that within a fixed set of active channels, tightening tolerance changes required precision by only O(1) bits. Using an exactly solvable multiscale toral system and a forced Lorenz–84 model, it demonstrates that finer accuracy can switch the horizon-scaling of required precision from logarithmic to linear by activating exponentially sensitive, expanding channels. This means prediction accuracy not only sets how much precision is needed but also determines which physical uncertainty channel governs its growth with horizon.|
