|**2026-09-07**|**Fukui-Kawakami chains: spectrum and hidden $\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)$ -symmetry**|Rob Klabbers, Antoine Lefebvre|[[arxiv:2609.04378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04378)]|This paper revisits the trigonometric Haldane–Shastry spin chain under twisted boundary conditions, introducing what they term Fukui–Kawakami (FK) chains. By connecting these chains to the untwisted version, the authors explain part of the spectrum through twisted Bethe equations and Yangian highest weight states, labeled by motifs. They also identify additional descendants whose energies combine single-particle terms, revealing hidden symmetries, and show that the antiperiodic case reduces to a known long-range model with a hidden \(\mathfrak{gl}(1/1)\)-symmetry. This connection links standard HS motifs to supersymmetric counterparts, deepening the understanding of integrable deformations and their symmetries.|

## Mathematical Physics

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-09-07**|**Naked Singularities beyond Spherical Symmetry: Instability of $\kappa$ -Self-Similar Solutions via an Iteration Scheme**|Xinliang An, Shengrong Wu|[[arxiv:2609.04723](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04723)]|This paper addresses the nonlinear instability of nonspherically symmetric, self-similar naked singularities in the Einstein-scalar field system. To overcome challenges from singular backgrounds with angular dependence, the authors develop a novel iterative scheme that freezes nonlinear coefficients using prior double-null geometry and solves equations in a triangular order, effectively incorporating borderline terms into the geometry. Key results show that after sufficient iterations, the scheme controls singular structures, proving that arbitrarily small anisotropic perturbations can trigger trapped surface formation and satisfy a matter-focusing condition for a marginally outer trapped surface. This establishes the instability of such solutions beyond spherical symmetry, offering a new framework applicable to broader Einstein systems.|
|**2026-09-07**|**Fukui-Kawakami chains: spectrum and hidden $\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)$ -symmetry**|Rob Klabbers, Antoine Lefebvre|[[arxiv:2609.04378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04378)]|This paper revisits twisted Haldane-Shastry (HS) chains, termed Fukui-Kawakami (FK) chains, to analyze their spectra as a function of the twist parameter. By connecting these chains to the untwisted HS chain, the authors explain how part of the spectrum follows from twisted Bethe equations and Yangian highest weight states labeled by motifs. They also identify additional descendants whose energies suggest hidden symmetries beyond those captured by the Bethe ansatz. For the antiperiodic case, they show the chain matches a known long-range model with a hidden \(\mathfrak{gl}(1/1)\) symmetry, providing a new link between HS motifs and their supersymmetric counterparts.|
|**2026-09-07**|**Large deviations of Dyson Brownian motion on the circle and multiradial SLE(0+)**|Osama Abuzaid, Vivian Olsiewski Healey, Eveliina Peltola|[[arxiv:2407.13762](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13762)]|This paper establishes a large deviation principle (LDP) for Dyson-type diffusion processes, such as Dyson Brownian motion on the circle, in the limit as the coupling parameter becomes large. The authors analyze both zero-energy and finite-energy systems, showing that zero-energy ones converge exponentially fast to a static configuration, while finite-energy ones converge polynomially and may never become static. They apply this result to derive an LDP for multiradial Schramm-Loewner evolution (SLE) as the parameter approaches zero, with the rate function given by the multiradial Loewner energy. The work also proves that finite-energy Loewner hulls consist of simple curves, except at their common endpoint, bridging statistical physics and complex analysis.|

## Condensed Matter

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|

|**2026-09-07**|**Spin-Charge Subordination in the Infinite- $U$ $SU(N)$ Hubbard Chain**|C\u{a}t\u{a}lin Pa\c{s}cu Moca, Ovidiu I. P\^{a}\c{t}u, Gergely Zar\'{a}nd, Bal\'{a}zs D\'{o}ra|[[arxiv:2609.04814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04814)]|This paper analyzes transport in a one-dimensional infinite-interaction SU(N) Hubbard model, focusing on the relationship between charge and flavor (spin) movement. The authors solve the full counting statistics by leveraging the system's no-passing rule, which lets them treat charge as free fermions while flavor transfer is strictly tied to particle crossings. They derive an exact formula connecting charge and flavor cumulants and show that the flavor distribution follows a universal non-Gaussian M-Wright law. This work establishes spin-charge subordination as a fundamental kinematic cause of the slow, anomalous transport observed in strongly interacting 1D gases.|
|**2026-09-07**|**Fukui-Kawakami chains: spectrum and hidden $\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)$-symmetry**|Rob Klabbers, Antoine Lefebvre|[[arxiv:2609.04378](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04378)]|This paper revisits the twisted Haldane-Shastry spin chains introduced by Fukui and Kawakami, termed Fukui-Kawakami chains, to analyze their spectra as a function of the twist parameter. Using a connection to the untwisted chain, the authors explain which states are described by twisted Bethe equations and Yangian highest weight states, labeled by motifs, while identifying other descendants with energies suggesting hidden symmetries beyond the known framework. They further show that the antiperiodic case coincides with a recently introduced `minimally polarised' model, which reveals a hidden $\mathfrak{gl}(1/1)$ -symmetry. This symmetry allows the authors to connect the original motifs to their supersymmetric counterparts, illuminating the underlying algebraic structure.|
|**2026-09-07**|**Nonlinear Drude weight of the one-dimensional Hubbard model**|Tetsuya Iwasaki, Hosho Katsura|[[arxiv:2608.20269](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.20269)]|This paper investigates nonlinear Drude weights (NLDWs) in the one-dimensional Hubbard model at zero temperature, using exact Bethe-ansatz calculations to test predictions from Tomonaga-Luttinger liquid (TLL) theory. The authors find that while band-curvature corrections produce finite higher-order NLDWs, TLL theory incorrectly predicts divergent weights, as exact finite-size scaling shows all NLDWs remain finite in the thermodynamic limit. At half filling, they analyze the Mott transition and propose a hyperscaling ansatz for NLDWs near the critical point, which is confirmed numerically. The results clarify how nonlinear transport coefficients depend on interactions and reveal the limitations of low-energy effective theories for higher-order response.|
|**2026-09-07**|**Fragmented ETH and Ensemble Inequivalence**|C. L. Sriram, Soumya Kanti Pal, Lea F. Santos|[[arxiv:2607.15350](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15350)]|This paper explores thermalization in finite quantum systems with strong long-range interactions. The authors find that nearly conserved quantities divide the Hilbert space into weakly coupled sectors, creating energy bands that prevent global ergodicity. To address this, they introduce a "fragmented eigenstate thermalization hypothesis" (fETH), where quantum chaos and thermalization occur within individual bands, guided by a symmetry-based selection rule for finite-size scaling. The key significance is that this band-resolved framework explains a natural mismatch between microcanonical (single-band) and canonical (multi-band) ensembles, resolving ensemble inequivalence without requiring phase transitions, with broad applicability to systems near a permutation-symmetric limit.|
|**2026-09-07**|**Hubbard vs. Emery model: spectra, transport and relevance for cuprates**|Jak\v{s}a Vu\v{c}i\v{c}evi\'c, Rok \v{Z}itko|[[arxiv:2604.08085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.08085)]|This paper compares two leading theoretical models for cuprate superconductors: the single-orbital Hubbard and three-orbital Emery models. By analyzing their spectral and transport properties, the authors find that while the models are qualitatively similar, they exhibit distinct quantitative differences that could be distinguished experimentally. Benchmarking against seven experiments on lanthanum-based cuprates, the study shows excellent agreement and suggests the effective coupling constant is larger than commonly thought. The findings identify sensitive observables, such as the Lifshitz transition doping and near-Fermi-level spectral weight, offering a practical path to refine model parameters through future experiments.|
