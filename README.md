|**2026-09-16**|**Diffusivity in Dissipative Quantum Transport from an Exactly Solvable Krylov Chain**|Zhi-Li Zhou, Jorge Noronha|[[arxiv:2609.16559](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16559)]|Computing transport coefficients in interacting quantum many-body systems is generally intractable analytically, so the authors construct an exactly solvable Krylov-space framework for the strong-dissipation regime of a one-dimensional noisy spin-1/2 XXZ chain. They show that a density-polarization-dressed bond coherence generates a Krylov subspace on which the dissipator closes exactly, mapping the dynamics onto a self-similar semi-infinite chain with a boundary defect whose Lanczos coefficients and boundary Green's function are all determined exactly. This yields the exact leading density-dependent correction to the Green-Kubo diffusivity and reveals that the boundary-to-bulk Green's function decays exponentially along the emergent Krylov chain. The result provides a rare analytic handle on dissipative quantum transport and establishes Krylov-space closure as a mechanism for exactly computing transport coefficients.|
|**2026-09-16**|**Analytical solutions to the non-equilibrium Green's functions in large- $q$ SYK models**|Jan C. Louw|[[arxiv:2609.16438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.16438)]|The paper addresses the non-equilibrium Green's functions in large-\(q\) SYK models, where only the instantaneously thermal time block was previously known analytically while out-of-equilibrium blocks required numerics. It derives closed-form analytical solutions for all time blocks following a quench between non-commuting \(q/2\)-body SYK Hamiltonians. The authors find a simple proportionality between pre- and post-quench energies yielding an exact temperature update rule, and prove via Cauchy-Schwarz that heating is inevitable, giving a geometric emergence of the second law. These results enable a companion paper's analysis of "instantaneous thermalization" at finite \(q\).|
|**2026-09-16**|**Krylov Complexity in Non-Inertial Quantum Systems**|Ming-Qi Ma, Shi-Cheng Liu, Lei-Hua Liu, Hai-Qing Zhang|[[arxiv:2606.29770](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29770)]|The paper addresses how Krylov complexity behaves for quantum systems undergoing non-inertial (Rindler) motion, where observer-dependent effects complicate standard spreading measures. Working with the single Rindler-pair SU(1,1) sector, the authors show that the Lanczos basis coincides with the pair-number basis, yielding the exact result that Krylov complexity equals the mean correlated pair number, C_k = /β_k/², with dynamics split into three regimes set by detuning versus coupling, including localization when detuning dominates. Extending to a multimode quadratic Bogoliubov Hamiltonian, they find that inequivalent Rindler wave-packet pairs break this correspondence, so mean pair-number eigenstates no longer determine Krylov complexity. The work thus offers an exactly solvable, observer-adapted benchmark and a new framework for studying Krylov spreading in non-inertial quantum systems.|
|**2026-09-16**|**Bridging the gap between numerics and experiment in free standing graphene**|Maksim Ulybyshev, Savvas Zafeiropoulos, Christopher Winterowd, Fakher Assaad|[[arxiv:2104.09655](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.09655)]|Large-scale quantum Monte Carlo simulations of freestanding graphene were performed with up to 20,808 electrons, reaching energy scales relevant to experiments. By combining constrained RPA estimates of short-range interactions with a Coulomb tail, the authors fixed the microscopic Hamiltonian parameters to reproduce experimental Fermi velocity renormalization data. Comparison with perturbation theory revealed graphene's non-Fermi liquid character and demonstrated that lattice-scale physics and corrections beyond RPA are essential for quantitative agreement with experiment. The same method showed that the optical conductivity remains essentially unrenormalized despite strong single-particle dispersion renormalization, suggesting the Hubbard-model theorem on absent optical conductivity renormalization may extend to long-range interactions.|

|**2026-09-16**|**Dynamics, periodic orbits and $C^1$ non-integrability of the ABC flow**|Wojciech Szumi\'nski, Jaume Llibre|[[arxiv:2609.17449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17449)]|The paper studies the ABC flow near its three integrable coordinate-axis limits, where a degenerate family of periodic streamlines exists. Using first-order averaging, the authors prove the existence of two isolated periodic orbits in each perturbative regime and relate their characteristic multipliers to the eigenvalues of the averaged system. Applying the Poincaré–Llibre–Valls criterion, they show that no nonconstant $C^1$ first integral regular along these orbits exists nearby, establishing $C^1$ non-integrability. Numerical shooting, monodromy computations, and branch continuation confirm the analytical predictions and show the averaging approximation stays accurate well beyond the strict asymptotic regime.|
|**2026-09-16**|**Deformations of (2+1)-dimensional integrable systems and isotropic and anisotropic Harry Dym variants**|Wang Fa-Ren, Jia Man, Lou S Y|[[arxiv:2608.01293](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01293)]|The paper addresses the open problem of generalizing conservation-law-based deformation, previously limited to (1+1)-dimensional systems, to (2+1)-dimensional integrable models.
