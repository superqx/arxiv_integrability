|**2026-09-10**|**Convex-Gaussianity of fermionic Gibbs states in perturbation theory**|Kaifeng Bu, Yuanjie Ren|[[arxiv:2609.09608](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.09608)]|The paper studies when Gibbs states of weakly perturbed interacting fermionic systems can be written as convex combinations of Gaussian states, a property known as convex-Gaussianity. For a sparse Hamiltonian $H = H_0 + V$ with quadratic $H_0$ and perturbation $V$ of scale $\epsilon$, the authors prove convex-Gaussianity holds for inverse temperatures $\beta \le O(\log(1/\epsilon))$, and show this bound is asymptotically tight via $\beta \le \Theta(\log(1/\epsilon))$ necessary conditions. They apply this to the weak-coupling Fermi–Hubbard model, and separately establish a distinct strong-coupling mechanism yielding convex-Gaussianity up to $\beta \le O(/U/^{-1}\log(/U//(D/t/)))$ . The results clarify temperature regimes where interacting fermionic Gibbs states retain a tractable Gaussian decomposition.|
|**2026-09-10**|**Symmetries of the Generalized Yang--Baxter Equations**|Pramod Padmanabhan, Somnath Maity, Akash Sinha, Vladimir Korepin|[[arxiv:2606.26510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.26510)]|The paper studies generalized Yang–Baxter equations, multi-site extensions of the standard Yang–Baxter equation that are expected to yield integrable Hamiltonians with local multi-degree-of-freedom interactions. The authors characterize the continuous and discrete symmetries of these equations and use them to define equivalence classes of solutions, finding that the symmetry set depends on the number of sites and can exceed that of the standard equation. These extra symmetries strongly constrain the number of inequivalent solutions and associated integrable models. As an application, they reduce a generic 16×16 ansatz to a 30-dimensional subspace invariant under a chosen discrete symmetry subgroup and present an explicit 16×16 solution.|
|**2026-09-10**|**Solving Nonequilibrium Dynamics via Influence Matrix Bootstrap: Floquet-PXP Model**|Xiao-Yang Yang, He-Ran Wang, Zhong Wang|[[arxiv:2606.19430](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.19430)]|The paper addresses the challenge of exactly characterizing nonequilibrium dynamics in integrable quantum many-body systems, which remains difficult despite progress on equilibrium properties. The authors develop an influence matrix bootstrap approach that uses generalized zipper conditions—local tensor relations constrained by finite-time data—to construct exact solutions valid for arbitrarily long times, demonstrated on the integrable "Rule 201" quantum cellular automaton (a Trotterized PXP model). This yields analytical results for nonthermal relaxation constrained by conservation laws, numerically exact entanglement growth across many initial states, and a refined information-theoretic structure of multitime correlations (a hidden Markov order splitting memory into finite-length and long-range components). The method enables unified study of thermalizing and nonthermalizing regimes in one tractable model and is experimentally testable in platforms like Rydberg atom arrays.|
|**2026-09-10**|**A Pontryagin class obstruction for purely electric and purely magnetic Weyl curvature tensors**|Thijs de Kok|[[arxiv:2604.03707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03707)]|The paper asks whether every manifold admitting a Lorentzian metric also admits one with purely electric (PE) or purely magnetic (PM) Weyl curvature. Using the behavior of algebraic curvature tensors under orientation-reversing isometries, the authors prove that all products of Pontryagin forms landing in top degree vanish for 4k-dimensional pseudo-Riemannian manifolds with PE or PM Riemann or Weyl curvature. This yields nontrivial cohomological obstructions on compact manifolds, with applications to Lorentzian metrics of various Petrov subtypes and to foliations by nondegenerate umbilic hypersurfaces.|
|**2026-09-10**|**Second-order superintegrable systems from semi-simple and nilpotent Frobenius structures**|Andreas Vollmer|[[arxiv:2601.01978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.01978)]|The paper addresses the difficulty of classifying second-order maximally superintegrable systems in higher dimensions, where conventional PDE-based methods become intractable. It exploits the recently established correspondence between such systems on constant-curvature spaces and Hesse–Frobenius structures, using a Neumann series expansion to construct local solutions directly from algebraic initial data (commutative, possibly non-associative Frobenius algebras). Two new constructions are introduced—a conification that lifts non-zero constant-curvature systems to flat ones and thereby reduces the algebraic problem to the associative case, and a direct product construction generating higher-dimensional examples from lower-dimensional ones. Applying these to basic semi-simple and nilpotent algebras yields explicit systems, and notably all non-degenerate second-order maximally superintegrable systems in three dimensions are shown to arise from these examples.|

The authors map the local Boltzmann weights onto a non-symmetric eight-vertex R-matrix satisfying the free-fermion condition for arbitrary couplings, then analyze the Yang–Baxter structure for the cases J'i = Ji and J'i = -Ji and derive the corresponding 1D quantum spin chains in the anisotropic limit. This establishes the anisotropic kagomé Ising model as an inhomogeneous XYZ integrable model, extending its exact solvability and connecting it to distinct quantum spin-chain classes.|

It finds that doping induces electronic modes inside the band gap in an orbital-selective way, with momentum-shifted spin-mode dispersion relations. Modes tied to conventional spin excitations (including low-energy ones) emerge in the doped orbital, while those from inter-orbital spin excitations appear in the undoped orbital. These emergent modes are argued to be an essential feature of the OSMT, offering a new signature for identifying it.|

This establishes a minimal, material-independent framework showing that anomalous finite-temperature spin fluctuations are intrinsic to the flat-band degeneracy, thereby bridging exact ground-state results and finite-temperature quantum Monte Carlo studies.|

|**2026-09-10**|**Rogue-like waves from collision of mKdV solitons**|Stephen C. Anco, Jaskaran Maa|[[arxiv:2609.10512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10512)]|The paper investigates whether collisions between two solitary waves of opposite polarity in the modified Korteweg-de Vries equation can generate rogue-like waves. By analyzing the interaction profiles, it shows that for asymptotically vanishing waves the maximum amplification ratio is 2.41 at nearly equal speeds, falling to 2 when the speed ratio is 1.5, while for waves approaching a nonzero constant at infinity the ratio peaks at 2.65 when the speed ratio is 6.32. These results establish a soliton-collision mechanism for rogue-wave formation and quantify how the amplification depends on the relative speeds and boundary conditions of the interacting waves.|
