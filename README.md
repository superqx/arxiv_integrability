|**2026-09-02**|**Scalar wave scattering by black holes embedded in dark matter halos**|Shi-Qian Hu, Federico Piazza|[[arxiv:2609.01391](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.01391)]|This paper investigates how a dark matter halo, modeled with a Hernquist profile, alters the scattering patterns of massless scalar waves off a black hole. The authors employ multiple theoretical methods, including geodesic analysis and wave-based approaches, to calculate the scattering cross-sections. They find that while the halo introduces small shifts in certain parameters, the primary correction scales with the halo's compactness, and much of the effect can be mimicked by a simple frequency shift in a vacuum Schwarzschild model. However, a residual discrepancy confirms a genuine halo contribution, which their complex angular momentum analysis shows can be efficiently modeled using only a few Regge poles.|
|**2026-09-02**|**Collective superintegrable systems from the Guillemin--Sternberg torus action**|L. Feher|[[arxiv:2608.01878](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01878)]|This paper introduces a new method for establishing the superintegrability of collective Hamiltonians that remain invariant under a Hamiltonian action of a connected semisimple compact Lie group on a symplectic manifold. The authors use a specific Hamiltonian torus action from Guillemin and Sternberg to derive a precise equality linking the functional dimension of the invariant Hamiltonians and their centralizer to the manifold's dimension. This equality, combined with a non-triviality condition, confirms the existence of a superintegrable system and shows that the torus action's momentum map provides the action variables. The work offers a novel geometric perspective that complements previous findings by Bolsinov and Jovanović.|

The researchers proved a theorem showing that a zero-field O(n)-vector chain with competing interactions is mathematically equivalent, via a temperature-independent mapping, to a simpler chain with nearest-neighbor interactions and an external axial field, applicable to Ising, XY, and Heisenberg models. The significance lies in demonstrating that sustained human-AI teamwork can yield fundamental theoretical advances, potentially fostering autonomous scientific discovery.|

|**2026-09-02**|**A Human-AI Theorem Connecting Spontaneous and Field-Induced Mechanisms of Collective Behavior in One Dimension**|Weiguo Yin|[[arxiv:2609.00322](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00322)]|This paper documents a human-AI collaboration that proved a theorem connecting two fundamental mechanisms in statistical physics: collective behavior arising without an external field versus that induced by one. The theorem shows that a zero-field chain with complex interactions can be mathematically mapped to a simpler chain with an external field, unifying the descriptions of these phenomena across various spin models. This equivalence provides exact solutions for previously unsolved systems, including the frustrated \(J_1\)-\(J_2\) Potts model. The key significance is that the theorem originated from an AI working outside the researcher's active hypothesis space, demonstrating a potential pathway for AI to autonomously generate scientific breakthroughs in sustained collaboration.|
|**2026-09-02**|**Engineering tunable $p$ -wave magnetism in antiferromagnetic bilayers**|Yu-Han Lin, Jin-Wei Dong, Ziqiang Wang, Sen Zhou|[[arxiv:2609.00277](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00277)]|The paper introduces a design strategy for creating tunable \(p\)-wave magnetism in bilayer antiferromagnets by shifting the layers relative to each other, which breaks spin-degeneracy symmetries without requiring spin-orbit coupling. Using symmetry analysis and a mean-field Hubbard model, the authors demonstrate that the direction and magnitude of odd-parity spin splitting can be controlled continuously via in-plane displacement. They further show that spontaneous \(p\)-wave magnetic states, including coplanar and chiral spin textures, emerge over large regions of the phase diagram. This work provides a minimal, experimentally feasible platform for realizing and controlling \(p\)-wave magnetism in two-dimensional materials.|
|**2026-09-02**|**Green-function Zeros Encode Competing Mott and Charge-ordering Scales**|Peizhi Mai, Philip W. Phillips|[[arxiv:2609.00124](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.00124)]|This paper investigates what the "zeros" in the Green's function of correlated insulators physically signify. Through analytical and numerical study of the extended Hubbard model, the authors show that the dispersion of these zeros is governed by both microscopic spin-spin correlations and the kinematics of charge defects. A key finding is that this dispersion changes discontinuously at the transition between Mott and charge-density wave phases, indicating a first-order transition. The work establishes that the dispersion of Green-function zeros provides a fundamental descriptor of competing orders in strongly correlated systems.|
|**2026-09-02**|**Doping-driven Mott transition from steady-state density functional theory**|David Jacob, Stefan Kurth|[[arxiv:2608.21029](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.21029)]|This paper applies steady-state density functional theory (i-DFT) to model the doping-driven Mott transition in the Hubbard model. The authors derive new conditions for the exchange-correlation bias based on Fermi-liquid theory, linking it to the quasiparticle weight, which guides the creation of a new approximation for the functional. Numerical results show that this functional successfully captures the Mott transition, marking a key result for i-DFT. The work is significant because it provides an explicit functional map between the many-body spectral function and the ground-state density, advancing the theoretical toolkit for out-of-equilibrium systems.|
|**2026-09-02**|**Exact Gaussian pinning of a relative-phase mode and interaction-driven detuning from the pinned frequency**|Yogeshwar Prasad|[[arxiv:2605.10387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.10387)]|This paper analytically demonstrates that in paired fermionic systems, the interlayer hybridization energy \(2t_h\) appears as an exact pinning frequency for a collective relative-phase mode within Gaussian fluctuation theory. The proof relies on a parity-pair identity and holds universally across different chemical potentials, band structures, and dimensions. However, this Gaussian-level result is not protected by symmetries, as an exact interaction torque and numerical simulations on small clusters reveal significant detuning—for example, at \(/U/=4t\), the dominant excitation shifts to \(0.467(2t_h)\) while retaining most of the spectral weight. The work establishes \(2t_h\) as a key reference frequency whose many-body renormalization can be substantial, providing a benchmark for understanding interaction effects on collective modes in superconductors.|
|**2026-09-02**|**Using the slowest observable in one-dimensional Markov processes to construct quasi-exactly-solvable generators with two explicit levels**|Cecile Monthus|[[arxiv:2604.15908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15908)]|This paper reinterprets the construction of quasi-exactly-solvable (QES) quantum Hamiltonians through the lens of one-dimensional Markov processes with detailed balance, where the ground state corresponds to the steady state and the first excited state determines the slowest relaxation mode. The authors propose that by treating the ratio of the two quantum eigenstates—the "slowest observable"—as the primary object, one can more intuitively and simply build generators that explicitly solve for two energy levels. They demonstrate this method for both continuous Fokker-Planck generators and discrete Markov jump generators. The key significance lies in offering a simpler, more physically grounded approach to QES model construction, with potential applications across statistical physics and quantum mechanics.|
|**2026-09-02**|**Stability and complexity of global iterative solvers for the Kadanoff-Baym equations**|Jo\v{z}e Ga\v{s}perlin, Denis Gole\v{z}, Jason Kaye|[[arxiv:2512.11371](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11371)]|This paper investigates global iterative solvers for the Kadanoff-Baym equations as an alternative to standard time-stepping, which could benefit from compressed data representations. The authors analyze various iterative methods, including fixed-point and Newton-Krylov variants, across different strongly correlated models. They find that while most methods converge stably for long times, the iteration count scales linearly with the number of time steps, a trend linked to a method-dependent propagating front in the error. This linear scaling presents a key challenge that must be overcome for these solvers to become computationally competitive with conventional time-stepping approaches.|
|**2026-09-02**|**Interplay of disorder and interactions in the bilayer band-insulator : A determinant quantum Monte Carlo study**|Yogeshwar Prasad, Hunpyo Lee|[[arxiv:2309.05243](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.05243)]|This paper investigates how random on-site disorder affects the half-filled bilayer attractive Hubbard model, which in its clean form exhibits a quantum phase transition from a band insulator to a superfluid. Using determinant quantum Monte Carlo simulations, the authors find that disorder localizes pairs and suppresses effective hopping, increasing double occupancy similarly to stronger attraction. However, the critical interaction strength for the phase transition remains unchanged, but the superfluid region shrinks, especially in the strong-coupling limit. The study also estimates the critical disorder strength needed to destroy superfluidity at a specific coupling, clarifying how disorder and interactions compete in this system.|
