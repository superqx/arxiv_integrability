|**2026-09-11**|**Certified reduced-basis emulation of conformal field theories on the fuzzy sphere**|Virgil V. Baran|[[arxiv:2609.11675](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11675)]|The paper addresses the high computational cost of locating critical points in fuzzy-sphere regularized 3D CFTs, where testing for criticality across microscopic coupling space requires many exact diagonalizations. It combines total-angular-momentum-resolved exact diagonalization with a certified reduced-basis emulator that reconstructs energies, observables, and their parameter derivatives from a few exact solutions per system size. The method reproduces published ground-state-energy and conformal data for the fuzzy-sphere Ising and O(2) Wilson–Fisher models, and for the O(2) model without CFT input locates the critical coupling within 0.5% of a DMRG value from systems over twice as large while mapping the critical line. This reduces search cost by roughly two orders of magnitude, though the authors note that ground-state criteria pin down the critical line robustly but determine the selected point along it only weakly.|
|**2026-09-11**|**Lattice 2-group symmetries: operators, defects, and gauging**|Lucas Z. Brito, Salvatore D. Pace|[[arxiv:2609.10682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10682)]|The paper addresses how finite 2-group symmetries, combining 0-form and 1-form symmetries, can be realized in (2+1)-dimensional quantum lattice systems with finite-dimensional tensor-product Hilbert spaces. It constructs lattice symmetry operators for split 2-groups with trivial Postnikov class and central 2-groups with trivial action, showing that the latter generally require ancillae to be made onsite. The authors show that these operators reproduce quantum field theory expectations through charge transformations, defect structures, and dual fusion 2-category symmetries obtained by gauging. They also propose local symmetric Hamiltonians with exactly solvable limits exhibiting spontaneous 2-group symmetry breaking, symmetry-enriched topological order, and lattice realizations of 2-group gauge theories with computed ground-state degeneracies.|
|**2026-09-11**|**Higher-spins on Taub-NUT and higher-spin Taub-NUT**|Evgeny Skvortsov, Yihao Yin|[[arxiv:2508.18804](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18804)]|The paper studies how higher-spin fields can consistently propagate on gravitational instantons by extending self-dual Yang-Mills (HS-SDYM) and self-dual Gravity (HS-SDGR) to the Taub-NUT background. The authors construct exact solutions to the higher-spin equations on Taub-NUT for HS-SDYM, and show that for HS-SDGR the perturbation theory converges at each spin, yielding a higher-spin generalization of Taub-NUT. They identify a commutative non-associative algebra that governs the structure of this perturbation theory. This establishes Taub-NUT as a viable background for higher-spin theories and provides a new algebraic framework for their analysis.|
|**2026-09-11**|**Tremblay-Turbiner-Winternitz (TTW) system at integer index $k$ : polynomial algebras of integrals**|Juan Carlos L\'opez Vieyra, Alexander V Turbiner|[[arxiv:2503.09502](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.09502)]|The paper addresses the TTW system, a family of superintegrable and exactly-solvable quantum models on a plane characterized by an integer index \(k\). It conjectures that the Hamiltonian and the two integrals of the TTW system possess a hidden algebra \(g^{(k)}\), and verifies that for \(k=1,2,3,4\), the Hamiltonian, two integrals, and their commutator generate a polynomial algebra of integrals of order \(k+1\). These results show that the polynomial algebra of integrals is a subalgebra of \(g^{(k)}\), and the authors conjecture this holds for all integer \(k\), providing new structural insight into the integrability of these models.|

|**2026-09-11**|**Infinite temperature at zero energy**|Matteo Ippoliti, David M. Long|[[arxiv:2509.04410](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.04410)]|The paper addresses the challenge of constructing local Hamiltonians whose ground states exhibit high (volume-law) entanglement, a property normally associated with highly excited states. The authors adapt the Feynman-Kitaev clock construction by imposing periodic boundary conditions on the clock register, effectively encoding Floquet dynamics into a static, geometrically local Hamiltonian. Assuming the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis for the input circuit, they show that eigenstates across the entire spectrum—including the ground state—display infinite-temperature properties such as volume-law entanglement entropy. By combining this with an exactly solvable Floquet circuit family obeying ETH at infinite temperature, they obtain the first provable local Hamiltonians with volume-law-entangled ground states for all contiguous subsystems.|

The result suggests that this polynomial algebra structure extends to all integer \(k\), providing a deeper algebraic understanding of the TTW system's integrability.|

|**2026-09-11**|**Rare-History Transitions in Temporally Random Integrable Quantum Circuits**|Tingfei Li, Jie Gu|[[arxiv:2609.11415](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11415)]|The paper studies current fluctuations in temporally random integrable quantum circuits, where randomness in the drive history complicates the statistics of transported charge. By exploiting commutativity, each drive history is reduced to its layer composition, and for each fixed composition the authors combine homogeneous thermodynamic Bethe ansatz dressing with ballistic fluctuation theory to obtain conditional current statistics. Annealed large-deviation contraction then reveals a first-order transition between two dominant history classes, ending on a line of regular cusp endpoints, with finite-time analysis showing how this switch is rounded. The results establish temporal randomness as an emergent order-parameter-like coordinate in trajectory space.|
|**2026-09-11**|**Instabilities in self-consistent diagrammatic approaches and how to cure them**|Marcel Gievers, Herbert E{\ss}l, Stefan Rohshap, Anna Kauch, Alessandro Toschi|[[arxiv:2609.11405](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11405)]|Self-consistent diagrammatic methods used for correlated quantum materials can converge to unphysical fixed points—a failure called "misleading convergence"—precisely in the intermediate-to-strong interaction regimes of greatest interest. Working within the boson-exchange formalism, the authors show that this problem arises from the stability properties of the iteration scheme itself rather than from any intrinsic breakdown of the underlying diagrammatic description. They cure it by redefining the iterative procedure so that the unstable eigendirections of the Jacobian at the fixed point are inverted, and validate the approach on exactly solvable testbed models. The remedy stabilizes the physical fixed point across essentially the entire parameter range, including strong-coupling regimes, substantially extending the applicability of these widely used methods.|
|**2026-09-11**|**Disordered ground states in one-dimensional exactly solvable fluids**|Igor Trav\v{e}nec, Ladislav \v{S}amaj|[[arxiv:2609.11394](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11394)]|The paper studies one-dimensional classical fluids with soft repulsive pair potentials that reduce to nearest-neighbor interactions when the potential range satisfies \(a' \le 2a\), allowing exact treatment in the isothermal-isobaric ensemble. Focusing on zero-temperature ground states, it derives the equation of state for the mean nearest-neighbor spacing and the pair correlation function, identifying a special "incompressibility" pressure \(p_i\) where the ground state can become disordered for concave potentials. For strictly concave potentials the disordered ground state has a specific spacing and a pair correlation function given by weighted Dirac delta functions, while for a linear ramp it yields a continuous correlation function built from Heaviside step functions times polynomials. The nonzero isothermal susceptibility at \(p_i\) shows these disordered ground states are non-hyperuniform, resembling finite-temperature fluids, and their correlation functions remain predictive for thermodynamic states over a range of pressures around \(p_i\).|
|**2026-09-11**|**Lattice 2-group symmetries: operators, defects, and gauging**|Lucas Z. Brito, Salvatore D. Pace|[[arxiv:2609.10682](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10682)]|The paper addresses how finite 2-group symmetries—generalizing ordinary group symmetries—can be realized on 2+1d quantum lattice systems with finite-dimensional tensor-product Hilbert spaces. It constructs lattice symmetry operators for split and central 2-groups, showing the former can be onsite while the latter require ancillae, and examines how the Postnikov class and action manifest in charged operators, symmetry defects, and dual fusion 2-category symmetries after gauging. The authors also propose local symmetric Hamiltonians with exactly solvable limits exhibiting spontaneous 2-group symmetry breaking and nontrivial symmetry-enriched topological order, including lattice realizations of 2-group gauge theories with computed ground-state degeneracies. These results connect lattice models to quantum field theory expectations and provide concrete exactly solvable settings for studying 2-group symmetry and its gauging.|
|**2026-09-11**|**Krylov Edge Spectroscopy of Symmetry-Protected Topological Phases**|Heiko Georg Menzler, Rishabh Jha|[[arxiv:2609.10676](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.10676)]|The paper addresses the challenge of detecting and classifying one-dimensional symmetry-protected topological (SPT) phases without relying on exact diagonalization, ground-state wavefunctions, entanglement spectra, or guessed edge operators. It introduces Krylov edge spectroscopy, a many-body operator-space protocol in which a Hermitian boundary operator generates a semi-infinite Krylov hopping chain whose boundary weight satisfies an exact zero-frequency normalizability criterion, with classification based on minimizing normalized commutators over symmetry-resolved boundary operators. Demonstrated on cluster, clock, Haldane, and trivial spin-1 chains, the method reveals that the topological-to-trivial transition in the cluster chain appears as a localization-delocalization transition of the Krylov edge mode occurring exactly at the bulk gap closing, with a localization-length exponent matching the Ising correlation-length exponent. This work establishes local boundary evolution as a direct spectroscopic probe of many-body topology, offering a scalable alternative to conventional SPT diagnostics.|
|**2026-09-11**|**Anomalous Diffusion and Superdiffusion in Integrable Spin Chains via a Soliton Gas Mapping**|Andrew Urilyon, Romain Vasseur, Sarang Gopalakrishnan, Jacopo De Nardis|[[arxiv:2603.02171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.02171)]|The paper addresses anomalous spin and charge transport in integrable quantum systems by introducing a multi-species soliton gas in which each species has a distinct effective scattering length and repulsive scattering shifts are governed by the smaller of two colliding particles. The authors show that fixing only the functional decay of bare velocities with particle size reproduces the XXZ spin-transport phase diagram, yielding diffusion with anomalous fluctuations in the anisotropic regime and superdiffusion at the isotropic point. They further find that charge-transfer statistics differ qualitatively from particle-trajectory statistics: large-particle trajectories are Gaussian in the diffusive regime but appear KPZ-like at the isotropic point, while charge-transfer fluctuations are anomalous in the anisotropic regime and become Gaussian at late times at the isotropic point. This establishes the soliton gas as a minimal framework for anomalous spin and charge transport and offers new insight into the origin of KPZ fluctuations in isotropic integrable models.|
|**2026-09-11**|**Symbolic recursion method for strongly correlated fermions in two and three dimensions**|Igor Ermakov, Oleg Lychkovskiy|[[arxiv:2512.23678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23678)]|The paper addresses the challenge of computing dynamical correlations and transport in strongly correlated fermionic systems on one-, two-, and three-dimensional lattices, where existing methods struggle with the strongly correlated regime and finite-size limitations. It introduces a symbolic implementation of the recursion method that works directly in the thermodynamic limit and covers all time scales, from short times to late-time asymptotics. Applying the approach to interacting spinless fermions and the Hubbard model, the authors confirm the universal operator growth hypothesis, compute infinite-temperature current autocorrelations, and extract the charge diffusion constant and high-temperature conductivity across both perturbative and nonperturbative interaction regimes, locating the boundary between them. The work is significant for establishing a symbolic computational paradigm in which the costly step is performed once to produce reusable symbolic output yielding observables for arbitrary model parameters, and for agreeing with the Majorana propagation method where the latter converges.|
|**2026-09-11**|**Autoregressive Neural Network Extrapolation of Quantum Spin Dynamics Across Time and Space**|Hubert Pugzlys, Shreyas Varude, Sam Dillon, Huy Tran, Ta Tang, Brian Moritz, Zhe Jiang, Chunjing Jia|[[arxiv:2512.13103](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.13103)]|Conventional numerical methods like DMRG struggle to simulate long-time dynamics in strongly correlated quantum systems because entanglement growth makes simulations increasingly expensive, limiting access to long-range correlations. The authors introduce an autoregressive machine-learning framework that extrapolates dynamical spin correlations in both time and space without explicitly evolving the many-body wavefunction, trained on DMRG data for the gapless XXZ model and validated against exact solutions. They find that a multi-layer perceptron with ReLU activations outperforms convolutional neural networks and linear regression for longer-time extrapolation, and that error accumulation remains highly robust to perturbations. This approach offers a complementary, stable route to probing gapless quantum many-body dynamics beyond the reach of state-of-the-art numerical methods.|
|**2026-09-11**|**A path to superconductivity via strong short-range repulsion in a spin-polarized band**|Zhiyu Dong, Patrick A. Lee|[[arxiv:2511.17466](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.17466)]|The paper addresses whether strong short-range electron repulsion, normally destructive to superconductivity, can instead facilitate pairing in a spin-polarized band on a two-dimensional triangular lattice. Using the Hubbard model with on-site and nearest-neighbor repulsion, the authors show that symmetry constraints cause the usually pair-breaking first-order interaction to vanish in certain f-wave channels, leaving subleading-order processes to drive pairing. They find an f-wave superconducting transition temperature of roughly 1% of the electron bandwidth, and note that for a general screened interaction the mechanism holds asymptotically but requires a third-order calculation to pin down the pairing strength. This identifies a route to superconductivity in which strong repulsion, rather than being an obstacle, becomes the enabling ingredient.|
