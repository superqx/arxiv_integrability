|**2026-08-12**|**Monodromy defects in ABJM theory and integrability**|Charlotte Kristjansen, Andrey Shusharin|[[arxiv:2608.10840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10840)]|The paper addresses the challenge of computing one-point functions in the presence of supersymmetric monodromy defects (vortex loops) within ABJM theory, connecting them to integrable spin chains. By applying the algebraic Bethe ansatz and the KT relation, the authors derive a closed-form expression for these one-point functions at leading order, specifically for non-protected operators. This result generalizes existing overlaps, such as those for three-point functions involving maximal giant gravitons, and is extended to include first quantum corrections. The work establishes a rigorous integrability framework for defect correlators, offering a new tool for probing non-perturbative aspects of ABJM theory and its holographic dual.|

## Mathematical Physics

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-08-12**|**Nonlinear systems of PDEs admitting infinite-dimensional Lie algebras and their connection with Ricci flows. II: The two-dimensional space case**|Roman Cherniha, John R. King|[[arxiv:2608.11128](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11128)]|This paper extends prior work to construct all possible two-component (1+2)-dimensional second-order evolution systems of PDEs that admit an infinite-dimensional Lie algebra. The authors then show that generalizing this algebra to higher dimensions breaks the infinite-dimensional symmetry, and they identify a recently derived system related to Ricci flows as a special case. For this Ricci-flow system, they construct all stationary radially symmetric solutions by exploiting the rich Lie algebra of the reduced ODE system, proving it simplifies to a fifteen-dimensional algebra. Additionally, they derive several time-dependent exact solutions and demonstrate that these solutions remain bounded and smooth for appropriately chosen parameters, highlighting the algebraic structure's role in solving nonlinear systems.|
|**2026-08-12**|**Superconductivity and Low Energy Excitations in an Attractive Hubbard Model**|Yukimi Goto, Tohru Koma, Hironobu Yoshida|[[arxiv:2509.19780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.19780)]|This paper investigates the attractive Hubbard model on bipartite lattices, focusing on its superconducting properties and low-energy excitations. The authors prove the existence of superconducting long-range order in the ground state for Lieb lattices at half filling within the grand canonical formalism. For nontrivial fermion densities, they show that pairing excitations are gapless, while a specific class of single-fermion excitations exhibits a spectral gap in the strong-coupling, low-density regime. These rigorous results clarify the conditions for superconductivity and gap formation in this fundamental model.|
|**2026-08-12**|**Magnetization of electronic ground states in frustrated superstable graphs**|F. P. M. M\'endez-C\'ordoba, J. Tindall, D. Jaksch, F. Schlawin|[[arxiv:2509.07079](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.07079)]|This paper analytically determines the ground-state magnetization of the half-filled Hubbard model on frustrated lattices that exhibit "superstable" eigenstates. By focusing on this broad class of geometries, including those relevant to altermagnetism and superconductivity, the authors derive exact results for the system's magnetic properties. Their findings reveal evidence for phase transitions characterized by a geometric rearrangement of magnetic correlations in the thermodynamic limit. This work provides a foundation for understanding unconventional quantum phases in strongly correlated electron systems.|

## Condensed Matter

|**2026-08-12**|**Work distribution for strongly coupled many-body open quantum systems**|H. T. M. Nghiem, T. A. Costi, S. Campbell, A. K. Mitchell|[[arxiv:2608.11196](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11196)]|This paper investigates the quantum work distribution function (WDF) for strongly coupled many-body open systems following a quench. To address this, the authors extend the time-dependent numerical renormalization group (TDNRG) method to non-perturbatively compute the WDF in the thermodynamic limit for arbitrary quench amplitudes at any temperature. The key result is the observation of power-law threshold behavior in the WDF near its minimum, driven by the Anderson orthogonality catastrophe, which exhibits universal scaling collapse below a low-energy scale emergent from system-bath correlations. The work provides a numerically exact framework for exploring non-equilibrium thermodynamics in strong-coupling regimes, offering new insights into universal behavior in quantum impurity models.|
|**2026-08-12**|**Composite-Boson Ansatz for Fractional Quantum Hall Manifolds of Lattice Bosons at Generic Fillings $\nu<1/2$**|Umut Can Turhan, A. Levent Suba\c{s}\i, Rifat Onur Umucal\ilar|[[arxiv:2608.10747](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10747)]|This paper introduces a composite-boson ansatz to describe fractional quantum Hall (FQH) states in the Hofstadter-Bose-Hubbard model on a torus at fillings ν<1/2. By attaching two vortices to each boson, the authors construct a trial basis whose translation-resolved rank matches the expected low-energy manifold dimension, offering a microscopic interpretation of established quasihole counting rules. For small systems, diagonalization within this ansatz reproduces the exact projected spectrum, while variational Monte Carlo extends the analysis to larger systems. The manifold's FQH character is confirmed via many-body Chern numbers and quasihole-like excitations with fractional density depletion and a topological braiding phase, establishing a general framework for lattice boson FQH states.|
|**2026-08-12**|**Spin Rotatory Strength as the Equilibrium Observable for Chiral-Induced Spin Selectivity**|Rub\'en Dar\io Guerrero|[[arxiv:2608.10697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10697)]|This paper addresses the challenge of finding an equilibrium observable for chiral-induced spin selectivity (CISS). The authors identify the spin rotatory strength, a finite-frequency spin-dipole cross response, as this observable, using exact diagonalization of a Kane-Mele-Hubbard model. They find that gapped molecules suppress this response, while near-degenerate systems amplify it at small sizes, though the effect at larger sizes is uncertain. The work provides a concrete, testable signature for CISS that connects the effect to time-reversal symmetry and Wilson-loop flux.|
|**2026-08-12**|**Symmetrized Block-Product Periodic Marginals in Infinite Translation-Invariant Quantum Chains**|Xiao Zeng, Kaiyan Yang, Lingxia Zhang, Zizhu Wang|[[arxiv:2608.10365](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10365)]|This paper addresses the problem of detecting hidden finite-period order in infinite one-dimensional translation-invariant quantum systems using local marginals. The authors propose a symmetrized block-product periodic marginal problem, approached via two complementary methods: a semidefinite-programming relaxation with permutation and positivity constraints for certified infeasibility tests, and a symmetrized matrix product state ansatz for variational upper bounds. Tested on models like Majumdar-Ghosh and transverse-field Ising, the framework accurately captures expected periodic structures and yields improving variational energies with larger periods and bond dimensions. The work provides a practical diagnostic tool for hidden order and a rigorous method for bounding ground-state energies, extended also to contextuality witnesses via a periodic-NPA relaxation.|
|**2026-08-12**|**Ripple Signatures of Majorana Hybridization across a Topological Quantum Quench**|Xin-Xin Wang, Jin-Xin Li, Ya-Wen Tang, Lu Qin, Zun-Lue Zhu, Wu-Ming Liu, Liang-Liang Wang, Xing-Dong Zhao|[[arxiv:2608.10052](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10052)]|This paper uses time-dependent Bogoliubov-de Gennes theory to study Majorana boundary modes after a topological quantum quench in a fermionic superfluid. It shows that quenches within the topological regime preserve these modes, but they undergo coherent boundary oscillations. For a quench that changes topology, a distinct ripple pattern appears in the density, which is traced to the coherent hybridization of the initially separated Majorana states. These results offer a new, nonequilibrium method for detecting Majorana physics that complements traditional equilibrium probes.|
|**2026-08-12**|**Beyond the Markovian limit: Exact solutions for active motion in a power-law viscoelastic bath**|Mintu Karmakar, Jure Dobnikar, Ignacio Pagonabarraga|[[arxiv:2606.10145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10145)]|This paper develops an analytical theory for active particles moving through power-law viscoelastic media, addressing the limitations of the classical active Brownian model which assumes instantaneous friction. By solving coupled non-Markovian generalized Langevin equations for translational and rotational motion, the authors uncover novel phenomena like fractional short-time transport and enhanced long-time persistence. They show that the viscoelastic memory kernel sets the anomalous scaling exponents, while activity governs transitions between sub-diffusive, ballistic, and diffusive regimes. This work offers a theoretical foundation for modeling micro-swimmers in complex biological and polymeric environments.|
|**2026-08-12**|**Superconductivity beyond band geometry: emergence of pair quantum geometry**|Mehmet Akif Keskiner, M. Iskin|[[arxiv:2606.06017](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.06017)]|This paper extends the concept of quantum geometry from single particles to bound pairs and Cooper pairs in multiband systems. The authors derive an exact effective-mass theorem for two-body bound states and a geometric framework for Cooper pairs near the superconducting critical temperature, revealing additional mass contributions from pair-specific quantum metrics. They show that these pair-geometric terms are significant in exact numerical calculations across several lattice models, including sawtooth and Hofstadter lattices. The findings establish pair quantum geometry as a crucial factor in understanding bound-pair dynamics and multiband superconductivity, beyond what band geometry alone predicts.|
|**2026-08-12**|**Emergent gauge flux and spin ordering in magnetized triangular spin liquids: Applications to Hofstadter-Hubbard model**|Jiahao Yang, Hao Tian, Si-Yu Pan, Gang v. Chen|[[arxiv:2601.12898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12898)]|This paper studies spin-1/2 systems on a triangular lattice, motivated by moiré superlattices and antiferromagnets, to understand how orbital magnetic flux and Zeeman coupling (external magnetic field) affect spin-liquid states. The authors show that orbital flux favors a chiral spin liquid, while Zeeman coupling instead drives a transition to a state with uniform internal flux, Landau-level behavior, and conical spin order. They demonstrate this using a J1-J2-Jχ model and identify distinct thermal Hall and magnetic signatures for these competing phases. The findings offer experimental predictions applicable to moiré Hofstadter-Hubbard systems and triangular-lattice antiferromagnets.|
|**2026-08-12**|**Thermalization in a closed quantum system from randomized dynamics**|Nikolay V. Gnezdilov, Andrei I. The authors show that strong random perturbations, combined with a constraint on total energy, produce a canonical ensemble as the first moment of the Porter-Thomas distribution of chaotic eigenstates. They demonstrate this effect numerically using the 1D transverse-field Ising model, observing canonically distributed observables with a temperature-dependent correlation length. This work offers an alternative pathway to thermalization that could simplify thermal state preparation on quantum computers.|

The authors derive exact results for the magnetization in these systems, which include lattices known to host altermagnetic and superconducting phases. Their findings reveal evidence for phase transitions involving a geometric rearrangement of magnetic correlations in the thermodynamic limit. This work provides a rigorous framework for understanding how geometric frustration dictates magnetic order in strongly correlated electron systems.|
