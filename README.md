|**2026-09-28**|**On the Cauchy problem for the Tzitz\'{e}ica equation: Soliton resolution conjecture and asymptotic analysis**|Shou-Fu Tian, Jia-Fu Tong|[[arxiv:2609.30858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30858)]|The paper studies the long-time asymptotics of solutions to the Cauchy problem for the Tzitzéica equation, an integrable model from affine differential geometry, extending earlier work limited to purely continuous spectrum. Using a $\bar{\partial}$-nonlinear steepest descent method, the authors analyze three asymptotic regions depending on $\xi = x/t$ and derive rigorous decay and approximation estimates, including $O(t^{-1})$ decay outside $[-1,1]$ and $O(t^{-3/4})$ errors inside, where an $N$ -soliton solution plus interaction terms emerges. These results confirm the soliton resolution conjecture for the Tzitzéica equation and provide a detailed asymptotic description across all regions.|
|**2026-09-28**|**The para--Racah Hamiltonian as an exactly solvable discretization of the P\"oschl--Teller box**|St\'ephane Z. Beaulac, Nicolas Cramp\'e, Quentin Labriet, Lucia Morey, Marlon Josue Rivera Valladares, Luc Vinet|[[arxiv:2609.30624](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30624)]|The paper identifies the para-Racah Hamiltonian as an exactly solvable finite-matrix discretization of the trigonometric Pöschl-Teller box, showing that its mirror-symmetric tridiagonal Jacobi matrix contracts to the continuum Pöschl-Teller Hamiltonian as the number of sites grows. The authors prove this both through the recurrence relation and at the level of polynomials, where the limit corresponds to the confluence Racah → Jacobi, with the grid parameters yielding a centrifugal term at the midpoint and confining walls at the ends. The resulting spectrum splits into two quadratic sublattices with Jacobi eigenfunctions differing only in the sign of one parameter, and the discrete model's bispectral Racah algebra 𝔯₁ contracts to the Jacobi algebra 𝔧₁. This establishes a precise discrete-to-continuum bridge one rung down the Askey-Wilson hierarchy and carries fractional revival phenomena into the Pöschl-Teller limit.|
|**2026-09-28**|**Stationary reduction method based on nonisospectral deformation of orthogonal polynomials, and discrete Painlev\'{e}-type equations**|Xiao-Lu Yue, Xiang-Ke Chang, Xing-Biao Hu|[[arxiv:2407.09875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09875)]|The paper introduces a new "stationary reduction method based on nonisospectral deformation of orthogonal polynomials" for deriving discrete Painlevé-type (d-P-type) equations. The method is applied to several classes of (bi)orthogonal polynomials, including ordinary orthogonality, (1,m)-biorthogonality, generalized Laurent biorthogonality, Cauchy biorthogonality, and partial-skew orthogonality. This yields several seemingly novel classes of higher-order d-P-type equations and integrable difference systems, along with particular solutions and Lax pairs; notably, the Cauchy biorthogonality case is a stationary reduction of a nonisospectral CKP-type Toda hierarchy, while the partial-skew case connects to a nonisospectral BKP-type Toda hierarchy with Pfaffian solutions. The work thus provides a systematic route to new integrable discrete equations and reveals connections between orthogonal polynomial theory and nonisospectral integrable hierarchies.|

|**2026-09-28**|**Colored Weingarten Calculus for Block-Unitary Ensemble**|Daniele Iannotti, Elisa Vallini|[[arxiv:2609.30385](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30385)]|The paper addresses the need for a random-matrix framework when Haar randomness is restricted to subspaces of Hilbert space, as occurs in symmetry-resolved sectors and ETH energy windows. It introduces a Block-Unitary Ensemble of independent Haar-random unitaries on each subspace and develops a constructive Weingarten calculus for computing moments and averages over it. The construction reveals a symmetry-resolved Schur-Weyl duality in which the relevant commutant is the algebra of color-preserving permutations rather than the ordinary permutation algebra. This provides a unified tool for quantum information and many-body physics, with applications to symmetry-constrained quantum states and energy-resolved ETH ensembles.|
|**2026-09-28**|**Observation of universal hierarchical relaxation in a quantum simulator**|Jiaozi Wang, Manoj K. Joshi, Luca Capizzi, Rainer Blatt, Christian F. Roos, Leonardo Mazza, Dario Poletti|[[arxiv:2609.29817](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29817)]|The paper addresses the challenge of characterizing how autocorrelation functions relax in interacting many-body quantum systems, building on the theoretical prediction of a "relaxation-overlap inequality" that implies a hierarchy of relaxation exponents. The authors extend this theory to systems with multiple conservation laws and realize it experimentally using a trapped-ion quantum simulator in the diffusive regime. Their results confirm the predicted universal hierarchical relaxation, providing a new experimental handle on the onset of hydrodynamics and thermalization in quantum matter.|
|**2026-09-28**|**Captured weight and boundary leakage bound the error of sample-based spectral functions**|Nicol\'as Bonilla Vargas|[[arxiv:2608.16436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16436)]|Sample-based quantum diagonalization reconstructs spectral functions from measured bitstrings, but it was unclear how subspace restriction affects accuracy. The authors prove error bounds for Rayleigh–Ritz projectors in terms of captured weight \(w\) and boundary leakage \(\widehat\Lambda\), testing them on the 1D Hubbard model via a real-time-evolved probe without a Hadamard test. They show weight alone cannot certify accuracy—error persists at \(0.35\)–\(0.43\) even when \(w=1\)—and that measured bounds are vacuous at all practical resource fractions, becoming informative only well above operating regimes. The results reveal that full-weight accuracy requires a subspace containing all symmetry-allowed determinants (or a momentum block), and that fermionic magic does not predict this support.|
|**2026-09-28**|**Altermagnetic phases and phase transitions in Lieb- $5$ Hubbard model**|Sougata Biswas,  Achintyaa, Paramita Dutta|[[arxiv:2601.14200](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.14200)]|The paper investigates altermagnetic (AM) order in a repulsive Hubbard model on the Lieb-5 lattice, a system where momentum-dependent spin-split bands coexist with zero net magnetization. Using mean-field theory, the authors show that with only nearest-neighbor hopping, a transition occurs from a nonmagnetic state to a unique altermagnetic isolated-band metal (AMIM) phase, while adding diagonal hopping stabilizes an intermediate altermagnetic metallic (AMM) phase. They characterize these phases via spin-resolved band structures, spectral functions, and the AM order parameter, and confirm their robustness against spin-orbit coupling and temperature. This work extends altermagnetism to a new lattice geometry and demonstrates that multiple distinct AM phases can emerge without second-neighbor interactions, broadening the scope of altermagnetic materials design.|
|**2026-09-28**|**Steady-state density large deviations in driven diffusive systems**|Soumyabrata Saha, Tridib Sadhu|[[arxiv:2501.03164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.03164)]|The paper addresses the lack of exact results for the steady-state density large-deviation functional in driven diffusive systems, which is understood only for a few special models. It introduces a field transformation within Macroscopic Fluctuation Theory that, in one dimension, yields two broad exactly solvable classes with local and non-local large-deviation functionals, while also determining optimal fluctuation paths and revealing the dynamical origins of non-locality and time irreversibility. For generic transport coefficients, the transformation provides a perturbative scheme in the boundary drive, showing that a finite bulk drive shifts the onset of non-locality from quadratic to linear order and generates long-range multipoint correlations of arbitrarily high order. In higher dimensions, this onset depends on the orientation of the bulk drive relative to the boundary-induced density gradient, extending the applicability of exact large-deviation results.|

|**2026-09-28**|**On the Cauchy problem for the Tzitz\'{e}ica equation: Soliton resolution conjecture and asymptotic analysis**|Shou-Fu Tian, Jia-Fu Tong|[[arxiv:2609.30858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30858)]|The paper studies the Cauchy problem for the Tzitzéica equation, an integrable model from affine differential geometry, extending recent work that only handled the purely continuous spectrum case to establish the soliton resolution conjecture and long-time asymptotics. Using a $\bar{\partial}$-nonlinear steepest descent method, the authors derive asymptotics across three regions of $\xi = x/t$: algebraic decay with error $O(t^{-1})$ for $/\xi/ \geq 1$, and $N$-soliton plus soliton-dispersion interaction approximations with error $O(t^{-3/4})$ in the middle and transition regions. |**2026-09-28**|**Stationary reduction method based on nonisospectral deformation of orthogonal polynomials, and discrete Painlev\'{e}-type equations**|Xiao-Lu Yue, Xiang-Ke Chang, Xing-Biao Hu|[[arxiv:2407.09875](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.09875)]|The paper introduces a new "stationary reduction method based on nonisospectral deformation of orthogonal polynomials" to derive discrete Painlevé-type (d-P-type) equations, addressing the need for systematic construction of such integrable difference systems. The method is applied to five classes of (bi)orthogonal polynomials—ordinary, (1,m)-biorthogonal, generalized Laurent biorthogonal, Cauchy biorthogonal, and partial-skew orthogonal—yielding novel high-order d-P-type equations and integrable difference systems with their particular solutions and Lax pairs. Notably, the system from Cauchy biorthogonality arises as a stationary reduction of a nonisospectral generalization involving the first two flows of the CKP-type Toda hierarchy, while the partial-skew case connects to the BKP-type nonisospectral Toda hierarchy and admits a Pfaffian solution. This work significantly extends the applicability of stationary reduction techniques and reveals new integrable structures linking orthogonal polynomials to discrete Painlevé hierarchies.|
