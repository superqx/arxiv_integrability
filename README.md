## High Energy Theory

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-09-21**|**Sine-Gordon Model with Bosonic Tensor Networks: Continuum Matching and Soliton Scattering**|Florian Hechenberger, Tommaso Rainaldi, Felix Ringer|[[arxiv:2609.21846](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21846)]|The paper addresses the difficulty of quantitatively connecting lattice Hamiltonian formulations of the sine-Gordon model to their continuum theory. By matching the lattice vertex operator to its conformal normalization at the free-boson ultraviolet fixed point, the authors derive an exact relation between the bare lattice coupling and the renormalized continuum mass without free parameters. Using uniform matrix product states and a quasiparticle ansatz, they reproduce Zamolodchikov's exact soliton mass, the relativistic soliton dispersion, and the two lightest breather masses to percent-level accuracy, and they extract the Wigner displacement in real-time soliton-antisoliton collisions, recovering its characteristic rapidity dependence. These results establish bosonic tensor networks as a quantitatively reliable tool for continuum matching, with implications for renormalization in other lattice field theories and for benchmarking continuous-variable quantum simulations.|

## Mathematical Physics

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-09-21**|**Entanglement-Inducing Quantum Markov Processes**|J. Fransson, A. P. Sowa|[[arxiv:2609.21438](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.21438)]|The paper addresses the challenge of modeling interacting bosons in lattice arrays without relying on conventional mean-field approximations, which preserve product states and cannot generate entanglement. It introduces the Schrödinger-Dirichlet equation, a nonlinear nonlocal evolution equation closely tied to the Bose-Hubbard model and generalized bosons. Unlike standard mean-field closures, this dynamics does not preserve product structure and can produce entanglement from initially separable states. The analysis relies on harmonic analysis over the multiplicative group of positive rationals, offering a new mathematical framework for entanglement-inducing quantum Markov processes.|
|**2026-09-21**|**A Pontryagin class obstruction for purely electric and purely magnetic Weyl curvature tensors**|Thijs de Kok|[[arxiv:2604.03707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03707)]|The paper asks whether every manifold admitting a Lorentzian metric also admits one whose Weyl tensor is purely electric (PE) or purely magnetic (PM). Working on \(4k\)-dimensional scalar product spaces, the authors show that products of Pontryagin forms landing in top degree vanish for algebraic curvature tensors that are even or odd under an orientation-reversing isometry, and lift this to a vanishing result for products of Pontryagin classes on pseudo-Riemannian manifolds with PE or PM Riemann or Weyl curvature. For compact manifolds this yields nontrivial cohomological obstructions to the existence of such metrics, with consequences for Lorentzian metrics of certain Petrov subtypes and for foliations by nondegenerate umbilic hypersurfaces.|
|**2026-09-21**|**Bihamiltonian tests for integrable systems associated to rank- $1$ F-CohFTs**|Alexandr Buryak, Jianghao Xu, Di Yang|[[arxiv:2601.13203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13203)]|The paper investigates which double ramification (DR) hierarchies associated with rank-1 F-cohomological field theories are bihamiltonian, a key property for integrability. Using bihamiltonian tests, the authors conjecture that the bihamiltonian cases form a 2-parameter family and identify a 1-parameter subfamily whose DR hierarchy is conjecturally Miura equivalent to the Camassa–Holm hierarchy. They also prove a conjecture on bihamiltonian Hodge hierarchies and, through a systematic study of Miura invariants, propose a link between another 1-parameter subfamily and the Degasperis–Procesi hierarchy. These results connect abstract integrable hierarchies to well-known nonlinear wave equations and advance the classification of bihamiltonian structures in this setting.|

## Condensed Matter

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-09-21**|**Correlation enhanced magnetism at a superconducting cuprate/manganite interface**|Vitor A. M. Lima, Marcello Civelli, Walber Hugo de Brito|[[arxiv:2609.22051](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22051)]|Motivated by experiments on cuprate/manganite heterostructures, this work asks how superconductivity and interfacial magnetism interact in a strongly correlated system. The authors study a bilayer Hubbard model with a local interfacial exchange field using cluster dynamical mean-field theory in the underdoped cuprate regime. They find that superconductivity coexists with manganite-induced Cu spin polarization and, surprisingly, carries a larger magnetic moment than the corresponding normal state, with correlations driving this enhancement; stronger exchange fields cause an abrupt, hysteretic collapse of superconductivity and a spin-polarized pseudogap. The results demonstrate that strong electronic correlations can allow superconductivity to enhance, rather than suppress, interfacial magnetism.|
|**2026-09-21**|**Nonlocal Magic Spreading in Many-body Quantum Dynamics: From Chaotic Evolution to Quasi-particle Picture in Integrable Models**|Sreemayee Aditya, Piotr Sierant, Xhek Turkeshi|[[arxiv:2609.20951](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.20951)]|The paper addresses how nonlocal magic—the irreducible quantum resource that survives any local basis change—spreads in many-body quantum dynamics, a question left open by its distinct behavior relative to entanglement. The authors connect nonlocal magic to the capacity of entanglement, enabling analytical predictions that they verify with large-scale numerics across chaotic and integrable systems. In chaotic systems, nonlocal magic grows logarithmically before decaying to a size-independent value, whereas in integrable systems a quasiparticle picture predicts saturation at a value logarithmic in subsystem size. These contrasting behaviors imply that the strongest scramblers support only transient entanglement embezzlement, while free-fermionic dynamics can sustain universal embezzlement in the steady state.|
|**2026-09-21**|**Intractable enumeration problems are like Russian nesting dolls: structural properties of monomer-dimer coverings on two-dimensional quadratic lattices**|Yong Kong|[[arxiv:2609.19231](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19231)]|The paper addresses the #P-complete problem of counting monomer-dimer coverings with exactly \(s\) dimers on two-dimensional quadratic lattices, a task generally considered computationally intractable. It derives an exact closed-form expression for this count, structured as \(s-1\) nested sums, and shows this yields an exponential-time algorithm with complexity \(O(2^s)\). The resulting solution is governed by a sequence exhibiting double-exponential growth, revealing unexpected structural regularity in an otherwise intractable enumeration problem.|
|**2026-09-21**|**The critical slowing down in training diffusion models**|Luca Maria Del Bono, Giulio Biroli, Patrick Charbonneau, Marylou Gabri\'e|[[arxiv:2605.12597](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12597)]|Training diffusion models near criticality is poorly understood and often suffers from slow convergence, limiting their use for sampling complex physical systems. The authors analyze this by training a score-based diffusion model on the Gaussian-limit \(O(n)\) model with an exactly matched one-layer network, revealing a critical slowing down in parameter learning that also degrades generation. They show that adding architectural depth—a two-layer network—drastically reduces this slowdown, changing training-time scaling from quadratic to logarithmic in system size, and demonstrate a practical Fourier implementation without excessive complexity. This establishes a controlled framework for understanding and improving learned samplers in statistical physics and beyond.|

## Nonlinear Sciences

|**2026-09-21**|**A 2+1-dimensional extended Dym equation: moving boundary problems solvable via Painlev\'e II symmetry reduction**|Colin Rogers, Pablo Amster|[[arxiv:2606.25739](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25739)]|The paper addresses moving boundary problems of Stefan type for a 2+1-dimensional extension of the solitonic Dym equation, a setting where exact solutions are generally hard to obtain. The author shows that this extended Dym equation admits a symmetry reduction to the Painlevé II equation, which is then used to construct exact solutions to the moving boundary problem. The main result is a class of exactly solvable Stefan-type problems, demonstrating that the higher-dimensional Dym equation can support analytically tractable free-boundary dynamics.|
|**2026-09-21**|**Bihamiltonian tests for integrable systems associated to rank- $1$ F-CohFTs**|Alexandr Buryak, Jianghao Xu, Di Yang|[[arxiv:2601.13203](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.13203)]|The paper investigates which double ramification (DR) hierarchies associated with rank-1 F-CohFTs admit a bihamiltonian structure, a key property for integrability. Using bihamiltonian tests on these hierarchies, the authors conjecture that the bihamiltonian cases form a 2-parameter family. Notably, they identify a 1-parameter subfamily whose DR hierarchy is conjecturally Miura equivalent to the Camassa–Holm hierarchy, and propose a relation between another 1-parameter subfamily and the Degasperis–Procesi hierarchy. They also prove a conjecture on bihamiltonian Hodge hierarchies and systematically study Miura invariants, advancing the classification of integrable systems tied to F-CohFTs.|
|**2026-09-21**|**On vector Schwarz-KdV equation**|M. Balakhnev, V. Sokolov|[[arxiv:2509.03155](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03155)]|The paper addresses the lack of systematic understanding of integrable systems related to the Schwarz–KdV equation, particularly in the vector setting. The authors construct a collection of continuous, semi-discrete, and discrete integrable systems associated with the Schwarz–KdV equation and extend these constructions to the vector case. Their main finding is that vector generalizations of these systems exist, an observation they describe as a non-trivial experimental fact with no known mathematical explanation. This work thus expands the known integrable landscape around Schwarz–KdV and highlights an unexplained phenomenon that calls for future theoretical investigation.|
