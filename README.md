|**2026-10-05**|**Generalized eigenstate thermalization beyond leading order in 2D large c CFTs**|Ziyuan Wang, Jiaju Zhang|[[arxiv:2610.03514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03514)]|The paper addresses the generalized eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (GETH) in 2D large-central-charge conformal field theories, where the conjecture that qKdV eigenstates mimic generalized Gibbs ensemble thermal states remains untested beyond leading order. The authors develop a Fock-space formulation that maps expectation values of vacuum-family quasi-primaries in qKdV eigenstates onto a free-bosonic problem, using it to show that the first two qKdV charges generically lift all degeneracies and to build a toolkit for testing GETH via the infinite tower of qKdV charges. They directly falsify a stronger form of GETH at subleading order in the large-\(c\) expansion, finding that at levels 6 and 8 in the vacuum family, quasi-primary expectation values are not fixed by finitely many qKdV charges and require microscopic eigenstate information. This result establishes that GETH fails beyond leading order for these observables and provides a systematic framework for further tests or falsifications in large-\(c\) CFTs.|
|**2026-10-05**|**Universality of Quantum Gates in Particle and Symmetry Constrained Subspaces**|Andreas Stergiou, Nicolas PD Sawaya|[[arxiv:2605.00979](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.00979)]|Near-term quantum simulations often need to prepare states restricted to symmetry-constrained subspaces, such as fixed particle number or spin, but universality of hardware-efficient gates in these subspaces is not generally guaranteed. The authors use Lie algebraic methods to show that overlapping-gate commutators generate Pauli Z "dressing" operators that act as spectator projectors, decomposing multi-plane rotations into generators spanning the full so(w) algebra, with added complex phases extending this to su(w). They also give an efficient Jacobian criterion for verifying that a circuit can explore all directions on the target manifold from almost any parameter setting. The framework is applied to prove completeness of a binary-encoded multi-level particle ansatz and to build symmetry-preserving circuits for the fuzzy-sphere regularization of the 3D Ising CFT, where ground and excited states are variationally prepared and CFT data extracted.|

Moving boundary problems solvable via Painleve' II Reduction**|Colin Rogers, Sandra Carillo|[[arxiv:2610.02927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02927)]|The paper extends the canonical 2+1-dimensional Dym equation by introducing temporal modulation, addressing the need for solvable nonlinear moving boundary problems of generalized Stefan type. |**2026-10-05**|**Majorana-Pauli stabilizer codes and duality webs of fermionic topological phases**|Meng Sun, Zongyuan Wang, Nathanan Tantivasadakarn, Yu-An Chen|[[arxiv:2606.25048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25048)]|The paper addresses the lack of systematic stabilizer-code descriptions for intrinsically fermionic topological phases by introducing Majorana-Pauli stabilizer codes, whose stabilizers combine generalized Pauli and Majorana operators. As a key example, it constructs an exactly solvable fermionic toric code using Z8 Pauli operators coupled to Majorana modes, from which anyons, string operators, fusion rules, and braiding statistics follow directly. It further shows this model sits within a duality web linking bosonic topological orders, SET phases, and bosonic/fermionic SPT phases, and extends the construction to Abelian fermionic topological orders and SPT phases, including a nontrivial Z8^F SPT with no free-fermion analog. These results broaden the stabilizer-code framework to intrinsically fermionic phases, connecting fermionic many-body physics to quantum error correction.|
|**2026-10-05**|**Deep-water and shallow-water limits of statistical equilibria for the intermediate long wave equation**|Andreia Chapouto, Guopeng Li, Tadahiro Oh|[[arxiv:2409.06905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06905)]|The paper studies invariant measures tied to higher-order conservation laws of the intermediate long wave equation (ILW) and how they behave in the deep-water and shallow-water limits. Using the complete integrability of ILW, the authors construct conservation laws at the \(H^{k/2}\) level and show they converge to those of the Benjamin-Ono (BO) equation in the deep-water limit and to those of the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation in the shallow-water limit, including a novel 2-to-1 collapse of ILW conservation laws to KdV ones. They then build generalized Gibbs measures for ILW and prove convergence to the corresponding invariant Gibbs measures for BO and KdV, along with invariance under ILW dynamics for \(k \ge 3\). These results establish the first complete family of shallow-water conservation laws with nontrivial limits and extend the known invariance theory for KdV's \(H^2\) Gibbs measure.|

|**2026-10-05**|**Finite-momentum pairing and magnetic halos in a spin-imbalanced Holstein model**|Chunhan Feng, George Batrouni, Richard Scalettar|[[arxiv:2610.03678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03678)]|The paper addresses the difficulty of numerically studying Fulde-Ferrell-Larkin-Ovchinnikov (FFLO) superconductivity in two dimensions, where the fermion sign problem in the spin-imbalanced attractive Hubbard model severely limits quantum Monte Carlo simulations at low temperatures. The authors show that the Holstein model, with its retarded electron-phonon attraction, strongly suppresses sign fluctuations under spin imbalance, enabling unbiased determinant quantum Monte Carlo simulations in this regime. They find that increasing magnetization drives a transition from zero-momentum to finite-momentum FFLO pairing, with the finite-Q susceptibility exceeding the zero-momentum value by up to 70%, and at high density FFLO pairing coexists with short-range checkerboard charge-density-wave order and a magnetic halo of staggered spin correlations at larger distances. These results establish a numerically accessible platform for studying intertwined FFLO, charge, and spin orders in two dimensions and suggest experimentally detectable signatures for cold-atom realizations.|
|**2026-10-05**|**Reaching the Limits of Ground-State Metrology with Many-Body Probes**|Erik L. Andr\'e, V\'ictor Izquierdo, Ricard Puig, John Calsamiglia, Mart\'i Perarnau-Llobet|[[arxiv:2610.03653](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03653)]|The paper examines what physical conditions are needed to reach the fundamental limits of ground-state quantum metrology—estimating an unknown parameter of an N-body Hamiltonian from its ground state—using many-body probes with short- or long-range interactions. The authors analyze the requirements to saturate the static ground-state bound (F ≤ N²/Δ²) and the dynamical Heisenberg limit (F ≤ N²τ²), showing that short-range systems need a gap closing with N (yielding the universal condition ν(d+z)=1 at second-order critical points), while all-to-all interacting models can achieve optimality even when gapped. They demonstrate that the dynamical bound is tied to ground-state preparation time and can be approached via local adiabatic protocols with an inverse-gap profile. Using the transverse-field Ising chain, XXZ chain, a squeezing Hamiltonian, and the two-mode Bose-Hubbard model, they identify concrete probes approaching both limits, providing a theoretical foundation for designing many-body ground-state sensors at ultimate precision.|
|**2026-10-05**|**Bogoliubov self-consistent $GW$ and second-order Green's function methods for attractive fermionic interactions**|Mauricio Rodr{\'i}guez-Mayorga, Johannes T\"olle, Pierre-Fran\c{c}ois Loos|[[arxiv:2610.03357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.03357)]|The paper addresses the challenge of describing fermionic systems with attractive interactions beyond mean-field theory, using the attractive Hubbard model as a benchmark. It implements Bogoliubov self-consistent GW (Bog. scGW) and second-order Green's function (Bog. scGF2) methods within a unified imaginary-time and imaginary-frequency framework, comparing them against full configuration interaction. Bog. scGF2 proves more accurate than Bog. scGW, which systematically overestimates the correlation energy with rapidly growing errors as interaction strength increases. The findings underscore the importance of exchange contributions in strongly correlated fermionic systems, even when treated only at low perturbative order.|
|**2026-10-05**|**triangulax: Differentiable Simulations with Triangular Meshes across Soft Matter Physics**|Samuel Silliman, Qiwei Yu, Nikolas Claussen|[[arxiv:2610.02566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.02566)]|Many physical and biological systems are naturally described as two-dimensional surfaces that undergo large deformations and host complex dynamics, but simulating them requires specialized discretization and force computation. The authors introduce triangulax, an open-source Python library that combines coordinate-free discrete differential geometry on triangular meshes with JAX automatic differentiation, enabling gradients through entire simulations, including those with stochastic forces or topological changes. They implement a mesh-quality-preserving algorithm and validate it on membrane mechanics coupled to an on-membrane concentration field, matching exact solutions and existing software, then demonstrate applications to vertex models and inverse design of a shape-morphing elastomer sheet. The library's differentiability simplifies multi-physics simulation and enables parameter fitting to experimental data or design objectives, broadening access to gradient-based approaches in soft matter physics.|
|**2026-10-05**|**Majorana-Pauli stabilizer codes and duality webs of fermionic topological phases**|Meng Sun, Zongyuan Wang, Nathanan Tantivasadakarn, Yu-An Chen|[[arxiv:2606.25048](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.25048)]|The paper addresses the lack of systematic stabilizer-code descriptions for intrinsically fermionic topological phases, which are well developed for bosonic orders but not for fermionic ones. The framework yields anyons, string operators, fusion rules, and braiding statistics directly from the stabilizer algebra, reveals a duality web linking bosonic topological orders, SET phases, and bosonic/fermionic SPT phases, extends to all Abelian fermionic topological orders with gapped boundaries, and realizes a nontrivial Z8^F fermionic SPT phase with no free-fermion analog. These results extend the stabilizer-code paradigm to a broad class of intrinsically fermionic phases, bridging fermionic many-body physics and quantum error correction.|
