|**2026-08-06**|**Machine-Learning Search for Lax Connections**|Osamu Fukushima, Tomohiro Shigemura, Ryosuke Suda, Norihiro Tanahashi, Kentaroh Yoshida|[[arxiv:2608.05146](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05146)]|The paper introduces a machine-learning framework to search for Lax connections in two-dimensional non-linear sigma models, using local current data as input. The method successfully recovers full spectral-parameter families for the SU(2) principal chiral model and the S^2 symmetric coset without prior knowledge of spectral curves. For the non-symmetric coset T^{1,1}, the optimization finds a low-loss, compact ansatz, but analytic checks reveal it is a "fake Lax" connection—flat on-shell but not encoding the equations of motion, though its point-particle reduction yields a genuine Lax pair. The work shows machine learning can propose viable candidates and reveal spectral structures, yet low flatness loss alone is insufficient to certify integrability, highlighting the need for analytic validation.|
|**2026-08-06**|**Single-eigenstate test of eigenstate thermalization hypothesis via perturbed eigenstate quench**|Zhouhao Guo, Jiaju Zhang|[[arxiv:2608.04696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04696)]|This paper introduces a new method to test the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH) using a single eigenstate. The approach involves applying a weak random perturbation to the eigenstate and then measuring the time-averaged evolution speed of its subsystems as a function of their size. The authors find a clear qualitative difference: ETH-satisfying eigenstates produce an S-shaped curve, while ETH-violating ones produce a J-shaped curve. Tested across various spin chain models, this diagnostic accurately identifies thermalization behavior without needing to construct a thermal ensemble, offering a simpler and experimentally viable probe.|
|**2026-08-06**|**Breakdown of Monotonic Impurity Entropy Flow in $\mathscr{PT}$ -Symmetric Multichannel Kondo Systems**|Pradip Kattel, Abay Zhakenov, Natan Andrei|[[arxiv:2608.04083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04083)]|This paper investigates a non-Hermitian, \(\mathscr{PT}\)-symmetric multichannel Kondo model with two impurities. Using an exact Bethe Ansatz solution, the authors map out four distinct impurity phases as a function of the non-Hermiticity parameter, and they compute the impurity free energy and Affleck-Ludwig \(g\)-function for the \(\mathscr{PT}\)-unbroken phases. The key result is that while the standard Kondo phase exhibits monotonic impurity entropy flow consistent with a generalized \(g\)-theorem, the zero-mode and local-moment phases display non-monotonic entropy, demonstrating that a real spectrum does not guarantee RG irreversibility. This finding challenges fundamental assumptions about the connection between unitary physics and renormalization group flow in non-Hermitian systems.|

The authors use the (G'/G'+G+A) method to derive exact solutions after transforming the equation into a reduced ODE, obtaining bright and anti-kink soliton solutions. They further convert the ODE into a 2D dynamical system to analyze its qualitative properties, including bifurcations, phase portraits, chaotic attractors, and sensitivity through various graphical tools. The work is significant for providing both exact soliton solutions and a comprehensive dynamical analysis, deepening understanding of complex nonlinear wave phenomena in higher-dimensional systems.|

Using an exact Bethe Ansatz solution, the authors map out four distinct impurity phases controlled by a non-Hermiticity parameter and compute the impurity entropy in the PT-unbroken regimes. The key finding is that while the Kondo phase exhibits the expected monotonic decrease in impurity entropy, the zero-mode and local-moment phases display non-monotonic entropy flow, demonstrating that a real spectrum and defect CFT consistency do not guarantee renormalization group irreversibility. This result challenges the universality of the g-theorem in non-Hermitian systems and provides an exact counterexample to monotonic impurity entropy flow.|
|**2026-08-06**|**The bosonic Hubbard model on a three dimensional flat band lattice**|Leon Haag-Fank, Andreas Mielke|[[arxiv:2604.19703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19703)]|This paper investigates the bosonic Hubbard model on a three-dimensional flat band lattice, specifically the line graph of a cubic lattice with periodic boundary conditions. The authors construct exact multi-particle ground states by placing particles in localized single-particle states such that they avoid each other, which is possible up to a critical particle number. They prove that at this critical density, the ground state entropy is subextensive, scaling as N^(2/3), while at lower densities the entropy is extensive. The work establishes a connection between the ground state properties and the number of 4-cycle decompositions of the lattice, providing new rigorous results for three-dimensional flat band systems.|

|**2026-08-06**|**Single-eigenstate test of eigenstate thermalization hypothesis via perturbed eigenstate quench**|Zhouhao Guo, Jiaju Zhang|[[arxiv:2608.04696](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04696)]|The paper introduces a new method to test the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH) using a single energy eigenstate. This is done by applying a weak random perturbation to the eigenstate and measuring how fast a subsystem evolves over time as its size grows relative to the total system. The method distinguishes thermalizing eigenstates, which show an S-shaped response curve, from non-thermalizing ones, which show a convex J-shaped curve. Successfully tested on various 1D spin models, this approach offers a practical, experimental tool for verifying ETH without needing to construct a thermal ensemble.|
|**2026-08-06**|**Breakdown of Monotonic Impurity Entropy Flow in $\mathscr{PT}$ -Symmetric Multichannel Kondo Systems**|Pradip Kattel, Abay Zhakenov, Natan Andrei|[[arxiv:2608.04083](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04083)]|This paper investigates a non-Hermitian, PT-symmetric multichannel Kondo model with two impurities, where complex-conjugate Kondo couplings introduce a non-Hermiticity parameter. Using exact Bethe Ansatz solutions, the authors map out four distinct impurity phases—overscreened Kondo, zero mode, Yu-Shiba-Rusinov (YSR), and local moment—with the YSR phase spontaneously breaking PT symmetry. They calculate impurity free energy and entropy flows, revealing that while the Kondo phase exhibits monotonic entropy decrease consistent with defect conformal field theory, the zero-mode and local-moment phases show non-monotonic entropy behavior, implying a breakdown of RG irreversibility. This finding challenges the assumption that a real spectrum and consistent defect entropies guarantee a generalized g-theorem, providing exact evidence that PT-symmetric systems can evade monotonic impurity entropy flow.|
|**2026-08-06**|**The bosonic Hubbard model on a three dimensional flat band lattice**|Leon Haag-Fank, Andreas Mielke|[[arxiv:2604.19703](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.19703)]|The paper addresses the bosonic Hubbard model on a three-dimensional flat band lattice, specifically the line graph of a cubic lattice. The problem is to understand the ground state properties at a critical particle density where particles can be placed in localized single-particle states without occupying the same site. The method involves constructing exact many-particle ground states using localized states from 4-cycle decompositions of the lattice and analyzing their entropy. Key results show that at the critical particle number, the ground state entropy is subextensive, scaling as N_c^{2/3}, while at lower densities it is extensive. The significance is that this provides the first rigorous results for flat band bosonic systems in three dimensions and links the problem to combinatorial structures of lattice decompositions.|
|**2026-08-06**|**Monte Carlo Diagonalization for Hubbard Model**|B. Bernard, M. Charlebois|[[arxiv:2509.22975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.22975)]|This paper introduces Monte Carlo diagonalization to overcome the exponential scaling limitations of exact diagonalization (ED) for the Hubbard model. The method constructs a truncated Fock space using a Monte Carlo approach, then applies Lanczos algorithms to this reduced basis to compute ground-state properties and Green's functions. This approach significantly reduces computational resources and time, while enabling simulations beyond the ~20-site limit of conventional ED. The key significance is extending numerically exact quantum impurity solvers to larger system sizes.|
