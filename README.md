## Mathematical Physics

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-09-14**|**Integral Representations for Solutions of the Linearized Fourth Painleve Equation**|Oleg M. Kiselev|[[arxiv:2609.12876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12876)]|The paper addresses the linearization of the fourth Painlevé equation by recasting it as a linearized Hamiltonian system and reducing the scalar variational equation to normal form. Using canonical fundamental solutions of the Jimbo–Miwa Lax pair, the authors construct quadratic expressions that satisfy a key differential identity and employ rapid-decay cycles to obtain integral solutions. They show that nontrivial cycles must have asymptotic branches in at least two distinct Stokes sectors, and that the Wronskian of two such solutions depends holomorphically on monodromy data, with numerical evidence of nonzero values. The significance lies in providing integral representations for variations of Stokes multipliers, the connection matrix, and local monodromy, thereby deepening the analytic understanding of the linearized fourth Painlevé equation.|
|**2026-09-14**|**Topological Recursion in the Seiberg-Witten partition function via AGT correspondence**|Bastam Tajik|[[arxiv:2609.12672](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12672)]|The paper reviews Seiberg-Witten theory and instanton calculus for $\mathcal{N}=2$ $SU(N)$ gauge theory, emphasizing the need for algebro-geometric tools like non-commutative resolutions and localization to compute instanton partition functions efficiently. It then uses the AGT correspondence—linking 2D Liouville conformal field theory to 4D $\mathcal{N}=2$ super Yang-Mills—to connect the classical Zamolodchikov recursion relation with Nekrasov's partition function. This connection yields an explicit instance of topological recursion that would otherwise demand highly advanced mathematical machinery to uncover.|
|**2026-09-14**|**Large $N$ expansions of the partition function for Coulomb systems on the surface of a cylinder**|Bo-Jian Shen, Peter J. Forrester|[[arxiv:2609.12487](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12487)]|The paper studies the large-$N$ expansion of the partition function for a two-dimensional one-component plasma at coupling $\beta=2$, confined to the surface of a cylinder with a general one-body potential along the axis. By a change of variables, the cylinder problem is mapped to an annular droplet plasma model plus a Jacobian that acts as the characteristic function of a linear statistic, extending the analysis to hard-wall boundary conditions where standard soft-edge formulas fail. Using Laplace's method and the Euler–Maclaurin formula, the authors handle one or two hard walls at or inside the droplet boundary, treating the two-hard-wall case by direct analysis. A key finding is that, unlike the annular partition function, the logarithm of the cylinder partition function never contains a $\log N$ term, regardless of hard walls.|
|**2026-09-14**|**Quasi-exactly solvable deformations of quantum systems associated with exceptional orthogonal polynomials**|Siyu Li, Ian Marquette, Sarah Post, Yao-Zhong Zhang|[[arxiv:2609.12428](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12428)]|The paper constructs new quasi-exactly solvable (QES) deformations of solvable quantum systems by exploiting Darboux-Crum transformations and the classification of exactly solvable systems tied to Hermite-type exceptional orthogonal polynomials (EOPs). These deformations destroy exact solvability but introduce model parameters that allow a finite number of polynomial solutions whose roots satisfy algebraic (Bethe ansatz) equations, with polynomial and rational cases analyzed alongside parameter constraints. The authors focus on deformations related to type III Hermite EOPs of arbitrary codimension and apply the approach to models with one, two, and up to four gaps, giving closed-form wavefunctions and spectra. The work extends QES theory beyond classical orthogonal polynomials and provides a systematic framework for identifying and counting QES solutions in parameter space.|

## Condensed Matter

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-09-14**|**Nonequilibrium Broken-Symmetry States from Retained Observables**|Sankha Subhra Bakshi, Gia-Wei Chern|[[arxiv:2609.12661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12661)]|The paper addresses how nonequilibrium broken-symmetry states can be identified without simulating slow, protocol-dependent dynamics. It reformulates this dynamical selection as a static constrained variational problem, where a small set of retained observables (particle number, energy, and slow quantities) defines a constraint space, and entropy maximization over quasiparticle occupations and candidate orders determines the selected order; restricting to the Gaussian manifold gives a cheap constrained Hartree-Fock scheme. The method reproduces the long-time TDHF suppression of square-lattice antiferromagnetism in photoexcited Hubbard models, predicts incommensurate spiral order on the triangular lattice instead of the equilibrium 120° state, and finds a region favoring Néel order in a driven double-exchange model. This turns the search for nonequilibrium orders into a practical static screening problem, avoiding the need to simulate every preparation protocol.|
|**2026-09-14**|**Altermagnetism and Superconductivity: A Short Historical Review**|Zhao Liu, Hui Hu, Xia-Ji Liu|[[arxiv:2510.09170](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.09170)]|This review traces the historical and conceptual development of altermagnetism, showing that its underlying physics—nonrelativistic spin-momentum locking—was originally introduced in the context of electronic liquid crystal phases and later formalized through multipole expansions extended across multiple atomic sites. It then surveys superconductivity in altermagnetic systems from three perspectives: nonrelativistic spin-momentum-locked Fermi surfaces (yielding finite-momentum pairing, d-wave and spin-triplet states, and topological Bogoliubov Fermi surfaces), static altermagnetic order or fluctuations, and the competition/intertwining of altermagnetic order with superconductivity in the repulsive Hubbard model. The work serves as both an accessible introduction to altermagnetism and a conceptual guide for exploiting its unconventional superconductivity in future quantum technologies.|

## Nonlinear Sciences

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-09-14**|**Integral Representations for Solutions of the Linearized Fourth Painleve Equation**|Oleg M. Kiselev|[[arxiv:2609.12876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12876)]|The paper addresses the linearization of the fourth Painlevé equation by reformulating it as a linearized Hamiltonian system and reducing the scalar variational equation to normal form. Using canonical fundamental solutions of the Jimbo–Miwa Lax pair, the authors construct quadratic expressions that yield integral representations of solutions via rapid-decay cycles with vanishing boundary terms. They show that nontrivial cycles must have asymptotic branches in at least two distinct Stokes sectors, that a second homology class provides another independent solution, and that the Wronskian depends holomorphically on monodromy data, with numerical evidence of nonzero Wronskian for one complex dataset. The work also derives integral formulas for variations of Stokes multipliers, the connection matrix, and local monodromy, providing a rigorous contour-based framework for studying linearized Painlevé IV solutions.|
|**2026-09-14**|**Dynamics-preserving network reductions for ride-pooling paths**|Karolin Stiller, Nora Molkenthin|[[arxiv:2609.12603](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.12603)]|Ride-pooling algorithms generate interacting path dynamics that are computationally complex because they depend on full street-network structure with many passive nodes. The authors show that for a broad class of distance-based dispatchers, passive nodes can be exactly integrated out by constructing an effective network on active (pooling) nodes whose edge weights preserve shortest-path distances, yielding an equivalence class of network representations with identical dynamics up to stochastic degeneracy-breaking. For globally optimizing dispatchers, this equivalence is systematically violated through degeneracy amplification, though the reduction remains quantitatively accurate beyond the exactly solvable regime. This provides a general framework for identifying when spatial structure can be coarse-grained away without losing dynamical fidelity in interacting path processes.|
