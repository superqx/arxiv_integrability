Using a one-dimensional quasi-exactly solvable sextic oscillator with known eigenvalues, the authors prove that finite-order bounds on a centered observable remain strict even after adding arbitrary finite polynomial Gram tests and all visible eigenstate-annihilator relations, implying no finite sum-of-squares certificate exists. They then establish general convergence for polynomial Schrödinger operators satisfying sum-of-squares confinement and derivative bounds, obtaining moment estimates uniform in truncation order and a regular Schrödinger representation on the prescribed eigenspace. A four-mode extension further demonstrates nonphysical feasible points with positive definite moment matrices at finite order, highlighting fundamental limitations of finite bootstrap approximations.|

|**2026-10-07**|**Hamiltonian curl-forces in systems coupled to multiple thermal reservoirs**|Omer Chor, Alja\v{z} Godec, Oren Raz|[[arxiv:2610.08423](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08423)]|The paper addresses how systems coupled to multiple thermal reservoirs at different temperatures sustain nonequilibrium currents. By applying a canonical transformation, the authors map such a system onto a particle coupled to a single reservoir but driven by a deterministic, velocity-independent curl-force derivable from a Hamiltonian with anisotropic kinetic energy. Despite this Hamiltonian structure, the system does not relax to a Boltzmann distribution and remains genuinely out of equilibrium, as demonstrated through two examples and an exactly solvable N-dimensional underdamped Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process for which steady-state distribution and entropy production rate are derived. |**2026-10-07**|**Quantum geometry of collective pairing fluctuations in the superfluid weight of multiband superconductors**|R. N. Kalkan, M. The authors show that this correction is quantum-geometric in origin, arising not from Bloch states but from the space of collective pairing fields: for an attractive multiband Hubbard model at mean-field level, the thermodynamic Hessian equals the static Gaussian pair-fluctuation kernel at zero momentum for all temperatures below \(T_c\). They show that this interference produces multi-channel fusion rules at the macroscopic level, with examples including Fibonacci fusion rules, tensor products of Fibonacci rules, and lineon period transmutation.
