|**2026-09-04**|**Fate of "Space-like singularities" in $c=1$ Matrix Model**|Sumit R. Das, Shaun D. Hampton, Sinong Liu, Gautam Mandal|[[arxiv:2606.31925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31925)]|This paper examines time-dependent backgrounds in two-dimensional string theory that appear to create space-like singularities. The authors model these systems as quantum quenches in the dual matrix model, going beyond the standard double-scaling limit and retaining nonlinear terms in the potential. Using a fermionic Thomas-Fermi approximation, they show that singularities only emerge as an artifact of the strict double-scaling limit; at finite N, folds on the Fermi surface proliferate and relax to a time-independent equilibrium state with a power-law rate. This reveals that the apparent singularities are not fundamental, resolving a key puzzle about these backgrounds.|
|**2026-09-04**|**Exceptional Points as Manifestations of Analyticity Breakdown in the 't Hooft Model**|Kejun Liu|[[arxiv:2606.10141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10141)]|The paper examines analyticity breakdown in the meson two-point function of the 't Hooft model, an exactly-solvable confining gauge theory. By introducing a PT-symmetric deformation that mimics an imaginary chemical potential, the authors identify an exceptional point where the lowest meson states coalesce. They derive the critical threshold in closed form through a Jacobi continued fraction, confirming a square-root branch point with exponent 1/2 via finite-size scaling. The findings reveal a time-domain signature of linear propagator growth and link the threshold to confinement, establishing the first analytically-controlled example of exceptional-point breakdown in a confining gauge theory.|
|**2026-09-04**|**Cosmology of fractional gravity**|Iv\'an Salvador-Garc\'ia, Gianluca Calcagni|[[arxiv:2604.28188](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28188)]|This paper presents the first cosmological analysis of classical fractional gravity, a nonlocal theory of quantum gravity. The authors derive the covariant equations of motion for an arbitrary fractional exponent and reduce them to Friedmann equations for a homogeneous, isotropic universe. They find that de Sitter space is an exact, stable solution, while bouncing solutions require phantom or ghost fluids, with the latter leading to a novel finite-future singularity. The results confirm that different form-factor representations yield identical solutions, and the study offers an initial comparison with multi-fractional cosmological models.|

## Mathematical Physics

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|**2026-09-04**|**Exact Scaling Laws and Non-Hermitian Topological Phase Transitions of Active Continuum on Hyperbolic Manifolds**|Yu-Xin Xie|[[arxiv:2609.03500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03500)]|This paper introduces an exact algebraic framework to study collective motion in active matter on hyperbolic surfaces, moving beyond perturbative or simulation-based approaches. By deriving critical thresholds and topological free energies from geometric identities, the authors precisely define phase transitions and connect the system's behavior to the underlying curvature. Their analysis reveals non-Hermitian topological phenomena, including a second-order exceptional point that dictates a unique algebraic scaling law for system dynamics. This work offers a closed-form theoretical solution that bridges geometric frustration and active mechanics, establishing a foundational model for such complex systems.|
|**2026-09-04**|**Exceptional Points as Manifestations of Analyticity Breakdown in the 't Hooft Model**|Kejun Liu|[[arxiv:2606.10141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.10141)]|The paper uses the exactly-solvable 't Hooft model, a 1+1D confining gauge theory, as a framework to rigorously study how a PT-symmetric deformation breaks the analyticity of a causal response function. By introducing a non-Hermitian term, the authors drive the two lowest meson states to coalesce at an exceptional point (EP), precisely locating this threshold using a continued fraction approach rather than numerical methods. They confirm the square-root singularity exponent of 1/2 and demonstrate that at the EP, the propagator norm grows linearly in time (Jordan secular law), unlike the bounded or exponential growth on either side. This work provides the first analytically controlled example of EP-induced analyticity breakdown in a confining gauge theory, with observable signatures that could be probed in non-Hermitian photonic analogues.|
|**2026-09-04**|**Time-Dependent Logarithmic Perturbation Theory for Quantum Dynamics: Formulation and Applications**|Juan Carlos del Valle, Paul Bergold, Karolina Kropielnicka|[[arxiv:2604.14812](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.14812)]|This paper introduces a time-dependent extension of logarithmic perturbation theory for quantum systems, expanding the wave function's logarithm in powers of a coupling constant. The method preserves a closed-integral structure for corrections, enabling direct computation of instantaneous energy shifts and dynamic shifts like AC Stark effects. The authors validate their approach on a driven harmonic oscillator, where it exactly matches the known solution, and on a hydrogen atom in a laser field, achieving high accuracy in numerical simulations. This framework provides a new analytical tool for studying time-dependent multi-photon processes in the perturbative regime.|

## Condensed Matter

|**2026-09-04**|**A Superconducting Peierls Instability**|Pramodh Senarath Yapa, Joseph Maciejko, Frank Marsiglio, Annica M. Black-Schaffer|[[arxiv:2609.04164](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04164)]|The paper investigates a novel electronic instability in superconducting systems, drawing an analogy to the traditional Peierls transition. The authors propose that at the edge of a two-dimensional superconductor, a dispersing Andreev bound state can couple with pairing fluctuations to soften a pairing mode at a specific wavevector, leading to a superconducting Kohn anomaly. This instability causes the order parameter to condense into an edge pair-density wave, which gaps the bound state spectrum. Using self-consistent Bogoliubov–de Gennes calculations on a Hubbard model, they demonstrate this mechanism, highlighting its potential relevance to unconventional superconductors without requiring topological protection.|
|**2026-09-04**|**Exact Scaling Laws and Non-Hermitian Topological Phase Transitions of Active Continuum on Hyperbolic Manifolds**|Yu-Xin Xie|[[arxiv:2609.03500](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03500)]|This paper introduces an exact algebraic framework to analyze collective motion in active matter on negatively curved hyperbolic surfaces, moving beyond approximate methods. The authors derive precise formulas for the critical transition threshold and identify a special parameter regime connected to topological BPS states, showing how curvature acts as a form of regularization. By mapping convective instabilities to defect dynamics, they uncover a non-Hermitian exceptional point that governs a novel algebraic time-scaling law. This work provides a rare exactly solvable model linking geometric frustration to non-equilibrium topology, offering a new theoretical tool for soft matter physics.|
|**2026-09-04**|**Fate of "Space-like singularities" in $c=1$ Matrix Model**|Sumit R. Das, Shaun D. Hampton, Sinong Liu, Gautam Mandal|[[arxiv:2606.31925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.31925)]|This paper studies time-dependent backgrounds in two-dimensional string theory that exhibit superluminal "Liouville walls," previously linked to space-like singularities. The authors model these phenomena as quantum quenches in the dual matrix model, preserving non-linear terms instead of taking the strict double-scaling limit. Using a Thomas-Fermi approximation, they find that early behavior matches the double-scaled theory, but at logarithmic timescales, the infrared wall induces fermi surface folds that ultimately densely cover phase space. The late-time, coarse-grained phase space density relaxes to a time-independent equilibrium via a power law, demonstrating that the space-like singularity is an artifact of the double-scaling limit rather than a fundamental feature.|
|**2026-09-04**|**Strain-Tuned Incommensurate Kekul\'e Spiral Order in Twisted Bilayer Graphene: a Quantum Many-Body Study**|Cheng Huang, Yves H. Kwan, Maksim Ulybyshev, Fakher F. Assaad, Laura Classen, Zi Yang Meng|[[arxiv:2605.20677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20677)]|This paper investigates the strain-dependent ground state of twisted bilayer graphene at filling factor ν=±2, a regime that is computationally challenging to study. The authors use a combination of approximate quantum Monte Carlo, exact diagonalization, and Hartree-Fock methods to examine the transition between two proposed ordered states. They find that while all methods identify the Kramers intervalley coherent state, only exact diagonalization and Hartree-Fock can detect the more subtle incommensurate Kekulé spiral order under strain. The work establishes a combined computational protocol where approximate quantum Monte Carlo can reliably capture strong orders, potentially aiding future studies of correlated phases in twisted bilayer graphene.|
|**2026-09-04**|**Emergent Gauge Flux and Spin Ordering in Magnetized Triangular Spin Liquids: Applications to Hofstadter-Hubbard Model**|Jiahao Yang, Hao Tian, Si-Yu Pan, Gang v. Chen|[[arxiv:2601.12898](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12898)]|This paper investigates magnetic and orbital field effects in a triangular-lattice spin liquid. Using a J1-J2-Jχ model, the authors show that these fields generate distinct internal U(1) gauge flux states: orbital flux induces a chiral spin liquid with staggered flux, while a Zeeman field drives a transition to a Landau-level state with uniform flux and conical spin order. They identify unique thermal Hall and magnetic signatures for each phase. The results offer testable predictions for moiré Hofstadter-Hubbard systems and triangular antiferromagnets, linking theory to ongoing experiments.|
