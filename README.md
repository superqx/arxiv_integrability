Pawlowski|[[arxiv:2611.27768](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27768)]|The paper tackles fermionic sign problems, where complex probability distributions make standard Monte Carlo sampling inefficient or intractable. It extends the physics-informed kernel (PIK) generative sampling framework to handle generic weighted sums of complex Gaussian distributions: a first kernel (PIKfan) maps a real normal distribution to such a weighted sum at Gaussian sampling cost, and a second PIK evolves it into the target theory's distribution, reducing the sampling task to a Gaussian one. |**2026-11-23**|**Quasi-local form for $\alpha$--$z$ R\'enyi QNEC from fixed-ray escorts**|Tanay Kibe, Pratik Roy|[[arxiv:2609.04016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04016)]|The paper addresses the difficulty of relating Rényi divergences to energy and entanglement entropy, which is less direct than for ordinary relative entropy. It introduces an integral representation of the α–z Rényi divergence as an average of ordinary relative entropies of auxiliary "escort" states built from the excited and reference states, yielding energy–entropy decompositions, averaged entanglement laws, and energy bounds in regulated QFT. From this, the authors derive a quasi-local expression for the diagonal second null shape variation of the α–z Rényi divergence of an excited state relative to the vacuum, which includes a transport term arising because forming an escort after regional restriction generally differs from restricting a pre-formed escort. The conjectural α–z Rényi QNEC then bounds the average escort state's null energy density by an escort-averaged second null variation of von Neumann entropy; the conjecture is verified for coherent excitations of a free scalar field in the finite-divergence part of the upper data-processing region, where the positive second variation is entirely due to the transport term, and escort formation is shown to commute with the reduced encoding channel in finite-dimensional operator-algebra quantum error-correcting codes with exact complementary recovery.|

|**2026-11-23**|**Comparing weakly interacting and free quantum dynamics locally**|Jorge Acu\~na Flores, Joscha Henheik, Marius Lemm, Daniele Toniolo|[[arxiv:2611.27707](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27707)]|The paper addresses the difficulty of establishing many-body transport behavior from free (non-interacting) dynamics, using Lieb-Robinson bounds as the technical bridge. The authors show that weakly interacting many-body dynamics can be locally approximated by one-body dynamics on large perturbative time scales, letting free transport results carry over to the interacting setting. They apply this to two cases: proving quantum diffusion via an operator-spreading diagnostic in weakly interacting, weakly disordered Fermi systems in dimensions D ≥ 2, and showing that local multi-time spin-up correlators in low-density Fermi-Hubbard models are approximated by their free counterparts, yielding an OTOC analogue of numerically observed short-time entanglement patterns. The work is significant because it provides a rigorous route for transferring non-interacting transport and correlation results to weakly interacting many-body systems on finite time scales.|
|**2026-11-23**|**A posteriori estimates for inverse evolutionary problems**|Sergey Repin|[[arxiv:2611.26678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26678)]|The paper addresses a posteriori error analysis for evolutionary problems that are ill-posed in the Hadamard sense, where standard error estimation concepts fail because solutions may not exist and approximations can be highly unstable. The authors reformulate the estimation framework using functional-type a posteriori estimates, producing computable quantities that measure the distance between an approximate inverse-problem solution and the exact solution of a nearby well-posed problem sharing the same differential equation. This approach is developed for several problems based on the reaction-convection-diffusion equation, including backward parabolic, non-local time condition, Cauchy parabolic, and parameter identification problems. The derived estimates both control approximation accuracy and offer a unified route to numerical computation.|
|**2026-11-23**|**Linear statistics in the Coulomb gas: cumulants beyond rotational invariance**|Paul Bourgade, Pierre Le Doussal, Gregory Schehr|[[arxiv:2610.33659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33659)]|The paper studies higher cumulants of smooth linear statistics in a large-N classical Coulomb gas confined by a general external potential, going beyond the previously understood variance and the rotationally symmetric case. Using a tilted potential U_s = U + s f/β, the resulting deformation of the droplet, and shape derivatives of the Dirichlet energy, the authors derive explicit leading-order formulas for the third and fourth cumulants for general smooth droplets without radial assumptions, expressing them as intrinsic boundary integrals. The third cumulant involves the cube of the jump in the normal derivative of the harmonic extension of f, while the fourth also includes the exterior Dirichlet-to-Neumann operator, boundary mean curvature, and normal derivatives of the background density. The results extend earlier work of Wiegmann and Zabrodin (providing a rigorous proof in their β=2, d=2 setting) and are checked against exactly solvable non-radial deformations, including translated elliptic droplets in 2D and spheroidal deformations in 3D.|
|**2026-11-23**|**Entanglement and non-local magic in a non-unitarily deformed non-Hermitian bipartite system**|Chen-Huan Wu|[[arxiv:2608.08346](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.08346)]|The paper addresses how non-Hermitian degeneracies and entanglement can be decoupled, since entanglement depends on eigenvectors rather than eigenvalues alone. The authors construct a bipartite model by applying an invertible non-unitary similarity transformation to a Hermitian operator with a degenerate eigenspace, yielding an exactly solvable two-qubit realization in which right eigenstates evolve from product states to maximally entangled states while the spectrum remains unchanged. They derive a closed-form partial-transpose negativity and a Schmidt-gauged non-local magic measure, finding that entanglement grows monotonically with the non-Hermiticity parameter while non-local magic peaks at intermediate coupling and vanishes in both the product and maximally entangled limits. These results provide diagnostic tools that distinguish non-defective degeneracy, exceptional-point sensitivity, typical entanglement, and non-stabilizer correlations in non-Hermitian bipartite systems.|
|**2026-11-23**|**Superintegrability in the Interaction of Two Particles with Spin: First-Order Pseudo-Scalar Integrals of Motion**|Fatih T\"urkkan, O. O\u{g}ulcan Tuncer, \.Ismet Yurdu\c{s}en|[[arxiv:2605.07659](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07659)]|The paper extends a program classifying quantum superintegrable systems for two non-relativistic spin-1/2 particles in three-dimensional Euclidean space, which previously covered first-order scalar integrals of motion. Starting from the most general rotationally invariant spin-dependent Hamiltonian, the authors construct the most general pseudo-scalar operator of differential order at most one as a matrix polynomial in momenta and impose commutation with the Hamiltonian, yielding a system of determining equations. Solving these equations gives a complete classification of Hamiltonians admitting additional pseudo-scalar integrals of order at most one, with the integrals determined explicitly. For selected cases the authors also construct polynomial symmetry algebras generated by the integrals, and the new spin-dependent families are relevant to nucleon-nucleon interaction models and the broader classification of superintegrable quantum systems with spin.|
