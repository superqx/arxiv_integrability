## High Energy Theory

|Publish Date|Title|Authors|arXiv|Summary|

|**2026-11-09**|**Existence of extremal solutions in the 1d conformal bootstrap**|Filip Her\v{c}ek, Nat Levine|[[arxiv:2611.09867](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09867)]|The paper addresses the lack of rigorous proof that extremal solutions and dual functionals exist in the conformal bootstrap, especially in the infinite-dimensional (zero-truncation) limit. The authors formulate OPE maximization in 1d CFT as a convex optimization problem in a Hilbert space and apply the Fenchel-Rockafellar theorem. They prove that extremal solutions exist for a large class of unitary, interacting, exact crossing solutions, and that these problems have zero duality gap, so functionals arbitrarily close to extremality exist. This provides a rigorous functional-analytic foundation for extremal solutions and duality in the bootstrap.|
|**2026-11-09**|**Constructing Casimirs for the quantum reflection equation**|Erik Brodsky, Leonid O Chekhov, Quanyu Tang|[[arxiv:2611.08796](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08796)]|The paper addresses the construction of quantum Casimirs for the quantum reflection equation involving a full-dimensional \(n\times n\) matrix \(\mathbb A\), a setting where closed-form invariants were previously lacking. Using \(q\)-determinant techniques, the authors derive closed expressions for the quantum Pfaffian (for even \(n\)) and for new quantum Casimirs expressed as ratios of special quantum (Sklyanin) minors of \(\mathbb A\), with particular attention to their self-adjointness. They further conjecture that, under a natural ansatz for a quantum deformation of a classical Casimir, self-adjointness implies commutativity, and they explore connections to networks of quantum cluster variables. These results provide explicit algebraic invariants for the quantum reflection equation and link them to cluster-algebraic structures, offering a new perspective on when self-adjoint quantum Casimirs commute.|
|**2026-11-09**|**On deforming and breaking integrability**|Ysla F. Adans, Marius de Leeuw, Tristan McLoughlin|[[arxiv:2603.17018](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17018)]|The paper investigates how nearest-neighbour deformations affect integrability in quantum spin chains, aiming to classify the possible outcomes of such perturbations. By expanding in the deformation parameter, the authors identify four deformation types: those that preserve or simply break integrability, those integrable only when all orders are included (as in holographic long-range deformations), and those that are perturbatively integrable to some order but cannot be extended to a fully integrable model. Working with the XXZ spin chain, they analyze level statistics for each case and find numerical evidence that the onset of chaos differs across the models. Notably, perturbatively integrable deformations show a volume-scaling of the chaos-onset strength intermediate between strong and weak integrability-breaking models, clarifying the interplay between deformation structure and chaotic behaviour.|

|**2026-11-09**|**Raman anyon-Hubbard model in a 1D Bose lattice gas**|Josep Cabedo, Claudio Iacovelli, Leticia Tarruell, Alessio Celi|[[arxiv:2611.09855](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09855)]|The paper addresses the challenge of realizing lattice anyon models in experimentally accessible quantum gases. The authors propose the Raman anyon-Hubbard model, in which Raman-coupled bosons loaded into a 1D optical lattice acquire density-dependent Peierls phases during tunneling, and they show that this model reduces to the anyon-Hubbard model for small statistical phases while defining a distinct lattice anyon model with unbounded statistical interactions beyond that limit. Using numerical simulations, they demonstrate signatures of 1D anyons, including a density-dependent asymmetry in the ground-state momentum distribution, interaction-induced asymmetric transport, and chiral bound states. These effects are predicted to be observable with Raman-coupled $^{39}$ K atoms, enabling experimental study of 1D anyons beyond the few-particle regime.|
|**2026-11-09**|**$\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge and exact solution of the $\pm J$ Edwards-Anderson model in a transverse field**|Mikhail Vasin|[[arxiv:2611.09097](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09097)]|The paper addresses the quantum ±J Edwards–Anderson spin-glass model in a transverse field at zero temperature, where frustration from random coupling signs complicates the theoretical description. Using a Majorana representation of spin-1/2 operators, the author shows that the bond operators formed from the z-components commute with the Hamiltonian and, unlike in the Kitaev model, are not constrained by a Gauss law, allowing the disorder in the exchange signs to be removed by an exact gauge transformation. This reduces the model to one with uniform couplings and a quadratic Majorana action, from which the local Green's functions are computed. The Edwards–Anderson order parameter vanishes for ∆ > √Z J and equals 1/4 for ∆ < √Z J, signaling a second-order transition from a paramagnet to a replica-symmetric spin-glass ground state, with the result found to be independent of lattice geometry.|
|**2026-11-09**|**Tackling the Sign Problem in the Doped Hubbard Model with Normalizing Flows**|Dominic Schuh, Lena Funcke, Janik Kreit, Thomas Luu, Simran Singh|[[arxiv:2603.18205](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.18205)]|The finite-chemical-potential Hubbard model is important for doped correlated systems but notoriously hard to simulate due to the sign problem, and while the spin basis reduces sign severity it introduces ergodicity problems. The authors extend normalizing-flow methods from half-filling to finite chemical potential by adding an annealing scheme that enables ergodic sampling in the spin basis. Their approach reproduces exact diagonalization results and cuts statistical uncertainties by an order of magnitude compared with state-of-the-art hybrid Monte Carlo in the charge basis. This opens a practical route to simulating doped correlated quantum systems.|

Using Turkington's variational principle, the authors show via an exact virial partition that nonlinear APE enhancement (1.27–2.06×) is what selects the physical wave, and they compare constrained Deep Ritz (energy-based) objectives against strong- and weak-form residual objectives from a shared KdV-derived initialization. |**2026-11-09**|**Mersenne Representation, the Conolly Sequence, and Soliton Profiles over Finite Fields**|Fumitaka Yura|[[arxiv:2607.22202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22202)]|The paper studies the canonical skew-binary (Mersenne) representation of nonnegative integers and its split into binary and nonbinary parts, aiming to clarify the combinatorics of this representation and connect it to the Conolly sequence. It analyzes the successor structure of the nonbinary values (OEIS A055938), proving a previously conjectural relation with OEIS A080578 and showing that a shifted Conolly sequence serves as the counting function for the binary side and the increasing enumeration of the nonbinary side; it also develops the parent map, truncation blocks/remainders, and a Mersenne tau function whose row differences recover parent iterates and the counting function. The Mersenne combinatorics is then applied to a known finite-depth one-soliton family of the finite-field box-ball system, reconstructing a global integer-valued traveling-wave profile, proving an integer window-counting theorem, and obtaining finite-field solutions via reduction modulo 3, along with an integer-valued traveling-wave tau function whose front values come from the Mersenne tau rows. The significance lies in providing direct proofs of OEIS conjectures and demonstrating how a number-representation's combinatorics can feed directly into reconstructing solutions of an integrable system.|

Turbulence at infinite Reynolds number is defined through regularization and renormalization, and the loop equation on N-sided polygons yields an exact family of decaying solutions (the Euler ensemble) whose partition function reduces to Gibbs averages of two integrable Mathieu quantum pendulums. This gives explicit energy spectra falling as k^{-1}log(1/kL) at small k and k^{-7/2} at large k, with a slope divergence at kL=√2 and vorticity oscillations of period √2πL. The significance lies in connecting turbulence to integrable quantum systems and formalizable mathematics (verification in Lean 4 is forthcoming), though comparison with simulations and experiment remains qualitative and the theory rests on stated physical hypotheses.|
